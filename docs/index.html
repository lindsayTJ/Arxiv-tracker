From extreme weather to agricultural consequences with agentic AI
Authors: Minghan Yu, Mengyu Dai, Yixin Wen, Haizhao Yang
First: 2026-10-29T17:58:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:58:53+00:00
Comments: 34 pages, 4 figures, 6 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Extreme weather is forecast days ahead, yet its consequences for agriculture remain hard to predict: a forecast says where a storm will strike, not which crops will suffer or how much is at risk, and the records that could tell span many sources. We present WxCrop, the first agent that judges agricultural hazards from physically interpretable weather indices examined by causal analysis, and the first that combines weather and agricultural information to predict crop loss reliably for past and forecast dates. We demonstrate it in Florida on hurricanes, freezes and dry heat, with a loss model calibrated on insurance records for each, and the same procedure extends to other hazards and areas. Three checks show reliability: the hurricane index detects recorded cyclone days with an AUC of 0.98, recorded losses in held-out years are reproduced to within 2%, and independent production records confirm the county rankings (rank correlation 0.63). Users ask the agent for each crop group's loss as a fraction of expected season value, every number sourced, so that governments, growers and scientists respond to agricultural disasters quickly.

Abstract
This case study compares generalized linear models, generalized additive models, and semi-structured deep distributional regression for analyzing settlement patterns in historical land-cover maps. Using four reference years for Donau-Ries county (Landkreis Donau-Ries) in southern Germany, we quantify how distances to agricultural areas, forests, and rivers relate to residential presence, and we evaluate predictive performance through spatial block cross-validation. Classical models yield stable and interpretable distance effects, whereas the semi-structured framework improves prediction once local image context is included. The analysis illustrates a practical trade-off between interpretability, accuracy, and robustness when interpretable covariates are combined with deep components in spatio-temporal data analysis.

The distribution of violent event and interevent times in conflicts
Authors: Jeroen Bruggeman
First: 2024-12-20T12:42:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:56:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Ongoing violent conflicts at all scales are interrupted by lulls without violence. Studies of interevent times found power-law distributions, based on coarse-grained data with a resolution of days or longer. Fine-grained data with a resolution of seconds or shorter is rare. Here, interevent times in violent conflicts are represented as multiplicative processes, and video-recorded street fights are used as fine grained data. For multiplicative processes, a mathematical theorem predicts that coarse grained data are power-law distributed, but fine grained data are lognormal. To the street violence data, however, the lognormal does not fit better than the power law (and vice versa), although the lognormal covers more data. Violent events, by contrast, take much energy and their durations are shorter, hence their distribution is better approximated by an exponential.

Adaptive Seismic Inversion in Piecewise Smooth Media Using Whiteness-based Alternating Directions
Authors: Lian Zhao, Kamal Aghazade, Ali Gholami, Danping Cao
First: 2026-10-29T16:54:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:54:04+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Post-stack acoustic impedance inversion is ill-posed because seismic data are band-limited and contaminated by noise. Regularization improves the stability of the inversion, but Tikhonov regularization tends to blur formation interfaces, whereas total variation (TV) regularization may introduce staircase artifacts. To simultaneously characterize abrupt interlayer discontinuities and gradual intralayer variations, we propose an inversion method based on anisotropic Tikhonov-TV regularization and automatic parameter selection. The method decomposes the logarithmic impedance model into piecewise-constant and smooth components. TV and Tikhonov constraints are imposed on these components, respectively, in both the vertical and horizontal directions. The performance of the inversion depends critically, among other factors, on the relative weight of the total regularization term with respect to the data misfit, the relative weight of the two regularization components (the balancing parameter), and the efficiency of computing a candidate solution for a given set of parameters. In this paper, we propose a new adaptive algorithm based on alternating directions, in which (i) a fast candidate-solution computation scheme is developed in the spectral domain by exploiting the Sylvester structure and fast Fourier transforms, thereby reducing the computational cost of parameter searching; (ii) the regularization parameter is selected adaptively using the residual whiteness principle; and (iii) the balancing parameter is adaptively updated according to robust statistical characteristics of the impedance-model gradients. Synthetic and field-data tests demonstrate that, compared with conventional Tikhonov and TV methods, the proposed method better preserves impedance interfaces while reducing staircase artifacts and inter-trace discontinuities, thereby improving the reconstruction of impedance structures.

Accelerating fits in high-energy physics and beyond
Authors: Amr Elmarassy, Sebastian Schmitt, Eluned Smith
First: 2026-10-14T17:49:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:38:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Data analysis in particle physics often involves optimizing a loss function that quantifies how well a theoretical model describes the observed data, in order to estimate the parameters of the model. This is referred to as fitting. As datasets grow larger and more expensive analysis methods are used, the time required to perform this parameter estimation can be a limiting factor. This work explores techniques for accelerating the fitting process, and measures their performance on a set of models representative of a variety of physics fits. We compare the speed and precision of variants of well-known optimization algorithms, namely Newton's method, BFGS, and the Migrad algorithm (commonly used in particle physics fits). We observe significant speedups when using some algorithms: most notably, some variants of BFGS are about ten times faster than Migrad, while achieving the same precision. For quasi-Newtonian algorithms like BFGS and Migrad, we find that the initial value of the Hessian matrix has a large impact on the runtime. We additionally discuss methods for accelerating the evaluation of the loss function and its derivatives, using algebraic simplifications, automatic differentiation, and modern hardware features. We explore techniques for achieving good precision with 32-bit operations, which can achieve significantly higher throughput on the GPU. Lastly we explore efficient parallelization strategies for the CPU and GPU. All of the techniques and algorithms in this paper are implemented in our new FastFit library, which was used for all of our benchmarks.

Abstract
Phenomena responsible for emerging technologies originate from novel molecular states at buried interfaces. Yet quantitatively resolving these features is out of reach in most cases. Resonant soft X-ray scattering (RSoXS) has demonstrated sensitivity to these local states but is limited due to a lack of accurate interpretive models and spatial resolution. Here we demonstrate a machine learning (ML)-optimized optical model that establishes the direct and quantitative interpretation of molecular orientation, chemical identity and packing states with sub-monolayer resolution - reaching beyond the diffraction limit. Abstract
Rapid intensification (RI) is one of the most dangerous and unpredictable phases of tropical cyclones, and its environmental and structural drivers are difficult to identify and separate in observations. Here we introduce CausalVisionRI, a causal-prior-constrained deep-learning system for 24-h RI warning in the western North Pacific. It integrates infrared remote sensing imagery, atmospheric and oceanic fields, and an observationally discovered causal graph into a unified model. The graph and eight sign-verified effects on 24-h intensity change provide an auditable causal prior for training. Discovered from 522 cyclones in 2000-2017, it identifies causal pathways linking atmospheric and oceanic conditions, the cyclone's dynamical and structural state, and its 24-h intensity change. On a forward holdout covering 2020-2024 and 3,004 records, the five-member ensemble achieves a Brier skill score (BSS) of 0.259 relative to a fixed development climatology, significantly exceeding four reference systems; the three LDA baselines reach at most 0.105. It also attains higher detection rates than a published knowledge-block scheme and a reconstructed contrastive-learning scheme at matched false-detection rates under our USA 30-kt standard and the published RITCF standard. Ablations show that the graph pathway adds 0.032 BSS across the holdout and 0.063 over 2020-2021, with a significant contribution in one ensemble member. A deep-learning model constrained by observationally discovered causal structure thus offers a new route to physically informed RI warning.

Abstract
We present posterior-landscape, a Python framework to identify and characterize morphological features in two-dimensional probability densities. The framework combines concepts from topological data analysis, statistical density-ridge estimation, and scale-space image analysis. It extracts localized concentrations, valleys, ridges, and changes of slope; for Bayesian reconstructions, it propagates uncertainty in feature recovery, geometry, and correspondence across posteriors. The framework also connects two-dimensional structures to features in one-dimensional marginals, and quantifies their correlation with other inferred parameters. We validate the method on controlled densities, and demonstrate its use on gravitational-wave population reconstructions, where it provides a systematic way to identify and interpret population features and assess which ones carry information relevant for cosmology. The resulting catalogues also offer a common morphological representation for future comparisons between predictions and population reconstructions.

Conformal Annotation with Randomized Expert Review: Application to Online Cell-Type Labeling
Authors: Xuhe Zhang, Zhi Huang, Ying Jin
First: 2026-10-29T15:20:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T15:20:53+00:00
Comments: 48 pages, 17 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Artificial intelligence (AI) models can accelerate cell-type annotation in digital pathology, but safely scaling their use requires determining, with error control, which model-generated labels can be accepted without expert review. This is challenging in evolving workflows because cell morphology and type composition may change across tissue regions and slides, making one-time calibration unreliable. We propose Conformal Annotation with Randomized Expert review under Distribution shift (CARED), a distribution-free framework that leverages randomized expert audit to achieve long-run error control for AI-assisted annotation under distribution shift. For each incoming batch, CARED uniformly selects a subset of cases for expert review and uses the acquired labels to calibrate later decisions. Among unreviewed cases, CARED determines a subset of them to safely skip expert labeling, while suggesting candidate labels for the others for downstream resolution. We prove that this workflow provides long-run control of cumulative error over both the full stream and the cells outside of the audit sample, without stationarity or exchangeability assumptions. We further enhance the framework by introducing class-conditional guarantees to ensure error control within important but rare cell types, together with a utility module to target more automatic resolutions, smaller candidate sets, and greater biological coherence, without compromising validity. Experiments on pancreas, lung, and ovary annotation streams, together with a stream constructed from real expert annotations, show that CARED controls the cumulative errors while a substantial fraction of cells (up to 70%) bypass expert review.

Abstract
Unrecognized systematic errors can survive standard checks and undermine scientific discoveries. Null tests and experimental controls can expose these failures, but averaging measurements or reducing them to scalar statistics can hide important structure. We propose ensemble null tests that compare multivariate distributions with references from observations or simulations. We demonstrate their benefits across science. In cosmic microwave background simulations, they detect systematic effects missed by conventional $χ^2$ tests. In Gaia data, they detect velocity asymmetry as sensitively as an informed search. In a CRISPR screen, they expose a control mismatch in DNA damage and reveal which cellular profiles drive it. As scientific datasets grow, ensemble testing turns repeated measurements into a broader search for unknown unknowns, strengthening the empirical foundation for scientific discovery.

Abstract
Skillful medium-range precipitation forecasting at kilometer scale remains challenging over complex terrain because precipitation arises from multiscale nonlinear processes that global models cannot explicitly resolve at affordable cost. Global AI weather models can produce skillful medium-range forecasts, but their native 0.25 degrees resolution limits direct use for local hazard applications. Statistical downscaling can help bridge this gap, yet existing approaches often struggle with state-dependent, and especially lead-time-dependent, biases in global forecasts. We introduce SwAIther-Precip, a lead-time-aware downscaling framework that converts coarse-resolution AIFS forecasts into probabilistic km-scale precipitation fields over Switzerland. First, a U-Net conditioned on lead time via feature-wise linear modulation deterministically corrects systematic biases at coarse resolution. This targeted correction enables a cheaper super-resolution stage conditioned only on corrected precipitation, allowing direct training on observations rather than on the full atmospheric state. A diffusion-based model then generates fine-scale spatial variability independently of lead time. Using AIFS forecasts and CombiPrecip radar-gauge observations, SwAIther-Precip reduces CRPS by 48% relative to raw AIFS. The generated fields reproduce observed spatial variability with spectral fidelity above 0.85 at large scales and 0.88 at small scales, corresponding to an effective resolution of approximately 4 km on a 1 km grid for lead times up to 5 days. Training across lead times further improves long-range performance, yielding a 13% CRPS reduction at 6 days relative to lead-time-specific models. These results show that explicitly correcting lead-time-dependent biases before generative super-resolution is key to efficient km-scale probabilistic downscaling of global AI precipitation forecasts.

Consistent Bayesian meta-analysis on subgroup specific effects and interactions
Authors: Renato Panaro, Christian Röver, Tim Friede
First: 2025-12-21T15:57:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T14:11:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Clinical trials commonly report effects on the full study population as well as for patient subgroups. Random-effects meta-analyses of subgroup-specific effects and treatment-by-subgroup interactions might be misaligned, especially when subgroups contribute differently to study populations. We show that, after contribution adjustment, subgroup meta-analysis can recover the same interaction inference regardless of whether interaction or subgroup data are used. Our framework for subgroup-data interaction meta-analysis has four advantages. First, it adjusts for variation in relative subgroup contribution, quantified by the within-trial information fraction. Second, it is robust to prevalence imbalance and variation. Third, it provides a self-contained model-based approach. Fourth, it can incorporate prior information into Bayesian interaction meta-analysis. The method demonstration uses an example application including 7 trials of disease-modifying therapies in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis; the contribution-adjusted model manages to reproduce the results from the interaction meta-analysis while yielding matching estimates for the subgroup effects. By controlling subgroup contribution while retaining subgroup interpretability, this approach allows for interaction decision-making when published subgroup data are available. Although the proposed model is presented in the Bayesian context, it can also be implemented in frequentist or likelihood frameworks.

Abstract
Open-ocean in situ winds are sparse, limiting the calibration and validation of satellite wind products. We evaluate wind-vector retrieval from MELODI (Miniaturized Electronics Lagrangian Oceanographic DrIfter), a compact freely drifting GNSS/IMU wave buoy. Wind speed is inferred from the shape of the measured wave acceleration spectrum. A regularized regression with a gated nonlinear correction (Wind Inversion using Tikhonov Regularization, WITR), trained on scatterometer winds and evaluated by leave-one-buoy-out cross-validation against ASCAT MetOp-B/C and HY-2B/C, gives a root-mean-square difference (RMSD) of 0.91 m/s. Symbolic regression distills the trained model into compact spectrum-only and motion-enhanced equations suitable for onboard use; the reduced drag law gives 0.93-0.94 m/s. Wind direction is estimated, without any fitted reference-wind regression, from IMU-derived directional wave moments in the 0.60-0.90 Hz wind-sea band, with a circular mean absolute angular difference (MAAD) of 9.4 degrees against ASCAT. These differences are comparable to published scatterometer-moored-buoy comparisons and improve substantially on the traditional single-band Toba inversion. Validation is strongest from 4 to 14 m/s and sparse above approximately 16 m/s; stronger winds are assigned low confidence. The results show that small wave-sensing drifters, calibrated to the well-established scatterometer wind scale, can provide useful wind-vector observations for validating satellite wind products and for atmospheric and wave modeling; stronger-wind sampling remains a priority.

Subgroup comparisons within and across studies in meta-analysis
Authors: Renato Panaro, Christian Röver, Tim Friede
First: 2025-08-21T13:07:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T14:02:38+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Meta-analyses of published subgroup-level data may yield different interaction estimates depending on how the available data are weighted across studies. In particular, the difference between meta-analytic subgroup-specific effects need not equal the meta-analysis of within-study subgroup differences when these quantities are based on different study weights. Variation in subgroup prevalence across trials, including the presence of single-subgroup trials, may further amplify this discrepancy and introduce aggregation bias into across-study subgroup comparisons. We examine established approaches for jointly or separately estimating subgroup-specific effects and treatment-by-subgroup interactions and introduce the Same Weighting Across Different Analyses (SWADA) principle. SWADA applies a common set of study weights across the connected subgroup and interaction analyses, thereby guaranteeing algebraic agreement between the difference of subgroup averages and the average subgroup difference. Through simulation studies and meta-analyses of randomised trials of COVID-19 treatments, we compare several SWADA weighting schemes with existing approaches in terms of bias, coverage, precision, and root mean squared error. SWADA ensures internally coherent subgroup and interaction estimates, but may require down-weighting or excluding studies that provide information for only one subgroup. The choice of weights therefore involves a trade- off between algebraic coherence, use of the available subgroup evidence, and statistical efficiency. SWADA should be viewed as a general weighting principle for meta-analytic comparisons rather than as a universally preferable estimator.

Abstract
Many physical inverse problems are mediated by expensive stochastic simulators for which response gradients, adjoint sensitivities, or a differentiable simulator interface are unavailable. We introduce \emph{Multiple Target-based Confident Acquisition} (MUTACO), an uncertainty-aware sequential-design method for response matching that requires only noisy forward evaluations and learns the multicomponent response together with its predictive uncertainty. We develop and evaluate MUTACO using a heterogeneous network of stochastic phase oscillators. The inverse task is to infer statistical properties of the local potential and interactions from macroscopic rotation and synchronization curves under multiple driving conditions. Rather than requiring unique recovery of these statistical properties, MUTACO searches for parameter configurations whose predicted rotation and synchronization responses are compatible with the target response while accounting for predictive uncertainty. This response-centered formulation is appropriate when experimentally accessible observables characterize ensemble statistics rather than a particular microscopic realization. Under strictly limited simulation budgets, MUTACO achieves the lowest held-out response error among the tested adaptive-search, space-filling, and simulation-based inference methods, while also yielding lower parameter-recovery error than the compared methods. These results demonstrate that response-targeted sequential design is a simulator-efficient approach to inverse modeling from finite noisy forward evaluations and suggest broader applicability to stochastic physical systems with correlated multicomponent responses and weakly constrained parameter directions.

Abstract
Airflow-driven advection is the primary mechanism for gas and aerosol dispersion in real-world scenarios, making accurate airflow estimation essential for ventilation monitoring, gas source localization, and contaminant spread analysis. Since obtaining airflow maps via high-resolution spatial sensing is usually infeasible in practice, flow field estimation methods must cope with sparse measurements by introducing statistical or physical priors to compensate for limited data. Existing approaches mainly differ in how they incorporate fluid-flow constraints and environmental geometry. However, due to the lack of systematic comparisons across this spectrum of methods, the potential benefits of physics-based priors for airflow estimation under sparse sensing remain underexplored. To address this question, we quantitatively evaluate four airflow estimators across three simulated 2D scenarios under steady-state conditions and varying sampling densities. The methods range from Gaussian Process Regression and graph-based Gaussian Markov Random Fields to two Navier-Stokes-based approaches, including a novel strong-form residual minimization variant. Our results show that physical priors provide the greatest benefit at low sampling densities by compensating for missing spatial information. Although data-driven Gaussian Process Regression becomes increasingly competitive as sensor coverage increases, model-based estimators retain an accuracy advantage across the evaluated scenarios.

Abstract
Quantum tomography and quantum retrodiction are traditionally viewed as separate inference tasks. We show that the two are manifestations of the same underlying principle. Abstract
