Learning by Confusion Locates a Parametrization-Dependent Fisher Information Maximum
Authors: George Issa, Richard Scalettar
First: 2026-10-26T03:14:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:46:43+00:00
Comments: 5 pages, 4 figures; Supplemental Material: 2 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Learning by confusion (LBC) is a machine-learning method for locating phase transitions without assuming a known order parameter. Its characteristic accuracy curve contains a central maximum that is interpreted as the transition point, but what determines the location and finite-size scaling of this maximum has remained unclear. We derive an exact balance condition for the Bayes-optimal confusion peak and show that, for equilibrium systems with a single swept parameter, the peak closely follows the maximum of the corresponding Fisher information. This immediately gives its finite-size scaling and reveals an unexpected dependence on how the data are sampled: the same configurations arranged on grids uniform in $T$, $\ln T$, or $β=1/T$ can yield different finite-size pseudocritical trajectories. Tests on the Ising, Blume-Capel, Potts, and Baxter-Wu models confirm the prediction across continuous, tricritical, and first-order transitions. Our results identify the statistical quantity measured by LBC and show that finite-size machine-learning estimates can depend on the parametrization of the training data as well as on the underlying physics.

Stage-wise semiparametric analysis of multivariate event times under informative censoring via vine copula
Authors: Xinyuan Chen, Yiwei Li, Qian M. Zhou
First: 2025-02-28T00:18:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:40:43+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The analysis of times to multiple nonterminal events arises in many lifetime data settings, where the nonterminal event times are subject to informative censoring by a terminal event, posing substantial challenges. We propose a likelihood-based regression framework that models dependence among multiple nonterminal and terminal event times using a vine copula. This framework allows pairwise associations between nonterminal and terminal events, as well as among nonterminal events, to vary across event types, while enabling valid regression inference for the marginal distributions of individual event times. To facilitate computation, we develop a stage-wise pseudo-likelihood estimation procedure and establish its large-sample properties. We demonstrate satisfactory finite-sample performance of the proposed estimators and associated variance estimators through simulation studies. We also illustrate the proposed methodology using data from a crowdfunding platform to examine the relationship between different types of creator-backer interactions and a creator's lifetime on the platform.

Causal DAG-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Long-Term Physical Activity Maintenance: Using Prior Data to Inform Future Deployment
Authors: Xueqing Liu, Young Hyun Cho, Bhanu Teja Gullapalli, Predrag Klasnja, Susan Murphy
First: 2026-10-26T17:38:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:07:14+00:00

Abstract
Digital health interventions can provide scalable, personalized support for physical activity (PA), yet sustaining PA over the long-term remains difficult. Because affective experiences with PA are important for activity sustainment, we developed an online reinforcement learning (RL) system for the forthcoming ADAPT factorial trial that personalizes twice-daily activity suggestions aimed at improving affective associations with PA. This setting poses several challenges: participant-specific data are scarce relative to the candidate state space, important states and rewards are partially observed, interventions are frequent while the primary outcome is measured weekly, and activity suggestions can both improve PA-related experiences and increase intervention burden. We address these challenges using an expert-specified causal directed acyclic graph as an integrated RL design tool, guiding state construction, estimation of partially observed states, delayed credit assignment, and representation of beneficial and adverse intervention pathways. We further construct a longitudinal simulator calibrated from a prior micro-randomized trial to compare candidate RL designs under realistic state evolution, missingness, and participant heterogeneity before deployment. Together, this work illustrates how causal domain knowledge and prior trial data can make long-horizon, participant-specific RL more practical for digital health interventions targeting sustained behavior change.

Can LLM Agents Improve Imperfect Earth System Model Parameterizations?
Authors: Kara D. Lamb
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-11-02T18:05:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:05:04+00:00
Comments: Sim2Sci Workshop, NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Earth system models (ESMs) rely on parameterizations to represent unresolved processes such as convection, cloud microphysics, and turbulence. However, translating advances in physical understanding into stable, validated parameterizations and integrating them into ESMs often takes years. Agentic AI could accelerate this process and enable more systematic exploration of design choices. Using warm-rain collision-coalescence as a test case, we evaluate agents' ability to synthesize scientific literature, reimplement published bulk parameterizations, and propose new functional forms as differentiable JAX code. We compare conventional parameterizations, data-driven emulators, and agent-generated closures against high-fidelity superdroplet simulations, repeating unattended experiments across ten language models. With expert guidance or iterative feedback, agents produced executable, differentiable, mass-conserving code, although unattended performance depended strongly on the scheme and language model. Iterative feedback lowered median rollout error for eight of ten models. Retuned physical schemes matched or outperformed many learned parameterizations at substantially lower computational cost, but two of 100 unattended agent-generated closures achieved slightly lower rain-mass error than the most accurate retuned scheme on held-out simulations.

Soccer: a quantitative analysis of team resilience and the miracle of Bern

Abstract
Resilience is the ability to positively respond to adversity. It has been studied in psychology for several decades, with focus on how individuals overcome traumata or cope with setbacks and obstacles in their professional career. Research on resilience in the sport context is rather new. Activities are based on insights that in highly competitive environments, tiny effects tip the scales. A key question of measuring resilience in sports is what parameters to measure. Here a novel concept is proposed to measure the resilience of soccer teams. The relative frequency of matches is determined, where a soccer team, which is initially trailing by 2 goals, finally succeeds to win the match or at least to reach a draw. The analysis is applied to the last 59 seasons of the German premier soccer league Bundesliga. The empirical data are compared with a theoretical model derived from Poisson distributions. It is shown how leading teams in the premier soccer league differ from the average with respect to resilience, which provides further insights into the hidden secrets of top soccer teams.

A Geometry-Informed Neural Operator surrogate of LISFLOOD-FP for rapid pluvial-fluvial flood forecasting: the case of Forlì, Italy
Authors: Samuele Maria Garofalo, Fabio Limardo, Simonetta Bodojra, Mohammad Amin Nabian, Mehdi Taghizadeh
First: 2026-11-02T16:22:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:22:25+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 12 figures, 3 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Physically based two-dimensional hydraulic models are too slow for real-time inundation forecasting and large ensembles. We present a time-dependent geometry-informed neural operator (GINO) surrogate of LISFLOOD-FP for the municipality of Forlì (Italy), severely affected by the May 2023 Emilia-Romagna floods. The surrogate is designed for an operational setting in which only river stage gauges and rain gauges are available. A reproducible pipeline turns public regional observations into a physically based training set: each river inflow is driven by the first stage gauge downstream of it through a calibrated water-surface boundary condition, and rain enters as SCS-CN effective rainfall. The surrogate is an autoregressive GINO trained on 216 simulations of 24 observed events with scaled forcing. It predicts volume-conserving polygon-mean depths on an exposure-based Voronoi mesh of 659 nodes, with a 20 min step over 72 h. The surrogate is tested on 38 independent observed events that it never saw during training. On these events it reaches a critical success index (CSI) of 0.934 at 0.05m and 0.844 at 0.3 m, a depth RMSE of 0.008m and an $R^2$ of 0.984 for maximum-depth maps. On one NVIDIA A10G GPU a 72-h forecast takes 1.7 s instead of 55 s with LISFLOOD-FP, and a 50-member ensemble, computed in a single batched rollout, takes 6.2 s instead of about 46 min, which makes probabilistic flood forecasting practical. These results show that neural-operator surrogates can deliver accurate pluvial-fluvial flood forecasts driven by river stage for operational early warning.

Selecting Hyperparameters for Tree-Boosting
Authors: Floris Jan Koster, Fabio Sigrist
First: 2026-02-05T15:44:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:12:26+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Tree-boosting is a widely used machine learning technique for tabular data. However, its out-of-sample accuracy is critically dependent on multiple hyperparameters. In this article, we empirically compare several popular methods for hyperparameter optimization for tree-boosting including random grid search, the tree-structured Parzen estimator (TPE), Gaussian-process-based Bayesian optimization (GP-BO), Hyperband, the sequential model-based algorithm configuration (SMAC) method, and deterministic full grid search using $59$ regression and binary classification data sets. We find that the SMAC method clearly outperforms all the other considered methods on average, and it gives stable performance across a diverse collection of tabular data sets under a fixed tuning budget, which is relevant for users who cannot afford extensive manual trial-and-error tuning. We further observe that (i) a relatively large number of trials larger than $100$ is typically required for accurate tuning, (ii) using default values for hyperparameters or a full search over a small grid often yields very inaccurate models, (iii) all considered hyperparameters can have a material effect on the accuracy of tree-boosting, i.e., there is no small set of hyperparameters that is more important than others, and (iv) choosing the number of boosting iterations using early stopping yields more accurate results compared to including it in the search space for regression tasks.

Methods for Assessing Distributional Assumptions in Individual Participant Data Meta-Analysis of Diagnostic Tests
Authors: Diribsa T. Bedada, Zelalem F. Negeri
First: 2026-11-02T15:53:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T15:53:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Meta-analysis of diagnostic test accuracy (DTA) studies is commonly based on bivariate generalized linear mixed models (GLMMs) that assume normally distributed study-specific random effects. In practice, this distributional assumption is rarely examined, particularly in individual participant data meta-analysis (IPDMA), where empirical Bayes (EB) estimates exhibit heterogeneous precision across studies. Applying standard multivariate normality tests to such estimates can therefore lead to misleading inference. We propose a weighted extension of the Henze-Zirkler (HZ) test to assess bivariate normality of random effects, accounting for study-specific precision via inverse-variance weighting. To obtain valid finite-sample inference, we develop a parametric bootstrap calibration procedure. Extensive simulation studies showed that the classical HZ test applied to EB estimates can exhibit substantial Type I error inflation, whereas the proposed weighted procedures maintain nominal error rates while retaining power to detect departures from normality. The proposed method provides a practical, statistically principled diagnostic tool for assessing random-effects distributional assumptions in hierarchical models, with particular relevance to IPDMA of DTA studies. Although motivated by IPDMA, the proposed approach also extends to aggregate-data diagnostic test accuracy meta-analysis based on study-level 2-by-2 tables.

Abstract
A lightweight neural network is described that produces six-hourly, spatially detailed probabilistic quantitative precipitation forecasts (PQPF) from a sequence of hourly spatially detailed probabilistic forecasts, for lead times from 6 to 72\,h. The hourly forecasts were generated by a ``zero-inflated'' Attention Residual U-Net that outputs the parameters of a probability distribution including a probability of no precipitation and a weighted mixture of two Gamma distributions at each grid point for each hourly forecast lead time. The six-hourly network passes the six consecutive hourly parameter sets, together with summary statistics of the spatial texture of the raw deterministic forecast, through a small recurrent (GRU) layer and a multi-layer perceptron, and maps them to a corresponding zero-inflated mixture of three Gamma distributions describing the six-hourly accumulated precipitation. Training uses sets of hourly probabilistic forecasts, drawn preferentially from wet and heavy-precipitation cases (importance sampling), and matched Multi-Radar Multi-Sensor (MRMS) six-hourly precipitation accumulations, subject to a quality threshold. The resulting six-hourly probabilistic forecasts are well calibrated and skillful, as demonstrated by reliability diagrams, performance diagrams and Brier Skill Scores evaluated over an out-of-sample period spanning day 12 through the end of every month of 2025. The Brier Skill Scores are consistently and significantly higher than those of a baseline that sums independent draws from the hourly distributions. Forecasts show realistic modulation of precipitation related to terrain variations

Continuous SUN (Stable, Unique, and Novel) Metric for Generative Modeling of Inorganic Crystals
Authors: Masahiro Negishi, Hyunsoo Park, Kinga O. Mastej, Aron Walsh
Venue: Machine Learning: Science and Technology 7 (2026) 035064
First: 2025-10-14T11:38:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T15:28:59+00:00
Comments: 32 pages (19 pages of main text). Revised following peer review. Published in IOP Machine Learning: Science and Technology 7 (2026) 035064. Code: https://github.com/WMD-group/xtalmet
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
To address pressing scientific challenges such as climate change, increasingly sophisticated generative models are being developed to efficiently sample the large chemical space of potential functional materials. The proliferation of these models has necessitated the establishment of rigorous evaluation metrics. While uniqueness (U), novelty (N), and stability (S) of samples serve as standard metrics, their current formulations show several limitations. U and N rely on binary comparisons of crystals, rendering them dependent on heuristic thresholds, incapable of quantifying the degree of similarity, sensitive to atomic coordinate perturbations, and not invariant to sample permutation. Similarly, the binary assessment of S risks a premature exclusion of marginally unstable yet potentially novel candidates. These limitations are addressed by making the aforementioned metrics continuous. Furthermore, we integrate them into a unified metric ``continuous SUN" (cSUN), which offers a smoother score distribution and greater tunability than the conventional binary SUN metric. Experimental results demonstrate that our continuous metrics provide granular insights into sample distributions and secondarily serve as a soft screening score for selecting samples for further analysis. Finally, the use of cSUN as a reward signal in reinforcement learning is explored, showing that its adjustable weighting scheme effectively enhances sample diversity and convergence to a better optima in a Chemeleon2 case study.

At the same time, general political understanding seems to be that the House of Commons is currently further away from a pure two-party distribution of seats than at any time in the past century, perhaps longer. DeepWaveRL: Self-Supervised Full Waveform Inversion via Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Peng Jin, Yinan Feng, Yinpeng Chen, Youzuo Lin, Sharon Huang
First: 2026-11-02T15:10:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T15:10:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Full Waveform Inversion (FWI) is a fundamental inverse problem in geophysics to estimate subsurface properties, such as velocity, from seismic measurements. Recently, self-supervised learning methods remove the need for ground-truth velocity maps during training by leveraging a forward operator to simulate seismic data from predictions and backpropagate the gradients of reconstruction loss through it. In this work, we consider a more challenging setting without accessing such gradients. To this end, we incorporate reinforcement learning into self-supervised FWI and propose a framework named DeepWaveRL. Specifically, we reformulate FWI as a policy learning problem, where the policy network predicts velocity maps as actions, and a forward operator is used solely for simulation. A global scalar reward is computed based on data misfit for each prediction and used to weight policy gradients. Thus, learning proceeds without accessing the gradients from the forward operator. Given the indirect and coarse supervision signals, we further identify key strategies to stabilize learning and mitigate the spatial credit assignment bottleneck. Experimental results show that, despite relying entirely on a single scalar reward, DeepWaveRL achieves competitive performance compared to supervised and self-supervised baselines. These findings suggest that our framework offers a flexible and complementary learning paradigm for the FWI research community.

Anomaly-ranked confirmation slows new-class learning

Abstract
Anomaly detectors rank unfamiliar examples for expert confirmation, and the confirmed examples teach a classifier to recognise a new class. Across five benchmarks from physics, medicine and computer vision, the members flagged by the detector are those the classifier learns first. In three systems, confirming the highest-ranked members of a new class gives lower recognition than confirming the same number of random members. Handing subsequent selection to the promoted classifier recovers part of this loss in two systems. A paired intervention further shows that replacing nearby known classes with distant ones sharply reduces the confirmations needed for recognition. These results distinguish finding a new class from selecting examples that teach its extent: within the tested classes, concentrating confirmations at the top of the anomaly ranking makes learning less efficient.

Abstract
Using standard financial ratios as variables in statistical analyses has been related to several serious problems, such as extreme outliers, asymmetry, non-normality, and nonlinearity. The compositional-data methodology has been successfully applied to solve these problems and has always yielded substantially different results when compared to standard financial ratios. Focusing on predictive performance, the results show that compositional methods are better than standard ratios in terms of sensitivity (recall), with mixed results regarding specificity, compositional random forests and compositional logistic regression behaving the best.

Abstract
Global gridded crop models (GGCMs) are crucial for projecting climate impacts on agricultural productivity and assessing associated risks to food security. However, state-of-the-art GGCMs are subject to substantial uncertainties arising from reduced representations of complex processes. Projected increases in the frequency and severity of climate extremes require more reliable agricultural yield simulations. Recent machine learning approaches trained on observational data provide alternatives to GGCMs, but remain under-constrained due to sparse observations and show poor out-of-distribution generalization. For large-scale, long-term simulations, NeuralCrop runs nearly two orders of magnitude faster than the CPU-based LPJmL baseline model, mainly thanks to GPU acceleration. Our results highlight end-to-end hybrid crop modelling as a promising avenue for improving crop simulations and informing food security assessments under climate change.

Abstract
New federal legislation will require states with high payment error rates in the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) to pay a share of benefit costs, potentially costing some states over a billion dollars per year. These costs create substantial pressure to improve payment accuracy. One strategy to improve payment accuracy is preauthorization review, in which staff double-check applications and correct mistakes before benefits are issued. But limited staff capacity makes it important to target these reviews effectively. One leading targeting approach uses statistical risk models to prioritize cases likely to contain errors. Another novel approach uses AI to flag specific potential discrepancies for reviewers to check. Under our main assumptions, risk-based review alone costs about seven times as much as combining AI flags with risk scores. This cost advantage persists across a wide range of plausible assumptions. Our findings suggest that AI-assisted preauthorization review could be a key tool to help states avoid substantial cost-sharing obligations.

Abstract
Individual bicycle configurations can be assessed in situ using power and speed measurements without a velodrome or wind tunnel. We apply an integrated energy-balance workflow to five configurations tested by one rider on a road section. Five nominal power levels, each ridden in both directions, yield 25 paired observations. One-Sample Mendelian Randomization and Instrumental Variable Analyses in R and Stata: The OneSampleMR and ivonesamplemr Packages
Authors: Tom Palmer, Nicolai Vitt, Eleanor Sanderson
First: 2026-11-02T10:22:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T10:22:17+00:00
Comments: 25 pages, 1 figure, 2 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Mendelian randomization (MR) uses genetic variants as instrumental variables (IVs) to estimate the causal effect of an exposure on an outcome. When individual-level data on the instruments, exposure, and outcome are available in a single sample (one-sample MR), a range of IV estimators can be used beyond the standard two-stage least squares estimator, including structural mean models (SMMs) and two-stage predictor substitu- tion (TSPS) and two-stage residual inclusion (TSRI) estimators with nonlinear second stage models. These estimators are important when the outcome is binary, where in- vestigators typically wish to report causal risk ratios or causal odds ratios. However, implementations of these estimators are scattered across software packages and are of- ten unavailable with standard errors that account for the estimation of the first stage. We describe two packages which implement these estimators: OneSampleMR for R and ivonesamplemr for Stata. Both packages fit the multiplicative and logistic SMMs and TSPS and TSRI estimators with identity, log-additive, log-multiplicative, and logit links using the generalized method of moments (GMM), so that the standard errors account for the uncertainty in both stages of estimation. Abstract

We introduce the Temporal Seam Score (S-score, or S), a signed, robust measure for assessing continuity at a known transition time.
