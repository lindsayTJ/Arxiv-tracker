Unifying spherical convolutions through lifting
Authors: J. D. McEwen
First: 2026-10-09T17:39:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:39:02+00:00
Comments: 56 pages, 15 figures, Code available at https://github.com/astro-informatics/s2conv
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
We develop a unified description of convolution on the sphere by lifting to the rotation group. A number of definitions of spherical convolution have been proposed, differing in whether the kernel is axisymmetric or directional, whether the output is on the sphere or the rotation group, whether the signals carry spin, and in their computational cost. No unified treatment exists. Regarding the sphere as the homogeneous space $S^2 \cong \text{SO(3)}/\text{SO(2)}$, we lift a spin $s$ field to the rotation group, convolve on $\text{SO(3)}$, and project back to the sphere through the adjoint of the lift for a potentially different spin $s'$. We recover every rotation-equivariant convolution considered previously through constraints on the kernel, spin choices and inclusion or omission of the lift and projection. For maps between fields of fixed input and output spin, we prove that directional kernels, output on the sphere and equivariance cannot be achieved simultaneously. We make explicit that spin fields are sections of associated line bundles, linking the physics and bundle literatures, and express the spin rotation phase as the character of the twist of the section. We present harmonic-space algorithms for the exact and efficient computation of lifted spherical convolutions of band-limited fields by appealing to sampling theorems on the sphere and rotation group. All operators are implemented in the open-source s2conv code, and numerical experiments confirm the reductions and the equivariance of the operators to machine precision. Finally, we illustrate the operators on fields measured on the Earth and on the celestial sky.

Precision Tokenization for Multi-Modal Data in High Energy Physics
Authors: Jeffrey Krupa, Samuel Klein, Nilotpal Kakati, Daniel Murnane, Baran Hashemi, Eilam Gross, Lukas Heinrich, Michael Kagan
First: 2026-10-09T17:23:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:23:30+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Foundation models have recently gained significant attention in fields such as high energy physics (HEP). The tokenizers that convert detector data into discrete inputs for such models, however, are comparatively unexplored. This paper addresses this gap by learning discrete and compressed tokenized representations of HEP datasets. We demonstrate the approach on four modalities covering two different simulated detectors that vary in complexity. Treating tokenization as a rate-distortion problem, a systematic hyperparameter scan yields four design rules for tokenizing HEP data that hold across all set-based modalities studied. The cost of tokenization is evaluated on tasks including jet $p_T$ regression, particle flow reconstruction, and flavor tagging. We find that tokenization preserves much of the information needed for these tasks at compression factors of approximately 4-11$\times$ while keeping the jet $p_T$ resolution below 1%. We also present heptokens, an open-source library for tokenizing HEP data.

Final-state centrality in relativistic heavy-ion simulations from neural networks
Authors: Anibal Bezerra, Fernando G. Gardim
First: 2026-10-09T16:37:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T16:37:27+00:00
Comments: 16 pages + supplemental materials
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Experiments in relativistic heavy-ion collisions determine centrality from final-state quantities, usually the charged-particle multiplicity. Performing the same in hydrodynamic simulations requires millions of events, which is impractical for state-of-the-art hybrid models, and centrality is therefore commonly approximated by the initial entropy or energy. We show that neural networks trained on a few hundred hybrid events predict the event-by-event multiplicity and mean transverse momentum $[p_T]$ with relative errors of 1--2\%, which makes it possible to generate millions of collisions at a small fraction of the cost of the full simulation. For Pb--Pb collisions at $2.76$~TeV and Au--Au collisions at $200$~GeV, the initial-state and final-state definitions select nearly the same events in 10\%-wide classes, but up to 20\% of the events change class when the classes are 1\% wide. We then compute the fluctuations of $[p_T]$ and its Pearson correlations with a proxy of the elliptic flow, estimated from the initial eccentricity, and with multiplicity. The first correlation is insensitive to how centrality is defined, while the second changes substantially. The approach can be applied at every design point of Bayesian analyses at modest additional cost.

Differences among preparatory processes preceding major earthquakes in Kamchatka, 2014-2025, including the MW 8.8 event
Authors: Stanislaw Lasocki, Beata Orlecka-Sikora, Mateusz Olszewski
First: 2026-10-09T14:57:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T14:57:05+00:00
Comments: 29 pages, 7 figures, 1 table
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Earthquake preparation involves progressive damage accumulation, strain localization, and changes in the organization of seismicity. We investigate preparatory processes preceding seven episodes of major earthquakes in the Kuril - Kamchatka subduction zone during 2014 - 2025, including the 29 July 2025 MW 8.8 Kamchatka megathrust earthquake. We track damage evolution using the seismic regularity coefficient dc and seismic strain dynamics coefficient SSD, defined for seismic series. Before 2022, each episode of strong seismicity was preceded by a pronounced dc minimum, commonly accompanied by an SSD minimum, followed by an increase in dc toward a maximum near the strong event or event group. After 2022, dc minima became shallower and were superimposed on a sustained upward trend. We interpret this transition as evidence that the metrics recorded two superimposed processes. The cycles before 2022 reflect complete strain-localization histories on southern or more distant structures that did not contribute to the segment ruptured in 2025. In contrast, the upward dc trend from 2022, with only shallow minima preceding Episodes 6 and 7, marks coupled behavior within the eventual main rupture segment, also influenced by stress transfer from southern source regions.

Abstract
Thyroid nodules affect a large proportion of the general population. Although most are benign, a non-negligible fraction is malignant. Ultrasound imaging is the primary diagnostic modality, and accurate nodule segmentation is a prerequisite for CAD systems to classify malignancy and assess metastatic risk.\\ In this work, we propose a thyroid nodule segmentation framework that integrates a DeepLabV3+, channel-attention modules and post-hoc conformal prediction, providing both accurate mask and pixel-level uncertainty estimates. We address thyroid ultrasound nodule segmentation as the first stage of a pipeline that also includes malignancy classification and metastatic risk prediction. The plain backbone already improves upon previously reported results, with CBAM + Dice-BCE emerging as the best overall configuration.

A data-driven heuristic-ensemble method for behavioural inference in collective-risk dilemma experiments
Authors: Marc Sadurní, Martín F. Díaz, Josep Perelló, Miquel Montero

Abstract
The collective-risk dilemma has become a paradigm for studying cooperation in a wide variety of social experiments, initiatives that generate increasingly rich datasets describing individual actions over successive decision-making processes. Beyond the purely descriptive analysis of such empirical datasets, a methodological framework is required to interpret and reproduce the observed dynamics, a scheme that may be skewed by exogenous biases. Here, we introduce a data-driven, non-parametric method for simulating individual decisions together with a validation protocol for assessing the fidelity level of the outcomes they generate. Our methodological contribution is based on an empirical heuristic ensemble: agents are represented by a finite set of behavioural heuristics, probabilities conditioned on different observable features of the game state, endowed with a hard-voting arbitration that determines their per-round final decisions. This reproducible generative framework allows for behavioural inference in different collective-risk dilemma setups: Behavioural heuristics are seeded directly from empirical data assessing conditional probabilities, without a priori assumptions; a plurality vote with uniform random tie-breaking resolves between competing heuristics each round, adding no free parameters; validation follows a pattern-oriented approach, comparing synthetic and empirical macro-indicators against single-heuristic and uniform-random baselines; synthetic data generated allow exploration of scenarios or experimental setups that have not been empirically realised; as a worked example, we instantiate our methodological approach on the xAire citizen science collective-risk dilemma experiment run in Barcelona.

Abstract
Uncertainty quantification for electric vehicle (EV) charging demand is essential to turn the increasing pressure of EV adoption on power systems into a source of flexibility. We study a large-scale dataset of public charging stations in Scotland, where demand exhibits a hierarchical structure, organized across multiple aggregation levels, from individual charging points to regional and national scales. The charging infrastructure is continuously evolving, with new stations deployed and existing ones occasionally removed, which calls for forecasting and uncertainty quantification methods tailored to this evolving structure. To address these challenges, we combine hierarchical forecasting with recent advances in conformal prediction with missing data. The conformal procedures considered produce valid prediction intervals at every node of the hierarchy and the algorithm CP-MNR-Nested successfully accounts for the time-varying hierarchical structure of the data.

Abstract
Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR) offers a low-cost, wide-area solution for near continuous infrastructure monitoring. However, translating InSAR observations into automated anomaly warnings remains challenging because displacements are relative, affected by uncertainty, and acquired along line of sight (LOS). We introduced a probabilistic warning framework that accounts for these factors. LOS displacements were modelled through a Gaussian distribution. A limit state function was defined to describe the displacement exceedance, and the time-dependent probability of exceedance was evaluated. Instead of a fixed threshold, warnings were identified through abrupt increases in exceedance probability. The framework was validated using three case studies of quay walls in Amsterdam, providing timely anomaly warnings and issued no warnings for the non-collapse case. Results demonstrate the potential of a probabilistic interpretation of InSAR time series for deformation anomaly warning.

The Hydrological Cycle and Circulation of Tidally-Locked Planets with a Finite Moisture Inventory
Authors: Daniel A. Williams, Geoffrey K. Vallis, Stephen I. Thomson, William J. M. Seviour
First: 2026-10-09T12:38:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T12:38:42+00:00
Comments: 17 pages, 12 figures; submitted to PSJ
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Tidally-locked planets express a range of climate regimes distinct from those found in our solar system. Their atmospheres are often dominated by planetary-wide overturning circulations and super- rotating jets. In this paper we explore the hydrological cycle of such planets and how it might depend on the total inventory of condensate. To this end we use a set of idealised GCM simulations to probe the response of the hydrological cycle to the surface moisture budget in their atmospheres. We find that for low moisture inventories the climate quickly develops a barren day side and a moist night side where the moisture is trapped, as might be expected. For higher moisture inventories the drying of the day side is significantly delayed by the development of a substellar lake just east of the substellar point, which is sustained by the presence of a local thunderstorm-like convective system that persists even after the lake disappears. The timescales for the lake to disappear are millennia, suggesting that even a weak transfer of moisture through surface processes could maintain a moist day side. We also find that adjusting the threshold at which the evaporative rate becomes constrained by the remaining surface liquid depth modifies the longevity of the lake and surrounding day side conditions. These findings have implications for understanding a diverse range of planetary climates and habitability, given the distribution of moisture and presence of a substellar lake.

A functional mixed-effects model for the joint analysis of amplitude and phase variations in function-valued traits
Authors: Yilin Chen, Davide Pigoli, John A. D. Aston, Patrick A. Carter
First: 2026-10-09T11:57:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T11:57:53+00:00

Abstract
Quantitative genetics is the study of continuous traits and the statistical analysis of phenotypic variation, that is, the observable differences in physical traits among individuals within a population. If family relationships between individuals are known, phenotypic variation can be partitioned into genetic and environmental components. Some traits, such as growth trajectories, can be described as a function of a continuous index and are known as function-valued traits. However, function-valued traits from different individuals often vary not only in their shape (amplitude) but also in the speed at which the biological process develops (phase). In this work, we present and compare two approaches to account for amplitude and phase variations in estimating the additive genetic and environmental components. The first one follows from a conventional framework where warping functions remove phase variations, and then the aligned curves are analysed. The second approach adopts the perspective of looking at the aligned curve and the warping function as a joint functional object. In both cases, we propose a functional mixed-effects framework that uses functional principal components as the data-driven basis for genetically correlated data. The proposed procedure is applied to simulations and a real-case study of Tribolium castaneum growth trajectories.

Abstract
A realized tail-risk conditional autoregressive expectile (RTCARE) model is proposed for joint forecasting of value-at-risk (VaR) and expected shortfall (ES). Realized tail measures such as the realized quantile and the realized ES, constructed from intraday high-frequency returns, are incorporated into the dynamics and measurement equations of the conditional expectile, allowing information about lower-tail risk to enter the forecasts. The parameters are estimated by Markov chain Monte Carlo, and we evaluate the resulting VaR-ES forecasts in numerical examples and in an empirical application using U.S. stock-return data. Across the considered tail levels, the RTCARE models deliver competitive and often superior forecasting performance relative to existing alternatives, with stable results across Monte Carlo replications and rolling forecast evaluations.

Local Diagnostics of Learned Event Representations for Anomaly Detection at the HL-LHC
Authors: Donatella Genovese, Philip Ploner, Simone Scardapane, Stefano Giagu, Thea Klaeboe Aarrestad
First: 2026-10-09T11:37:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T11:37:06+00:00
Comments: Submission to SciPost

Abstract
Anomaly detection, the identification of collision events that deviate from Standard Model expectations, is one of the most promising model-agnostic strategies for new-physics searches at the Large Hadron Collider. Searching for rare events in learned low-dimensional representations removes the need to commit to hand-crafted observables a priori, but sacrifices the immediate physical interpretation those observables provide. For anomaly detection to enter a discovery pipeline, flagged events must therefore be diagnosed locally, both to distinguish potential new physics from mismodeling or detector effects and to identify observables for targeted follow-up searches. We evaluate supervised and self-supervised neural embedding strategies on COLLIDE-2V, a 750-million-event dataset simulated under realistic High-Luminosity LHC conditions, using the reconstruction error of an autoencoder trained in the learned latent space as the anomaly score. Across the configurations and benchmark signals considered, supervised objectives, which exploit simulation process labels during training, generally outperform their self-supervised counterparts and, in their best configurations, improve on an autoencoder trained directly on kinematic features, raising the signal efficiency at 10% background acceptance on all held-out processes. We further introduce a local diagnostic pipeline that fits a Gaussian Mixture Model to the SM latent space, profiles each component through high-level observables without process labels, localizes the flagged events, and ranks observables by their local discriminating power. The ranking recovers the defining kinematics of the held-out processes, while correlating the anomaly score with the same observables quantifies how closely the score tracks them, revealing cases where the most discriminating observable is not the one the score follows most closely.

Abstract

Is Earthquake Replenishment Sufficient for Unbiased b-Value Estimation?
Authors: Herbian Hokky Prabowo, Giuseppe Petrillo
First: 2026-10-09T10:52:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T10:52:30+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Short-term aftershock incompleteness biases Gutenberg-Richter b-value estimates because many small earthquakes remain undetected immediately after large events. Two strategies address this problem. Earthquake replenishment reconstructs missing events to restore a more complete catalog, whereas robust positive-difference estimators infer the b-value directly from incomplete observations. Whether these approaches provide equivalent information on the magnitude distribution remains open. We address this issue using synthetic aftershock sequences generated with the Epidemic-Type Aftershock Sequence (ETAS) model and degraded through a time- and magnitude-dependent incompleteness model. Incomplete catalogs are replenished using AutoBETA, and b is estimated using the standard maximum-likelihood, b-more-positive, and b-more-incomplete estimators. Replenishment reconstructs the overall structure of the missing-event population and substantially improves conventional maximum-likelihood estimates, although small residual biases remain. By contrast, both positive-difference estimators recover the imposed b-value directly from incomplete synthetic catalogs. Applications to the Ridgecrest, Landers, and Hector Mine sequences show that b-more-incomplete is less sensitive to replenishment than b-more-positive. Ten independent replenishment seeds indicate that seedto-seed variability is small relative to differences among estimators. Replenishment and robust b-value estimation therefore address different aspects of catalog incompleteness: replenishment provides a more complete aftershock representation, whereas robust estimators are preferable when accurate b-value estimation is the primary objective.

Authors: Yuriy Klymenko, Alla Fedorenko, Evgen Kryuchkov, Oleg Cheremnykh

Abstract
Currently available methods for processing atmospheric wave disturbances are often based on integral wavelet transform techniques, which efficiently estimate spectral data but are fundamentally limited by the uncertainty principle. To address this limitation, this work proposes a novel morphological approach based on partitioning spatial disturbance profiles into separate, well-defined wave segments. By focusing exclusively on the local waveform morphology of a central half-wave and its two adjacent out-of-phase counterparts, and taking the modified Morlet wavelet as a primary example of a model wave packet, we successfully reconstruct the complete set of local wave parameters directly from the amplitudes and positions of these three dominant half-waves. Consequently, the theory demonstrates good agreement with observations, confirming the high accuracy of the proposed waveform morphology approach in reconstructing the localized structure of atmospheric disturbances. When applied to evaluating the phase shift between different wave field components, the method reveals that this shift is a weighted combination of the classical monochromatic phase difference and a morphological one determined by the internal symmetry of the underlying wave packets. Although the classical factor yields the dominant contribution, the symmetry-related factor provides a moderate correction to it. The applicability and accuracy of the approach are verified using observational data gathered over several orbits of the low-Earth-orbit satellite Dynamic Explorer 2 as it passed over Earth's polar caps. The obtained results confirm that the observed disturbances correspond to medium-scale acoustic-gravity waves propagating at a small angle to the horizontal plane, demonstrating the broader methodological potential of the proposed technique for analyzing a wide range of wave phenomena.

TypedBench: A Benchmark for Calibration, Framing Sensitivity, and Cost in System One Decision Models
Authors: Rahul Sharma, Andrew B. Duncan, Gaétan Marceau Caron, Sebastian J. Vollmer

Abstract
System One models output calibrated probabilities over typed answers such as categorical choices, ordinal levels, or binary outcomes, via a non-generative interface. Software can act on these probabilities through thresholds, cost-weighted choices, and escalation rules. Consequently, if these probabilities are miscalibrated or wording-sensitive, the software ma take unintended actions leaving human operators with no textual rationale to inspect. Current evaluations largely report accuracy and calibration on public classification datasets without a clear reference. We present TypedBench, a benchmark for typed decision models like Jev built from seven policy-labelled generators and nine evaluation suites. We report accuracy as median and range across paraphrases, and calibration error relative to the finite-sample noise floor of a matched, perfectly calibrated predictor. We assess probability quality through selective prediction, ordinal proper scoring rules, and realised cost under asymmetric cost matrices. We evaluate a hosted model, an open encoder, and a family of open decoders spanning 0.8B-9B parameters on identical items. The hosted model follows the stated policy but is wording-sensitive and systematically underconfident; under asymmetric costs, using its probabilities can be worse than taking its top answer. Decoders route exactly and are slow as options or questions are added. The decoder is least accurate on policy questions and degrades with more options. Overall, typed decision models must be evaluated jointly on policy adherence, wording robustness, probability quality, and induced decision outcomes.

Sequential Conformal Calibration under Recursive Selection
Authors: Yuning Yang, Zexuan Liu, Tianci Wang, Danlin Li
First: 2026-10-09T03:38:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T03:38:10+00:00
Comments: 55 pages, 1 figure
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Conformal prediction provides finite-sample, distribution-free coverage guarantees that can support decision-making in specific settings. In multistage problems, earlier decisions determine the populations relevant to later calibration. When later scores are refit using earlier calibration results, reusing those cases for ranking need not preserve exchangeability with a surviving test case. We study sequential conformal calibration with a fresh fold at each stage: conditional on earlier folds, the selected fold scores and the surviving test score are exchangeable. This yields finite-sample coverage guarantees for every prefix of the decision sequence. For atomless survivor score distributions, we derive a universal Binomial-thinned Beta chain for the realized coverage process. Its law depends only on fold sizes and target levels, even under predictable refitting, and yields exact coverage slack, abstention probabilities, and calibration-conditional guarantees. We further characterize the tradeoff between freshness and data reuse. For fixed scores, strict-survivor reuse is exactly valid. For adaptive scores refit using past calibration data, reusing survivors can incur worst-case rank loss, while blocked designs provide an intermediate option between full reuse and stagewise fresh folds. As a case study, we apply the framework to serial multi-echelon inventory problem and propose a sequential conditional-quantile formulation. Each stock level is learned as a context-dependent quantile of cumulative demand among scenarios satisfying earlier no-backlog constraints. We connect these targets to common-shift stationarity and derive decision-level implications for the resulting calibrated policies. In a three-echelon simulation, fresh-fold refitting achieves 81.98% final-prefix coverage against an 81.82% target while reducing mean cost by 5.5% relative to the Bonferroni baseline.

Abstract
Estimating the first stage of an instrumental variables (IV) model with the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) requires choosing a dictionary of technical instruments and a penalty level. First-order asymptotic theory offers no guidance on these choices, as any consistent implementation yields a structural parameter estimator with the same limiting distribution. In finite samples, however, these choices can have a substantial impact on the resulting structural parameter estimate. Working in a model with a single endogenous regressor and homoskedastic Gaussian errors, we use first- and second-order Stein identities to derive the approximate mean squared error (AMSE) of the instrumental-variables LASSO (IV-LASSO) estimator, which can be consistently estimated and used to rank a prespecified list of dictionary-penalty candidates. The AMSE reveals a bias-variance trade-off: more complex first-stage fits better approximate the conditional mean of the endogenous variable but are also more correlated with the structural errors, with complexity measured by the degrees of freedom of the LASSO fit. The weight on this bias rises with the endogeneity of the regressor, a quantity that neither plug-in nor cross-validation penalty rules take into account. Despite the AMSE being derived in a Gaussian model, penalty selection by minimizing the feasible AMSE criterion delivers up to a one-third lower mean squared error compared to cross-validation and plug-in penalty rules in Gaussian and non-Gaussian simulation designs calibrated to the data of Gilchrist and Sands (2016).

Abstract
