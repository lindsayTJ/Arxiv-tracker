Abstract
This report applies Causal ANOVA to the COMPAS recidivism prediction dataset to study how demographic variables contribute to variation in two-year recidivism outcomes. Using a directed acyclic graph (DAG) to encode causal assumptions, we estimate counterfactual explainability scores for Race, Sex, and Age Category. We then examine whether the explanatory roles of Race and Sex change after conditioning on age group. The results suggest that Age Category explains the largest share of variation in the full causal model. After conditioning on age, Sex explainability is highest among younger defendants and declines with age, while Race explainability increases with age and is most pronounced among older defendants. As a complementary analysis, we use logistic regression models to interpret the demographic patterns suggested by the Causal ANOVA results. We provide a Python package of Causal ANOVA at https://github.com/devonxiong/causal-anova.

Phase Transitions in Collective Damage of Civil Structures under Natural Hazards
Authors: Sebin Oh, Jinyan Zhao, Raul Rincon, Jamie E. Padgett, Ziqi Wang
First: 2026-02-18T05:31:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:36:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The fate of cities under natural hazards depends not only on hazard intensity but also on the coupling of structural damage, a collective process that remains poorly understood. Here we show that urban structural damage exhibits phase-transition phenomena. As hazard intensity increases, the system can shift abruptly from a largely safe to a largely damaged state, analogous to a discontinuous phase transition in statistical physics. Higher diversity in the building portfolio smooths this transition, but multiscale damage clustering traps the system in a highly volatile regime that suppresses the emergence of predictable Gaussian statistics. The observed phenomenological patterns are interpreted through an effective random-field Ising model, with its key parameters translated into tangible engineering variables such as hazard demand, structural diversity, and modeling uncertainty. These insights establish a phase-aware description of collective damage, revealing that common engineering simplifications can distort the inherent phase of real urban portfolios. Critically, such phase distortions can substantially bias hazard loss estimates, producing relative errors of up to 50% in exceedance probabilities under moderate earthquakes---equivalent to a several-fold gap in anticipated repair costs. The phase-aware description turns the collective behavior of civil infrastructure damage into actionable diagnostics for urban risk assessment and planning.

Tangential and normal partial slip at the liquid-fluid interfaces: An application to a small liquid droplet, gas bubble, and aerosol
Authors: Peter Lebedev-Stepanov
First: 2026-04-20T04:35:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:36:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
An analytical solution is obtained for the problem of the slow movement of a small drop of a fluid in another immiscible fluid in an infinitely large reservoir with the boundary condition of the normal slip and(or) tangential partial slip at the liquid-liquid or gas-liquid interface. That generalizes the conventional Navier and Maxwellian boundary conditions of partial slip. Normal slip is accompanied by the density gradient in the fluid and is applicable only if one of the phases in contact at the interface is a gas. At the first time the generalization of the Hadamard-Rybczynski equation (HRE) is discussed within the formalism of two slip lengths. It is proven that each of the two fluids separated by an interface has its own slip length. The slip lengths of the fluids that contact each other through an interface are related by the ratio of the viscosities of these fluids. New equations describing the terminal velocity of gas bubble rise and aerosol falling have been obtained. The theoretical results are in good agreement with the experiments at moderate Knudsen numbers, Kn < 0.25. It has been shown that the gas density within a rising bubble and around a falling droplet in the air is not uniform. The relative magnitude of the density increment increases with the size of the bubble or aerosol. Presumably, the best applicability of the generalized HRE should be expected for the interface of hydrophobic liquid and hydrophilic one (water - hydrocarbons, water - higher alcohols, in general: aqueous emulsions, water - lipophilic organic liquids and oils, etc.). These are quite important emulsions in practical terms, for example, for the oil industry and medicine. Experimental methods for determining the slip length are considered.

Flowz: When Does Learning Forecast Errors Add Value? Conditional Flow Matching for Financial Price Forecasts
Authors: Giancarlo Vercellino
First: 2026-10-21T16:29:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:29:29+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Learning a forecasting model's errors offers a modular way to improve its predictive distribution while retaining its underlying dynamics. We study this approach with Flowz, a conditional flow model of direct multi-step price-level residuals fitted using random features and ridge regression. Each EWMA, ARIMA, ARFIMA, and structural baseline is compared with a Gaussian residual location-scale control and a conditional Flowz extension. The experiment uses 24 US stocks selected across four liquidity-volatility strata, 104 common forecast origins, horizons of 5, 10, 20, 40, 80, and 160 trading observations, and three fit seeds. Marginal CRPS is the primary score. At farther horizons, Flowz reduces mean CRPS by 30.7% against EWMA and 24.0% against its Gaussian residual control. The EWMA improvement holds at each fit seed and exceeds the Gaussian control for all 24 stocks in the far band. The ARIMA, ARFIMA, and structural extensions worsen both comparators. Conditional residual learning is therefore useful for the drifting EWMA specification in this experiment, with gains concentrated at longer horizons.

Topographically Forced Stationary Waves Can Explain the Near-Surface Zonal Winds Observed by Huygens on Titan
Authors: Sooman Han, Scot Rafkin, J. Michael Battalio, Bowen Fan, Juan M. Lora
First: 2026-10-21T16:03:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:03:29+00:00
Comments: Submitted to PSJ
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Titan's equatorial superrotation is associated with climatological retrograde (westward) near-surface winds at low latitudes, as consistently simulated by Titan general circulation models. However, the Huygens probe measured prograde (eastward) near-surface winds, contrary to the expected circulation. We investigate whether these observations reflect localized forcing by large-scale topography, using paired three-dimensional dry simulations with and without topography in the Titan Atmospheric Model. We find that topography generates longitude-locked, vertically structured zonal wind anomalies that reproduce key features of the Huygens wind profile in the lowest few kilometers. Helmholtz and spectral analyses show that this structure arises from the superposition of several vertically varying low wavenumber modes. A predominantly rotational, topographically forced stationary zonal wavenumber s=1 response provides a major eastward anomaly throughout the lowest 2 km, whereas a locally divergent s=4 response consistent with a gravity wave, opposes it near the surface. Weaker s=2 and s=3 responses also vary vertically and contribute eastward anomalies near the surface. Combined with the retrograde zonal-mean circulation, these modes produce a Huygens-like profile characterized by prograde flow within the lowest 1 km and shear toward retrograde flow aloft. The paired simulations establish their topographic origin, while coherence with the corresponding terrain-forcing components demonstrates that the responses remain phase locked to the forcing. Similar but weaker stationary responses are simulated near the planned Dragonfly landing site, providing baseline expectations for interpreting its future atmospheric measurements. This study demonstrates how large-scale topography can generate localized departures from Titan's zonal-mean circulation and highlights the importance of stationary waves.

Authors: Inna Polichtchouk, Simon Lang, Sarah-Jane Lock, Michael Maier-Gerber, Peter Dueben
First: 2026-03-05T14:47:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T14:51:14+00:00

Abstract
We present the first application of spectral nudging in a probabilistic ensemble forecasting framework, combining the physics-based ECMWF Integrated Forecasting System ensemble (IFS-ENS) with forecasts from the probabilistic machine-learned AIFS-ENS ensemble. Large scales of virtual temperature and vorticity are relaxed toward the machine-learned forecasts, while mesoscale structures remain governed by the physics-based model. This hybrid ensemble shows substantial improvements in large-scale forecast skill, with gains in predictive skill extended by up to two days in the tropics and by approximately half a day in the extra-tropics relative to IFS-ENS. Despite nudging being applied only to upper-air fields, improvements are also found in several near-surface parameters. Tropical cyclone track forecasts improve significantly, consistent with improved representation of the large-scale steering flow, without degrading storm intensity or ensemble spread. These results demonstrate that spectral nudging can be successfully extended to ensemble prediction and provide an attractive pathway for combining machine-learned and physics-based weather prediction systems.

Abstract
Synthetic data hold substantial potential to address practical challenges in epidemiology due to restricted data access and privacy concerns. However, many current methods suffer from limited quality, high computational demands, and complexity for non-experts. Furthermore, common evaluation strategies for synthetic data often fail to directly reflect statistical utility and measure privacy risks sufficiently. Against this background, a critical underexplored question is whether synthetic data can reliably reproduce key findings from epidemiological research while preserving privacy. We propose adversarial random forests (ARF) as an efficient and convenient method for synthesizing tabular epidemiological data. To evaluate its performance, we replicated statistical analyses from six epidemiological publications covering blood pressure, anthropometry, myocardial infarction, accelerometry, loneliness, and diabetes, from the German National Cohort (NAKO Gesundheitsstudie), the Bremen STEMI Registry U45 Study, and the Guelph Family Health Study. We further assessed how dataset dimensionality and variable complexity affect the quality of synthetic data, and contextualized ARF's performance by comparison with commonly used tabular data synthesizers in terms of utility, privacy, generalization, and runtime. Across all replicated studies, results on ARF-generated synthetic data consistently aligned with original findings. Even for datasets with relatively low sample size-to-dimensionality ratios, replication outcomes closely matched the original results across descriptive and inferential analyses. Reduced dimensionality and variable complexity further enhanced synthesis quality. ARF demonstrated favourable performance regarding utility, privacy preservation, and generalization relative to other synthesizers and superior computational efficiency.

Abstract
Airborne transmission of biological matter is likely to cause numerous respiratory distress in coastal populations during certain harmful algal blooms. While we know that dissolved algal toxins aerosolize, the contribution of particle-associated toxins is still unknown. The observation of microalgae in the atmosphere suggests that cells or cell debris could be a carrier. Exchange of particulate matter between ocean and atmosphere is driven by sea spray: microplastics and bacteria aerosolize in jet droplets, following the bursting of air bubbles. However, current models for particle capture only consider surface effects, which fail to explain how large microalgal cells (5-50 um) can be entrapped in jet drops (100-800 um). Here, we demonstrate with a variety of species that microalgae are consistently captured in jet drops, and derive a new particle capture model based on the hydrodynamics of the jet. Using the enrichment factor, which measures the increase in concentration in the first jet drop relative to the bulk, we uncover two regimes. The transition between regimes strongly depends on cell size. In addition, we highlight the dynamics of particles within the jet and derive an enrichment model based on hydrodynamics rather than surface considerations. This work widens and generalizes established results on bacterial and microplastic entrapment in jet drops, resolving discrepancies found in the literature. Finally, we demonstrate that sea spray can aerosolize toxic Ostreopsis cf. ovata cells. This reveals a potential particulate pathway for airborne toxicity during harmful algal blooms and raises the question of the relative contribution of particles to that of dissolved toxins.

Zero-group-velocity modes in textured steel plates: experiments and elastic modeling
Authors: C. Grünsteidl, C. Kerschbaummayr, G. Yan, G. Watzl, F. Rudinger, E. Scherleitner, M. Sonnleitner, C. Hoflehner
First: 2026-10-21T13:08:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T13:08:20+00:00

Abstract
Zero-group-velocity (ZGV) resonances are investigated as a non-contact ultrasonic probe of elastic anisotropy in textured steel plates. Directional laser-ultrasonic measurements were performed on 70% cold-rolled Ti-stabilized interstitial-free steel plates in the full-hard and recrystallized condition. Experimental results were compared with predictions based on effective elastic stiffness tensors derived from X-ray diffraction measurements. Classical Voigt-Reuss-Hill estimates and microstructure-resolved finite-element homogenization reproduce the measured angular dependence of the ZGV frequencies well for both states, with the finite-element KUBC/SUBC bounds considerably narrower than the classical Voigt-Reuss bounds. For the full-hard state, agreement with the texture-based model is obtained only after a recovery treatment. This indicates that dislocations present in the full-hard material contribute to its elastic response, an interpretation that is further supported by theoretical estimations. An identifiability analysis shows that the directional ZGV measurements provide access to at least three independently observable combinations of the stiffness tensor components, and concluding suggestions for application in recrystallization monitoring are provided.

Abstract
This paper describes an approach to constructing nowcast and forecast predictive models for the occurrence of high-latitude decameter ionospheric irregularities based on SuperDARN and SECIRA radar data and space weather parameters. The method relies on self-learning pattern recognition in the dynamics of space weather parameters, that influence the irregularities formation. The proposed approach is a data-driven, multiscale method in which the spatial distribution of irregularities is approximated using a neural network of an original architecture, inspired by graph neural networks. The model was trained and tested using data from 11 radars during the period 2010-2025 (SuperDARN: CVW, ICE, ICW, INV, PGR, PYK, RKN, SAS, STO; SECIRA: EKB, MAGW). It is shown that the model achieves optimal performance when using geomagnetic coordinates MLT,MLAT. The optimal set of parameters for the best nowcast and forecast over studied set of parameters is a combination of geomagnetic and solar disturbance indices ($a_{p}$, $K_{p}$, $AE$, F10.7) from ground-based observations, and satellite-measured solar wind and interplanetary magnetic field parameters ($P_{sw}$, $E_{sw}$, $\widetilde{B_{z}}$, $T_{sw}$). Hourly mean values for all the parameters were used in the analysis. The results indicate that acceptable forecast quality is achieved with a lead time of up to 7-9 hours, with the highest quality reached for nowcast (zero lead time). It is shown that the parameters $\widetilde{B_{z}}$ and F10.7 are significant for nowcasting only and can be neglected for other lead times. It is shown that $AE$ and $T_{sw}$ are significant for mid-term forecast (\textgreater 2hours ahead) and can be neglected for short-term predictions. Statistical relationships between the maximum latitude of the equatorial boundary of the irregularities and space weather parameters are also discussed.

Phase Transitions in Learning Driven by Extremal Data
Authors: Ethan Levien, Trevor GrandPre
First: 2026-10-21T10:22:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T10:22:18+00:00
Comments: 17 pages, 8 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study an extremal regression problem, an example of multiple instance learning (MIL), where the predictors are given as multisets, called bags. The bag elements (called instances) possess hidden labels which are pooled to produce an observed bag-level label. Max pooling is common in MIL applications, but gives rise to vanishing gradients. To handle this, modern MIL pipelines approximate the max with a softmax, treating the sharpness as a learnable parameter, denoted $λ$. When the max pooling model is the true data generating process, learning will be successful when $λ$ is increased during gradient descent, thereby driving the dynamics into regions of parameter space with vanishing gradients. To understand the conditions under which gradient descent breaks down, we study a minimal model and derive a low dimensional projection of the gradient flow in the joint limit of large bags $n$ and high dimension $d$ with $α=\ln n/d$ fixed. The flow has two distinct phase transitions: an intrinsic freezing transition related to the random energy model of disordered systems, and a retrieval transition occurring in modern Hopfield networks. Numerical simulation confirms both the phase diagram and the convergence of the finite-size training gradient to its predicted asymptotic form.

Distinct weak antisymmetric interactions shape human brain functions as probability fluxes
Authors: Yoshiaki Horiike, Shin Fujishiro
First: 2025-08-28T16:19:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T09:55:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The functional computation of the human brain is hypothesized to arise from the collective behaviour of the underlying neural network. A method based on an idea from equilibrium statistical mechanics has been applied to infer the underlying interaction network among the brain regions from whole-brain imaging, but recent studies have challenged the equilibrium assumption. Without equilibrium, how does the human brain encode its function in biased state transitions? Here we show that the probability fluxes, which quantify the irreversibility of state transitions, exhibit unique, task-dependent patterns in spontaneous and task-induced whole-cerebral-cortex activity. We then fit an Ising model with asymmetric interactions and reveal that the symmetric interactions among the brain regions are strong and task-independent, whereas the antisymmetric interactions are weak and task-dependent, and that the fitted model reproduces the observed fluxes for most tasks. Our results indicate that the human brain performs its functional computation by subtly modifying the antisymmetric part of the interactions among the brain regions, offering a new explanation for how a similar interaction network supports diverse brain functions. The method can be applied to the time-series data of other high-dimensional many-body systems to reveal the probability fluxes and infer the underlying interactions among components.

Abstract
Extreme heat represents a growing threat to public health as climate change intensifies. While its mortality impacts are well recognised, the interplay between heat, air pollution, and socioeconomic vulnerability remains insufficiently understood. Adaptation strategies should therefore combine heat-health warnings with air quality management and equity-oriented interventions.

Abstract

Recent models build in more physics knowledge or rely on large-scale pretraining, but the experiments place strict limits on their inference cost. Since this maintenance work entails high costs, vessel operators are confronted with the trade-off between lower fleet emissions and better economic performance. Abstract
Land use regression models relate intra-urban air temperature to spatial predictors such as building volume, tree canopy cover and topography, and are increasingly used to produce fine-scale temperature maps. However, the accuracy of these estimates can be compromised by unrepresentative monitoring networks. Here we show that reference weather station networks, designed for regional rather than intra-urban monitoring, sample only a narrow portion of a city's spatial heterogeneity, missing both the densely built core and the cool, vegetated periphery that set the two ends of the urban temperature range. As a result, city-wide predictions underestimate temperature differences, in ways that cross-validation against the station measurements does not detect. Using Zurich, Switzerland, as a case study, we compare multiple linear regression, generalized additive models and random forests as up to 117 low-cost temperature sensors are added to the four reference weather stations. When trained on reference stations alone, all three models achieve comparable cross-validated performance. As low-cost sensors broaden the sampled spatial heterogeneity, multiple linear regression scores lower on both metrics, whereas the non-linear models remain at their reference-only level. Although a third of the low-cost sensors already recovers nearly the full predicted temperature range, which predictors are identified, and with which sign, is determined by the station sample rather than the model class. At full coverage, only multiple linear regression leaves significant residual spatial autocorrelation, suggesting a linear form unable to capture non-linear responses of air temperature to urban form. Drawing on landscape ecology research on focal site study design, we provide a transferable framework for diagnosing station network representativeness and improving the reliability of city-wide environmental maps.

Abstract
Species distribution models based on thinned point processes are now commonly used in ecology to analyze presence-only data collected through citizen science, including in spatio-temporal frameworks.
