The Physics Is the Network: Fast, Training-Free Neural Emulation
Authors: Luka Todorovic, Kaushal Gianchandani, Sai Ravela
First: 2026-10-14T17:24:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:24:48+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Neural emulation need not require learning a substitute for known physics. We investigate PolyNets that couple a continuous-time neural representation of polynomial dynamics with a simple recurrent network for discrete-time integration. When the governing coefficients are known, the resulting tensor graph requires no training and preserves the prescribed numerical scheme while enabling efficient hardware execution and parallel simulation. Using the one-dimensional Korteweg-de Vries equation, we compare PolyNet with CPU and GPU finite-difference Runge-Kutta solvers and three trained neural models: a state-map emulator, a physics-informed predictor, and a learned-tendency integrator. Analytical soliton solutions provide a reference for amplitude and phase errors across grid resolutions, 10^6 dispersively scaled time steps, ensembles of up to 10^4 trajectories, and varying initial amplitudes. PolyNet achieves accuracy comparable to the numerical solvers, including at amplitudes outside the learned models' training conditions. It combines this fidelity with high GPU throughput, approximately constant over the tested resolution range, and favorable ensemble scaling.

Neural emulation need not require learning a substitute for known physics.
Probabilistic AI models outperform numerical weather prediction models on record breaking heat extremes
Authors: Jakob Benjamin Wessel, Fiona Spuler, Christopher A. T. Ferro, Marlene Kretschmer, Sebastian Sippel
First: 2026-10-14T17:21:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:21:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
AI weather prediction (AIWP) models have emerged as a key complement to physics-based numerical weather prediction (NWP) models, and now often outperform them on aggregate skill. Yet, several studies report that physics-based models still forecast extreme events better, in particular record-breaking extremes, based on the evaluation of deterministic forecasts. Here we evaluate heat extreme forecasts from probabilistic AIWP models over global land and show that they clearly outperform the ECMWF IFS ensemble, both raw and post-processed, including for record-breaking events, in all regions and at all lead times considered. AIWP models better forecast the occurrence and intensity of extremes, are better calibrated in the tail and miss fewer records. Station observations confirm these results. We argue that many reported AIWP limitations stem from unsuitable verification choices, specifically comparing deterministic forecasts that target different quantities. Instead, a fair evaluation of extremes necessitates probabilistic verification.

Propensity score with factor loadings: evaluating the effect of the Paris Agreement on the returns of European stocks
Authors: Angelo Forino, Andrea Mercatanti, Giacomo Morelli
First: 2025-07-11T17:20:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:26:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Factor models for longitudinal data, where policy adoption is unconfounded with respect to a low-dimensional set of latent factor loadings, have become increasingly popular for causal inference. Most existing approaches, however, rely on a causal finite-sample approach or computationally intensive methods, limiting their applicability and external validity. In this paper, we propose a novel method for panel causal inference within a super-population framework, based on inverse propensity score weighting, where the propensity score is a function of latent factor loadings. The approach relaxes the traditional restrictive assumptions of causal panel methods, while offering advantages in terms of causal interpretability, policy relevance, and computational efficiency. Under standard assumptions, we develop a three-step estimation procedure for the ATT and derive its large-sample properties using M-estimation theory. Simulation results show that the proposed estimator remains reliable across a variety of designs, including settings with misspecified factor structure and weakly dependent errors. We apply the method to assess the causal effect of the Paris Agreement - a policy aimed at fostering the transition to a low-carbon economy - on European stock returns. Our empirical results suggest a statistically significant and negative short-run effect on the stock returns of firms that demonstrate a strong commitment to sustainability.

Long-Range Distillation: Distilling 10,000 Years of Simulated Climate into Long Timestep AI Weather Models
Authors: Scott A. Martin, Noah Brenowitz, Dale Durran, Michael Pritchard
First: 2025-12-28T07:03:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:10:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Accurate long-range weather forecasting remains a major challenge for AI models, both because errors accumulate over autoregressive rollouts and because reanalysis training datasets provide limited sampling of the slow modes of variability underpinning predictability. Most AI and all numerical weather prediction models are autoregressive, repeatedly applying short-lead forecasts to reach subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) or seasonal lead times, often causing instability and miscalibration. Long-timestep probabilistic models that generate long-range forecasts in a single step offer an alternative, but training on the 40-year reanalysis record leads to overfitting, suggesting orders of magnitude more data are required. We introduce long-range distillation, whereby a long-timestep probabilistic ``student'' model is trained to forecast directly at long-range using a huge synthetic dataset generated by an autoregressive ``teacher'' model. Using the Deep Learning Earth System Model (DLESyM) as teacher, we generate over 10,000 years of simulated climate to train student models across a range of timescales. In perfect-model experiments, the distilled models outperform climatology and approach their teacher's skill while replacing hundreds of autoregressive steps with a single timestep. In the real world, both a diffusion model student fine-tuned on ERA5 and a linear inverse model student with mixed DLESyM-ERA5 training achieve S2S forecast skill competitive with the ECMWF IFS ensemble, with synthetic pre-training providing improvements beyond ERA5-only training. This demonstrates the benefit of synthetic pre-training for both deep learning and classical data-driven methods, and represents the first demonstration that real-world skill can be improved by training on AI-generated synthetic data in volumes far exceeding the observational record.

Improved Precipitation Variability in the Community Atmosphere Model through Implementation of a Neural-Network Deep Convection Scheme
Authors: Xavier J. Levine, Griffin Mooers, Jack W. Atkinson, Marat F. Khairoutdinov, Janni Yuval, Dominic Orchard, Paul A. O'Gorman
First: 2026-10-14T16:05:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:05:59+00:00
Comments: Submitted to Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems (JAMES). 45 pages, 8 figures; Supporting Information appended
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Conventional deep convection schemes remain a primary source of uncertainty in global climate models, yet replacing them with data-driven alternatives while maintaining physical consistency and numerical stability is an open challenge. We present a hybrid configuration of the Community Atmosphere Model version 6 (CAM6), in which a fully connected deep neural network replaces the Zhang-McFarlane deep convection scheme (ZM), while radiation, turbulence, and other cloud processes are left unchanged. The network is trained on output from a storm-resolving global model - the global System for Atmospheric Modeling (gSAM) - using a coarse-graining approach to derive subgrid-scale convective fluxes and tendencies. This data-driven parameterization is ported to CAM6 and found to run stably for more than a decade while conserving energy and moisture. It produces a broadly realistic climatology without retuning the remaining parameterizations. Relative to the default Zhang-McFarlane scheme, the neural network parameterization substantially improves some long-standing model deficiencies. In particular, the biases in light precipitation and extreme precipitation are markedly reduced, and the diurnal cycle of tropical land precipitation shifts from a spurious midday peak over many regions to the observed late-afternoon peak. These results support targeted ML-based convection schemes as a viable path toward reducing systematic model errors in next-generation climate models.

Cosmic Rays as an Interdisciplinary Earth Observation Tool: From Particle Physics and Atmospheric Processes to Geosciences and Urban Science
Authors: Bugra Bilin, Nuhcan Akçit

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The exploration of cosmic rays, which are high-energy particles originating from space and the atmosphere, has historically been associated with particle physics and astrophysics. In the last 20 years, these particles have evolved into valuable tools for observing Earth's systems. This review compiles the use of cosmic rays in three primary areas: (1) particle physics and atmospheric processes, which include cosmic- ray-induced cascades, ionization, and their impact on atmospheric chemistry and radiation; (2) geosciences, where cosmogenic radionuclides assist in the dating of geological materials and cosmic-ray neutrons are used for large-scale monitoring of soil moisture and snow water equivalents; and (3) urban science, where cosmic- ray muons are employed for non-invasive subsurface imaging and, when paired with distributed sensors, serve as the basis for smart city monitoring. This review places particular emphasis on integrating these methods with remote sensing and geographic information systems (GIS), which helps close the persistent scale gap between point measurements and satellite observations, thereby enabling three-dimensional digital representations of subsurfaces. This review concludes by discussing data standards, their integration into operational Earth observation workflows, and future research directions.

Hyperstatistics
Authors: Lucas Squillante, Samuel M. Soares, Constantino Tsallis, Mariano de Souza
First: 2026-04-23T22:30:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T14:56:57+00:00
Comments: 34 pages, 5 figures, 2 tables. Supplementary material upon request. This version includes a macroscopic generalized hyperstatistical Boltzmann factor $B_H$
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We propose a general approach, named by us hyperstatistics, to treat complex systems, in which Boltzmann-Gibbs statistics breaks down at the domain level of the system. Hyperstatistics preserves the concavity of the nonadditive $q$-entropy. We obtain analytical closed-form expressions for the here proposed ($q, n$)-generalized Boltzmann factor $B_q^{n}$ considering uniform, $γ$, Log-normal, F, and the $q$-$γ$ probability distribution functions. Remarkably, for all investigated distribution functions, $B_q^{n}$ reduces to a $q$-exponential-type function. To demonstrate the applicability of hyperstatistics, we use a table top experiment of the discharge of a capacitor considering $γ$-distributed relaxation times, the pressure decay over time associated with the pumping of $^4$He lines of a closed cycle cryostat, midrapidity data for $\textit{p}$-Pb collisions at the LHC, as well as data set for acceleration distribution in turbulent systems. Furthermore, we deduce the power-law-like $\textit{universal}$ dielectric response using a $q$-generalized $γ$-distribution function proposed in this work. Hyperstatistics is applicable to systems with inherent non-Boltzmann-Gibbsian statistics at the domain level. We demonstrate that at the domain level hyperstatistics and superstatistics are equivalent, while at the macroscopic level hyperstatistics recovers superstatistics for $q \to 1$ because the Boltzmann-Gibbs character of the domains is restored, as well as Boltzmann-Gibbs statistics for $n \to \infty$.

First: 2026-03-24T20:48:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T14:50:50+00:00
Comments: Mortality shocks, Long-lasting pandemic effects, Stochastic mortality modeling, Cause-specific mortality, Natural hedging
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic, empirical data have revealed that large-scale health crises not only cause immediate disruptions in mortality dynamics but also have persistent effects that may last for several years. Existing mortality models largely assume that mortality shocks are transitory and overlook how their effects can be long-lasting and heterogeneous across age groups and causes of death. In response to this limitation, we propose a novel stochastic mortality model that captures age- and cause-specific long-lasting effects of mortality jumps through a gamma-density-like decay function, estimated via a customized conditional maximum likelihood algorithm. Applying the model to recent U.S. mortality data, we reveal divergent persistence patterns across demographic groups and provide key insights into the tail risk profiles of life insurance and annuity products. Our scenario-based analyses further show that neglecting persistent shock effects can lead to suboptimal hedging, while the proposed model enables what-if testing to analyze such effects under potential future health crises.

Abstract
Building on the phase reduction theory formulated for reaction-diffusion systems with spatial translational symmetry, we develop a data-driven method that reconstructs the spatiotemporal phase dynamics of traveling and oscillating patterns. Spatiotemporal phase dynamics are described by spatial and temporal phases that represent the position and oscillation of the pattern, respectively. Using Bayesian inference, our method directly reconstructs phase equations from time-series data. When tested on simulation data from coupled Gray-Scott models exhibiting traveling breathers, the method accurately reconstructs the deterministic part of the phase equations in the weak-noise regime, in which the phase dynamics converge to a linearly stable fixed point.

Abstract
Principal stratification (PS) is a commonly used approach for understanding the mechanisms through which a treatment affects an outcome. The goal of this work is to extend the PS framework to studies with continuous treatments, which introduces a number of both challenges and opportunities in terms of defining causal effects and performing inference. This manuscript provides multiple key methodological contributions: 1) we introduce principal causal estimands for continuous treatments that provide insights into different causal mechanisms, 2) we show that nonparametric identification is possible under a principal ignorability assumption, but only under a restrictive assumption on the joint distribution of potential mediators, which can be dropped under certain parametric assumptions, 3) we utilize nonparametric Bayesian models for the joint distribution of the potential mediating variables to mitigate issues from model misspecification, and 4) we provide theoretical justification for utilizing an outcome model to identify the joint distribution of the potential mediating variables, and show that this is only possible if a principal ignorability assumption is violated. Lastly, we illustrate our methodology by applying it to a novel study of the relationship between the economy and arrest rates, and how this is potentially mediated by police capacity.

Abstract
Governments and development partners often seek Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) "accelerators" whose progress can propagate gains across human well-being, institutions, resources, and environmental priorities. Such claims require evidence on direction, timing, robustness, and trade-offs, not only network connectivity. We map directed SDG interdependence using a balanced panel of 114 countries observed annually from 2000 to 2024. Because the goal series are persistent, trending, and cross-sectionally dependent, we combine Dumitrescu-Hurlin panel Granger non-causality tests on first-differenced series with false-discovery control across 272 directed goal pairs, and panel local projections with Driscoll-Kraay standard errors for 31 theory-derived indicator linkages. We find 84 directed linkages after correction: 40 synergies and 44 trade-offs. This dense structure does not support a universal accelerator. Driver-receiver rankings are fragile across lag orders, centrality metrics, bootstrap resampling, and alternative dependence controls; only peace and strong institutions as a net receiver and poverty reduction as an effect-size-weighted driver are distinguishable in the bootstrap screen. Two local-projection pathways are supported, both leading to lower child mortality, and the education-child-health association is corroborated in independent World Development Indicators data across 183 countries. The policy implication is not to abandon SDG integration, but to replace fixed global accelerator rankings with adaptive portfolios built around supported, time-lagged linkages and monitored constituent indicators.

Abstract
Technological progress can give rise to new loss exposures or reduce existing risks. In either case, it strongly affects insurance companies' existing lines of business. As the magnitude of such effects is often closely connected to the innovation's market penetration, reliably predicting its adoption process is vital in enabling insurers to adapt their business strategy accordingly. To tackle this challenge, we propose a novel combination of contributions from the domain of innovation diffusion. Our methodology generates adoption scenarios that holistically account for different sources of uncertainty, offers a structured way to incorporate expert judgment and insights from pre-existing technologies, and updates its predictions as new information on the technology's diffusion becomes available. Abstract
Discrete directional data exhibiting p-fold rotational symmetry, where directions separated by integer multiples of 2π/p are equivalent, arise in various applications but are often modeled using continuous distributions. In this article, we develop two general methods for constructing distributions for such data: symmetrization and folding of discrete circular distributions, and wrapping of discrete linear distributions. Particular models arising from these constructions are studied, together with their properties and estimation methods. For axial sand-grain orientation data, the proposed models are compared with discretized continuous axial distributions, whose category probabilities require numerical integration. The framework is also applied to binned Manhattan street-orientation data, demonstrating its applicability to higher-order rotational structures, specifically the 4-fold case.

What If They Took the Shot? A Hierarchical Bayesian Framework for Counterfactual Expected Goals
Authors: Mikayil Mahmudlu, Oktay Karakuş, Hasan Arkadaş, Can Eyüpoğlu
First: 2025-11-28T11:01:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T11:56:39+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
How would the probability of scoring change if a different player took the same shot? Although expected goals models describe the difficulty of scoring opportunities, comparing players on identical chances requires accounting for uncertainty in their estimated conversion probabilities. We address this question through a hierarchical Bayesian framework that evaluates alternative shooters under the same observed circumstances. Using 35,700 eligible shots from four leagues during the 2015/16 season, we compare a contextual benchmark with a primary player-intercept model and a provisional extension that allows player differences to vary with shooting conditions. Paired posterior draws preserve shared uncertainty when individual probability differences are aggregated across opportunities. Chronological validation demonstrates improved predictive performance relative to a constant-rate baseline, but introducing player effects provides negligible additional gains. Controlled single-seed experiments show that greater information per player improves estimation accuracy and narrows uncertainty intervals, although distinguishing small differences remains difficult and confident conclusions about near-null effects can become more frequent. Applications involving Giroud--Vardy and Berardi--Sansone similarly yield estimated differences that are small relative to their posterior uncertainty. These findings highlight an important distinction between predicting the outcome of a shot and establishing whether one player would be more likely to convert it. The proposed framework makes this distinction explicit by evaluating alternative shooters on fixed recorded opportunities while accounting for their contextual exposure. The resulting comparisons concern conditional conversion rather than the causal consequences of replacing a player in a match.

Abstract
The 10 May 2024 geomagnetic storm, referred to as the Gannon Storm in this paper, was one of the most extreme to have occurred in over 20 years. In the era of smartphones and social media, millions of people from all around the world were alerted to the possibility of exceptional auroral displays. Hence, many people not only witnessed but also photographed the aurora during this event. These citizen science observations, although not from scientific instruments operated by observatories or research groups, can prove invaluable in obtaining data to characterise this extraordinary event. In particular, many observers saw and photographed the aurora at mid-latitudes, where ground-based instruments targeting auroral studies are sparse or absent. We created an online survey and circulated it within networks of aurora photographers to collect observations of the aurora and disruptions in technological systems that were experienced during this superstorm. We obtained 696 citizen science reports from over 30 countries, containing information such as the time and location of aurora sightings, observed colours and auroral forms during the geomagnetic storm. We supplemented the obtained dataset with 186 auroral observations logged in the Skywarden catalogue by citizen scientists. The main findings enabled by the data collected through these reports are that the aurora was widely seen from locations at geomagnetic latitudes ranging between 30° and 60°, with a few reports from even lower latitudes. This was significantly further equatorward than predicted by auroral oval models. The reported auroral emission colours, predominantly red and pink and intense enough to reach naked-eye visibility, suggest that the auroral electron precipitation contained large fluxes of low-energy (< 1 keV) particles. Abstract
This paper introduces a new cumulative residual Rényi entropy (CRRE), which addresses limitations of the existing cumulative residual Rényi entropy. The conventional measure suffers from two primary drawbacks: its failure to converge to the cumulative residual entropy (CRE) as the order parameter approaches unity, and its lack of guaranteed positivity. Our proposed CRRE resolves these issues, offering a theoretically consistent and always nonnegative measure of uncertainty in residual lifetimes. We explore its mathematical properties, including its relationship with existing information measures. Furthermore, the concept is extended to its dynamic version, and its time-dependent characteristics are investigated. A non-parametric estimator of the proposed CRRE is developed, its almost sure convergence is established, and is validated through Monte Carlo simulation. The effectiveness of CRRE as a tool for quantifying uncertainty is confirmed through its application in chaotic theory and financial data.

Abstract
Inferring climate fields at higher spatio-temporal resolutions from coarse representations is essential for accessing socioeconomic and environmental impacts. The downscaling of modelled climate fields faces a trade-off between data storage constraints, simulation efficiency, and resolution requirements. While many methods for spatial downscaling have been proposed, joint spatio-temporal downscaling has received comparable little attention. When deriving, for example, daily fields from climate datasets that are available only as monthly averages at coarser spatial resolution, it is highly challenging to faithfully reproduce extreme event statistics and spatiotemporal consistency. To address this task, we introduce a generative machine learning approach, using a diffusion model to jointly downscale monthly temperature and precipitation fields into temporally consistent daily fields at higher spatial resolution. We demonstrate that our approach can generate high-dimensional multivariate climate fields at daily temporal and 0.25° spatial resolution from monthly input fields at 1° spatial resolution, exhibiting coherent temporal dynamics and physically plausible spatial structures. In particular, by incorporating a conditional guidance technique, we show that a pre-trained diffusion model can be directly leveraged to efficiently generate daily high-resolution fields conditioned on monthly inputs, without requiring additional fine-tuning or retraining. Our probabilistic approach thus provides an efficient and generalizable framework for spatio-temporal climate downscaling, enabling flexible climate fields generation for climate impact assessments and digital Earth simulator constructions.

Abstract
Neurophysiologists are nowadays able to record from a large number of extracellular electrodes and to extract, from the raw data, the sequences of action potentials or spikes generated by many neurons. Unfortunately these ''many neurons'' still represent only a tiny fraction of the neuronal population that constitutes the network. Using association statistics such as the estimation of the cross-correlation functions, they are trying to infer the structure of the network formed by the recorded neurons. But this inference is compromised by the tremendous under-sampling of the neuronal population. We propose to focus instead on simple spike train statistics, like the empirical spikes frequency, or the interspike interval distribution. Their sampling distributions can be estimated by simulations, and, given a few observed spike train statistics, they provide enough information to infer the structure of the underlying network. We show that, on a ''toy model'', our method gives significantly better results than the sub-network reconstruction method with regards to the inference of the connection probability of the original network.

Abstract
The BRUIT-FM Challenge asks participants to reduce ''noise'' --- defined as any signal that masks the signal of interest --- on an open dataset of real and synthetic broadband seafloor seismology data. Abstract
Artificial warming via engineered aerosols presents a novel pathway to bypass volatile scarcity on modern Mars. Utilizing the Generic Planetary Climate Model, we evaluate the physical feasibility of aerosol-induced terraforming over a full Martian year (669 Sols). Unlike previous approaches that added aerosols to background dust or assumed continuous nanoparticle injection, we simulate the complete substitution of natural dust with a single-injection profile of radiatively active aluminium nanoparticles without continuous dumping. Abstract
This work introduces a Process Additive Neural Network (PANN) framework to disentangle compensating biases in atmospheric models, where errors in different physical processes can offset each other and obscure their origins. Using a single-column model, each PANN branch represents the tendency error associated with a separate physical process, and the summed branch outputs approximate the total error relative to a reference tendency. Once trained successfully, the branch outputs are expected to provide process-level error attributions and, when averaged, estimates of process-level bias. Initial experiments focus on cloud-related processes in OpenIFS SCM case studies, where cloud and convection tendencies are strongly coupled and compensation is difficult to diagnose using standard approaches. The aim is not to replace human interpretation of physical processes, but to extend it to regimes where model complexity, process interaction, and compensating errors make direct diagnosis difficult. By providing a physically structured form of machine-learning interpretability, PANN can help connect complex model behaviour with process-level explanations. This work supports both improved understanding of compensating errors and the development of more physically consistent weather and climate models.

Abstract
Accurately delineating Arctic coastal polynyas from sea ice concentration data remains challenging because of complex coastal geometry and spatially heterogeneous ice conditions. We develop a daily sea ice concentration (SIC) mapping framework based on hierarchical partitioning, connected-component analysis, and differentiated region growing for polynya and open-water regions. Applied to September-May ice seasons from 2002/03 to 2024/25, the framework achieves a mean overall accuracy of 96.74% and a mean kappa coefficient of 0.93 in the 63-scene visual assessment. Sensitivity experiments reveal a consistent pattern: the absolute magnitude of SIC-defined polynya extent is strongly configuration-dependent, whereas major temporal features are comparatively more stable. Relative to a 75% upper SIC threshold, seasonal extent integrals differ by 13.48%, 32.47%, and 65.04% at thresholds of 80%, 85%, and 90%, respectively, while the broad interannual structure remains recognizable. The response is also strongly regional and seasonal, with the largest threshold sensitivity in the Central Arctic Ocean, very weak sensitivity in the Gulf of St. Lawrence, and greater pan-Arctic curve separation from January onward. At an 80% threshold, product-comparison MAPE is 68.19% between the 6.25 and 25 km products and 11.47% between the 12.5 and 25 km products under their respective comparison periods and reference definitions. Regional product disagreement is likewise heterogeneous. Together, these results show that reproducible long-term polynya monitoring requires a fixed and explicitly documented combination of SIC product, threshold, and spatial classification rules, while broad temporal patterns can remain informative even when absolute extent differs substantially.

Abstract
In multiscale chaotic systems, a central question for closure is how much of an unresolved fine-scale target can be inferred from a simultaneous coarse observation. For a specified observation-target pair, we quantify instantaneous recoverability under the invariant snapshot measure as the fraction of target variance explained by conditioning on the retained coarse variables. Evaluating this quantity across scales produces a scale-resolved recoverability map. Applied to the Lorenz-96 system across a range of forcings, the maps reveal a strongly nonuniform landscape: lower-wavenumber targets have little variance explained by the retained lower modes, while a finite band of higher modes exhibits partial recoverability once the retained scales reach the energetically active intermediate range. Recoverability onset is organized around a reference cutoff at which two retained modes can directly couple to the target through the quadratic nonlinearity, although its precise location depends on attractor statistics. Across the tested forcings, this qualitative onset structure persists while recoverability decreases, leaving a larger fraction of target variance unexplained in more strongly driven regimes. This definition identifies the maximal explained-variance fraction attainable by instantaneous deterministic models under squared loss; the estimated maps provide a scale-dependent diagnostic for closure modeling.

Abstract
Response-spectrum matching is an underdetermined waveform inverse problem in which spectral compatibility must be achieved without uncontrolled changes in other engineering-relevant ground-motion characteristics. This study formulates the problem as differentiable waveform inverse design using a fixed SDOF dynamics engine and configurable optimization modules. An envelope-guided parameterization defines the correction waveform, while a hard projection enforces zero correction-induced terminal velocity and displacement. Differentiable SDOF responses then provide gradients for waveform optimization. Spectral fidelity and Arias-intensity accumulation are treated as adaptive objectives. Spectral feasibility, PGA, total Arias intensity, and displacement-path requirements are incorporated as constraints. On a common benchmark, maximum errors of 2.61% and 8.47% are obtained for single- and multi-damping matching, respectively, while retaining the additional waveform constraints.
