Adequacy radii for stepped-wedge trials with time-varying effects
Authors: Stanisław M. S. Halkiewicz, Sergey Alexeev
First: 2026-10-26T17:57:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T17:57:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Complete stepped-wedge designs guarantee eventual intervention delivery, but their final periods contain no concurrent controls. Under exposure-time heterogeneity this creates a design-analysis tension: a constant-effect analysis can be precise while targeting a design-dependent contrast, whereas an exposure-time indicator (ETI) analysis targets a prespecified time-averaged effect but may be much less precise. We develop an adequacy radius that quantifies the size of exposure-time heterogeneity compatible with a prespecified tolerance for estimand mismatch, and combine it with precision calculations to compare complete wedges with rollout-constrained hybrid designs that retain a wait-list arm. For a balanced wedge with a wait-list under the Hussey-Hughes model, the wait-list share and the correlation act on the constant-effect GLS estimand through a single scalar; we prove that the radius increases strictly with the wait-list share and decreases strictly with the within-cluster correlation, but remains bounded. Numerical optimisation and a model-based redesign of a weekend allied-health trial show that intermediate hybrids make estimand-aligned ETI analysis materially more affordable, giving a robustness-precision frontier rather than a binary choice between a complete wedge and a parallel trial. A second trial, of digital adherence support for tuberculosis treatment, has the same radius but no detectable heterogeneity; its test of a constant effect would detect heterogeneity large enough to halve the coverage of the nominal interval only 37% of the time. The contribution is a diagnostic and design principle: when exposure-time heterogeneity is plausible, retaining concurrent controls reduces the price of analysing the estimand one intends to report.

Abstract
Self-supervised training is monitored without labels, through the loss and statistics of the learned features. We report a case where we know the encoders lost part of what they had learned, so we can check whether these signals noticed. We trained LeJEPA encoders on synthetic images with known generating factors. In nine of ten seeds, the readability of those factors fell abruptly and stayed down, nearly always along the same direction in factor space. Meanwhile the loss kept falling, and most of the eleven label-free signals we tracked kept improving and rated the declining runs better than runs that did not decline. Standard change-point detection rarely flagged the decline near its onset, and selecting checkpoints by these signals mostly chose ones after it.

Quantifying and Predicting Pitch Sequence Effectiveness Using Visual Deception Metrics

Abstract
Pitcher performance is influenced not only by individual pitch characteristics, but also by the relationship and sequencing between consecutive pitches. In this study, we construct a sequence-level framework using MLB Statcast data from 2021-2025 to quantify the value of pitch deception with two geometric features: Decision Angular Separation (DAS) and Average Angular Separation (AAS). These metrics measure visual similarity of back-to-back pitches from the batter's perspective at a hitter's swing "decision point" during the flight of the ball from the pitcher's hand to the plate. We introduce Swinging Strike Lift (SwStr Lift), a residualized outcome isolating sequencing value beyond player identity, pitch type, location, and count. Across more than 740,936 two-pitch sequences, we find that tighter tunnels (low DAS/AAS) produce significant behavioral changes, for well-tunneled FB-to-BR (fastball-to-breaking-ball) sequences in the Shadow/Chase regions. This elevated swing propensity is consistent with swing induction as a primary behavioral pathway underlying the observed gains in swinging strikes. Our predictive modeling confirms the importance of tunneling, as adding DAS/AAS to gradient-boosted models yields substantial relative improvements in out-of-sample R-squared (e.g., +38% for FB-to-BR and +163% for FB-to-OS [fastball-to-off-speed]). These results demonstrate that tunneling generates a measurable and behaviorally coherent sequencing advantage, offering a tractable, perceptually grounded tool for evaluating pitcher deception which informs pitch design and game strategy. Quintile analyses confirm the effect is not confined to extreme tunnels; ICE plots show model-predicted lift declining sharply as DAS increases. Abstract
Machine learning Earth-system models are becoming increasingly important, and so is their development. Graph-based models, one of the most prominent approaches, allow developers to constrain the learning process before training via graph connectivity. Yet, developing these models remains challenging: the graph is built algorithmically with little intuition about the final result beyond static plotting, which requires extra work and is often not detailed enough. Even after successful training, how the model arrives at forecasts remains obscure. We present GNN-VIEW, an interactive browser-based tool that supports the development of such systems. GNN-VIEW exposes the graph's information pathways, allowing model developers to debug the graph structure before training. After training, GNN-VIEW uses attribution-based explainability to reveal which input features are used by the trained model for a given forecast. This helps developers understand why a model fails. We showcase the usefulness of GNN-VIEW in the construction stage of a graph for an ocean forecasting model, and in explaining poorly predicted precipitation of a trained weather forecasting model during Hurricane Laura.

Hyperspectral Unmixing using a Structured Linear Mixing Model with Additive Spectral Variability
Authors: Salah Eddine Brezini, Yannick Deville, Moussa Sofiane Karoui, Fatima Zohra Benhalouche, Abdelaziz Ouamri
First: 2026-10-26T15:12:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:12:31+00:00

Abstract
Linear hyperspectral unmixing aims at retrieving pure material spectra present in an observed scene and their associated abundance fractions in each pixel. In most practical situations, hyperspectral images are affected by spectral variability (SV) also known as intra-class variability, which is caused by different physical phenomena including atmospheric effects, intrinsic variability of the pure materials and illumination or topographic changes. Nevertheless, the standard hyperspectral unmixing methods that rely on the Linear Mixing Model (LMM) do not consider these phenomena. Thus, neglecting them will spread errors throughout the linear hyperspectral unmixing process. To avoid this problem, this paper proposes two hyperspectral unmixing algorithms dedicated to a parametric model called Structured Additive Variability LMM (SAV-LMM). This mixing model handles SV in an additive manner by incorporating two terms into the standard LMM. Moreover, the designed algorithms are based on Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF) and optimize a cost function which integrates additional penalization accounting for SV. Furthermore, a novel improved initialization scheme, which takes SV into account, is proposed. Experiments conducted on both synthetic and real datasets show the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms for linearly unmixing hyperspectral images with SV compared to the tested state-of-the-art methods.

Hierarchical Bayesian Joint Modeling of Athlete Performance Trajectories and Latent Peak Ages
Authors: K. Ken Peng, Tianyu Guan, Xiong Zhao
First: 2026-10-26T14:06:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T14:06:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Determining when athletes reach peak performance is a central question in sports science, with implications for talent identification, training design, and cross-discipline comparisons. Statistical inference is challenging because peak age is not directly observed: competition records are noisy and selective measurements of underlying performance capacity and are often limited to incomplete observation windows. We develop a hierarchical Bayesian joint modeling framework that links a longitudinal model for athlete-specific performance trajectories to a regression submodel for latent peak age. The longitudinal component models observed competition performances as noisy measurements of athlete-specific performance trajectories over age. The peak-age component then treats the maximizer of each trajectory as a latent response in a parametric accelerated failure time (AFT) model, allowing athlete-level covariates to explain earlier or later peak age. The hierarchical structure borrows information across athletes, while joint posterior inference propagates uncertainty from trajectory reconstruction to both individual peak-age estimates and population-level covariate effects. Simulation studies show that, as observation windows become less informative, the joint model preserves substantially smaller regression bias and reflects the information loss through wider uncertainty, whereas pseudo-peak and two-stage alternatives exhibit larger bias. An application to World Athletics ranking data illustrates how the joint model estimates discipline-specific peak-age patterns and covariate effects under incomplete and irregular observation windows. Beyond sports, the proposed framework applies broadly to problems that couple longitudinal trajectories with function-derived latent features.

Abstract
Dealing with climate change requires that all countries engage in costly efforts to reduce their emissions. Reaching this objective has so far been elusive because it is in the best interest of each country to let the others do the effort and benefit itself from a better environment. The presence of negative externalities and strategic behavior have made game theory a natural paradigm to design an international environmental agreement (IEA) that codifies what countries should do. Considering that countries are sovereign and no supranational entity can impose an agreement, a stream of literature adopted a noncooperative mode of play to the formation of an environmental coalition. On the other hand, as joint optimization of all countries' payoff leads to the best outcome, cooperative games approach has also been proposed to share the cost of climate change. Abstract
Verifying where commodities like timber were harvested is a forensic task, needed to detect illegal trade due to illegal deforestation or trade sanctions. Existing Gaussian-process models of wood chemistry give a Bayesian posterior over candidate harvest locations, but their credible regions in general do not deliver the coverage they promise (0.82-0.91 at the nominal 95\%). In this work we bring in a second, independent source of evidence: a prior over harvest locations learned from satellite (AlphaEarth) embeddings. We fit one classifier per genus that predicts presence from an embedding vector, supervised by occurrence records, and normalize its scores into a prior over the candidate grid. For such a prior to act on the prediction sets rather than merely mask cells, we build a cross-conformal predictor on the \emph{posterior cell mass}, so the prior moves every p-value while the coverage guarantee survives any misspecification of it. On four European genera, the embedding prior shrinks regions by up to 42\% compared to a uniform prior at no cost in coverage, which holds at 0.953-0.968 against a nominal 0.95.

Bistability of ice-shelf cavity circulation and melt under coastal polynya forcing
Authors: Louis Saddier, Corentin Herbert, Christopher Y. S. Bull, Louis-Alexandre Couston
First: 2026-10-26T12:48:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T12:48:51+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Antarctic ice shelves play a key role in controlling the mass loss of the Antarctic Ice Sheet. Increasing evidence from regional studies suggests the possibility of abrupt transitions from low to high basal melt regimes under future climate forcing. The computational cost and complexity of these realistic simulations yet limit their ability to systematically investigate the steady-state response of ice shelves to incremental forcing, the reversibility of abrupt transitions and the underlying positive feedback loops. Here, we report a detailed analysis of abrupt regime changes in an ice-shelf cavity with idealized geometry, subject to prescribed temperature and salinity stratifications in the far field and near-ice-front surface salt fluxes representing the impact of sea-ice production in coastal polynyas. We show that a cold regime emerges when the buoyancy loss in the polynya is sufficient to trigger deep convection, while a warm regime develops otherwise. The steady response of the system exhibits hysteresis, characteristic of irreversible transitions, and scaling laws are proposed for both melt rates and transition thresholds. We demonstrate that bistability emerges when a gyre recirculates the meltwater within the ice-shelf front area. The bistable forcing range increases with far-field ocean temperature but decreases with background stratification. We also show that increasing horizontal viscosity limits bistability, which may explain the range of behaviors reported in previous studies.

Shape-Preserving Treatment Effects for Functional Outcomes
Authors: Agathe Chabassier, Joris Olivier, Julie Josse, Erwan Scornet

Abstract
By recording health measurements daily rather than at a few clinical visits, connected devices pave the way for more precise real-world evidence that measures how the effect of an intervention unfolds over time: does the response rebound, peak late, or oscillate? Yet causal methods for such functional outcomes remain scarce, and those that exist target the Functional Average Treatment Effect (FATE). Its pointwise averaging flattens the features patients share whenever their curves are misaligned in time, as is common with biosignals, where patients respond with their own timing. We develop a causal inference framework that captures the shape of the effect of a binary intervention, based on the phase-centered elastic mean from elastic shape analysis. We lift this mean from a sample statistic to a functional of the data-generating law and prove that it is well defined under explicit geometric assumptions. Because it is not linear, it yields three causal estimands instead of one: populational, individual and local. We identify the populational estimand by inverse-probability weighting and the local estimand through the conditional outcome laws, estimate them by weighting and by matching, and show that the individual one is not identifiable. On simulations with timing variability or heterogeneous effect shapes, our estimators recover the shape that the FATE loses. On real weight trajectories following GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation, they reveal a rebound that the FATE averages away. This framework thus gives clinicians a treatment-effect curve whose shape and timing reflect genuine patient trajectories rather than a misalignment artifact.

Transient Supercriticality in Hawkes Processes: Detection, Identifiability, and Apparent Criticality

Abstract
Self-exciting (Hawkes) models of epidemics, earthquakes, financial markets, and social cascades are usually calibrated with a branching ratio $n$ below one. This is a condition for stationarity, not for existence: a process with $n>1$ grows exponentially but remains finite on any finite horizon. Imposing stationarity is also misleading, because transient supercritical episodes then masquerade as near-criticality. Reconstructing the latent genealogy of events, we show that a single finite record can reveal an excursion into the regime $n(t)>1$, and back, and date its onset and end. Detectability is governed by the excess $n-1$ times the square root of the number of exposed parents. Even when the memory time of the triggering is unknown, the data determine the growth rate of activity during the episode nearly as precisely as when it is known, and hence whether $n$ crossed unity, whereas the value of $n$ requires independent knowledge of the memory time. Letting the data choose between endogenous amplification and external forcing prevents confusing the two. In the 2015 Korean MERS outbreak, our reconstruction shows that transmission by hospital cohorts had collapsed by 28 May, before the incidence peak and two weeks before the instantaneous reproduction number fell below one; in real time, it establishes the collapse a few days earlier than this standard indicator. Our framework separates stationarity, non-explosion, and transient supercritical amplification, and provides a practical route for detecting short-lived endogenous instabilities in self-exciting systems.

Abstract

Abstract
We propose the dynamic conditional density network (DCDN) to correct the bivariate normal predictive distribution commonly used to postprocess ensemble forecasts of wind vectors. The conditional distribution of the observed wind vector given the ensemble forecast may depart from the bivariate normal distribution. DCDN multiplies a bivariate normal reference density by a correction density. The correction density is a B-spline mixture on the probability integral transform scale of the reference density, and a neural network predicts its weights. DCDN equals its reference density when the correction density is uniform. We apply DCDN to ensemble forecasts of the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts and observations at 154 sites in the Tohoku region of Japan. DCDN significantly improved the logarithmic score, energy score, and variogram score over the bivariate normal distribution predicted by a neural network. The improvement in the logarithmic score was large near zero wind speed and at higher wind speeds. This is consistent with non-normal features of the conditional distribution that the bivariate normal distribution misses.

Abstract
This article revisits entropy and related magnitudes (entropy density, effective measure complexity, and informational distance) for the analysis of sequential data. We present their mathematical foundations within information theory and ergodic theory, establishing connections between probabilistic Shannon entropy and algorithmic Kolmogorov complexity. A central result, Zivs theorem, demonstrates the asymptotic equivalence between the normalised number of distinct words and entropy density, providing the theoretical basis for estimation via Lempel-Ziv factorisation. Abstract
