Simulations of the Evolving Ozone Layer: Implications for the Early Earth, Exoplanets, and the Search For Life
Authors: Akhil Kumar, Gregory J. Cooke
First: 2026-10-13T17:59:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:59:34+00:00
Comments: 66 pages, 18 figures, resubmitted after first revision to Royal Society Open Science
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Over 4 billion years, Earth's atmosphere has been transformed from a composition devoid of molecular oxygen (O$_2$) to one where O$_2$ is now 21% of our modern atmosphere by volume. With high quantities of O$_2$, an ozone (O$_3$) layer can form and partially shield the surface from biologically harmful ultraviolet radiation (UV). The evolution of the O$_3$ layer has previously been simulated under the modern Sun by independent groups with separate models. Here, we present simulations of Earth's atmosphere from four open-source one-dimensional (1D) photochemical models and a 3D chemistry-climate model, focussing on O$_3$. We find that the O$_3$ layer thickness exhibits a clear deviation between the 1D models and the 3D model due to several factors, including treatments of O$_2$ photolysis, chemical networks, H$_2$O microphysics and 3D transport. We find that 1D models introduce a bias for unobserved climate states and show how these biases may propagate to calculations of biological UV damage and surface habitability. We show that when applied to specific exoplanet atmospheres, these discrepancies can become more pronounced, propagating through to observational predictions for the next generation of telescopes, such as HWO and LIFE, that will search for habitable and inhabited exoplanets.

Discrete-Time Survival Modelling with Dynamic Industrial Graphs for Mortgage Credit Risk
Authors: Shiqi Fang
First: 2026-10-13T17:40:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:40:40+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Mortgage credit risk may exhibit dependence across economically connected areas, even when they are geographically distant. However, current models typically represent cross-area dependence through fixed geographical neighbourhoods, potentially overlooking economic connections that extend beyond physical proximity and evolve over time. These models may fail to capture shared risks among geographically distant but economically similar areas, and cannot describe a changing dependence of risk; it therefore risks retaining economically obsolete connections while omitting newly emerging ones. This paper uses similarities of industrial composition as a measure of economic proximity and develops a discrete-time survival model with dynamic industrial graphs to capture evolving cross-area dependence in mortgage default risk. The resulting graphs are reconstructed annually, allowing both their topology and edge weights to change as regional industrial compositions evolve. Jointly combining a proper conditional autoregressive process with a first-order autoregressive process governs the temporal cross-area dependence and the temporal persistence in latent risk simultaneously. The model is estimated using Integrated Nested Laplace Approximation and evaluated on U.S. mortgage loan-level data against the static industrial graph specifications. The results show that the proposed dynamic model achieves better in-sample fit and out-of-sample discrimination, while its estimated parameters indicate substantial graph-induced cross-area dependence and temporal persistence. These findings demonstrate the value of allowing economically defined neighbourhoods to evolve when modelling mortgage default risk.

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a simple yet effective tabular data watermarking mechanism with statistical guarantees. We show theoretically that the proposed watermark can be effectively detected, while faithfully preserving the data fidelity, and also demonstrates appealing robustness against additive noise attack. The general idea is to achieve the watermarking through a strategic embedding based on simple data binning. Specifically, it divides the feature's value range into finely segmented intervals and embeds watermarks into selected ``green list" intervals. To detect the watermarks, we develop a principled statistical hypothesis-testing framework with minimal assumptions: it remains valid as long as the underlying data distribution has a continuous density function. The watermarking efficacy is demonstrated through rigorous theoretical analysis and empirical validation, highlighting its utility in enhancing the security of synthetic and real-world datasets.

From Fourier Amplitudes to Persistence Images for Impact-Echo Defect Detection
Authors: Keunsu Kim, Suyoung Choi, Meiyan Kang
First: 2026-10-13T17:07:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:07:04+00:00
Comments: 32 pages, 5 figures, including supplementary material

Abstract
Impact-echo testing is a nondestructive testing method used to detect internal defects in concrete structures. However, boundary reflections, structured interference, and measurement noise can produce complex Fourier amplitude spectra, making defect classification difficult. We constructed a two-dimensional frequency--amplitude persistence image (FA-PI) from the Fourier amplitude spectrum. Using a fixed parameterization of exact multi-parameter persistent homology, each retained Fourier component is represented with frequency-bin location as one coordinate and amplitude as the other, and Gaussian kernels are then applied in this two-dimensional space. This construction has a direct analytical connection to the corresponding one-dimensional Gaussian-smoothed Fourier amplitude spectrum. FA-PI was evaluated on an experimental backfill-void dataset and a public bridge-deck impact-echo dataset, and compared with raw and Gaussian-smoothed Fourier amplitude spectra using a linear classifier with a Softmax output and identical signal-level data splits. The evaluation included original signals and signals corrupted by Gaussian noise, sinusoidal interference, or mixed noise, with matching noise types and signal-to-noise ratios during training, validation, and testing. Performance was assessed using accuracy and macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1-score. Across the two datasets, FA-PI consistently outperformed the raw and Gaussian-smoothed Fourier baselines in the noisy-condition summaries. These findings indicate that representing Fourier spectral components in a two-dimensional frequency--amplitude space can improve impact-echo defect classification beyond frequency-axis smoothing alone.

Abstract
Reliable forward uncertainty quantification in engineering requires methods that account for aleatory and epistemic uncertainties. In many applications, epistemic effects arising from uncertain parameters and model form dominate prediction error and strongly influence engineering decisions. Because distinguishing and representing each source separately is often infeasible, their combined effect is typically analyzed using a unified model-error framework. Model error directly affects model credibility and predictive reliability; yet its characterization remains challenging. To address this need, we introduce a bootstrap-based stochastic subspace model for characterizing model error in the stochastic reduced-order modeling framework. Given a snapshot matrix of state vectors, the method leverages the empirical data distribution to induce a sampling distribution over principal subspaces for reduced order modeling. The resulting stochastic model enables improved characterization of model error in computational mechanics compared with existing approaches. The method offers several advantages: (1) it is assumption-free and leverages the empirical data distribution; (2) it enforces linear constraints (such as boundary conditions) by construction; (3) it requires only one hyperparameter, significantly simplifying the training process; and (4) its algorithm is straightforward to implement. We evaluate the method's performance against existing approaches using numerical examples in computational mechanics and structural dynamics.

Abstract
Accurate forecasting of optical turbulence (OT) is useful to optimise telescope scheduling and maximising observing efficiency, as the performance of adaptive optics systems strongly depends on turbulence conditions. While various numerical weather prediction and machine learning models are developed to predict these complex turbulence properties, properly evaluating their added value requires well-defined baseline reference models. This study establishes baseline performance thresholds for key astroclimatic parameters using two distinct models: one forecasting the parameter average over a specific timescale, and the other forecasting the temporal evolution over the same specific timescale. We apply these frameworks to extensive, multi-year datasets from the sites of the Very Large Telescope (VLT) and the Large Binocular Telescope (LBT) and discuss the possibility of extrapolating reference values of forecast performances for other sites based on their optical turbulence statistical characteristics. Our results show that baseline predictive accuracy varies according to the observing site, the specific parameter, the chosen timescale, and the forecasting approach. Therefore, an OT forecasting model's accuracy cannot be quantified in absolute terms, but relatively to the baseline.

From risk to flourishing: Seeds for responding to a complex world
Authors: Ryan M. McGranaghan, Elisabeth H. Krueger, Alexandre Dunant, Guillaume Falmagne, Lynee L. Turek-Hankins, Sara M. Constantino, Raissa D'Souza, Elke U. Weber, Graydon W. Manzke

Abstract
Complex risk arises when interacting hazards, infrastructures, ecological systems, institutions, and human behaviors produce nonlinear impacts that cannot be inferred from any component in isolation. We call the study of these dynamics complex risk science. Flourishing is the actualization of all beings toward their good--individually and collectively--answering the question of `how do we live well with and for each other?'. In science as in society, the tendency to reduce, segregate, specialize, and settle has precluded embracing the concept of flourishing as a guiding principle. We describe how complex risk science reframes risk science research so that it can promote the flourishing of socio-ecological-technological systems, and explore how its practice may point society toward a more flourishing future. Our exploration mirrors the unsettled and searching nature of flourishing: examine ideologies traditionally believed to be rigidly in opposition and consider what emerges in the space(s) and conversation between them. We call this a dialectical approach: not a means of resolving contradiction, but a practice of sustaining it, so that systems, and our understanding of them, remain capable of reframing as conditions change. Within this dialectical paradigm, we revisit longstanding tensions in risk science: physical|social, determinism|uncertainty, and contextuality|generality. Examining the conversation among these tensions reveals five seeds that are dispositions for practicing complex risk science: interdependence, responsivity, participation, pluralism, and ongoingness. They can support any scientific endeavor to embrace complexity and promote flourishing. Practicing these seeds at scale will depend on institutional reform: creating collectives toward complex risk science, supporting them with skillfully governed commons, and centering care in our research and evaluation.

Abstract
We analyze regional earthquake energy statistics from the Southern California and Japan seismic catalogs and find scale-invariant energy distributions characterized by an exponent $τ\simeq 1.67$. To quantify how closely scale-invariant dynamics with different exponent values resemble real earthquakes, we generate synthetic energy distributions over a wide range of $τ$ under conditions of constant activity. Abstract
Data arrive sequentially, each associated with a null hypothesis. We develop testing procedures to locate intervals in which some null hypotheses fail, with false discovery rate (FDR) control. The new procedures are called sequential resetting procedures, and they are based on e-values and test supermartingales. We also develop a refined version of the procedures by dropping less informative data points before the block minimum of the test supermartingale in each rejection block. These procedures have explicit FDR bounds under two settings: a classic setting of independence and the more general setting of possible dependence across null data and non-null data. These FDR bounds are independent of the testing horizon, and the general one has anytime validity, but it has an extra logarithm factor compared with the standard FDR level. We present simulation studies and data experiments with applications of sequential resetting procedures to LLM watermark detection and financial backtesting.

Abstract
The within-cluster subgroup composition of units can inform on the marginal distribution of those units outcomes, denoted informative subgroup size (ISS). Moreover, within-cluster subgroup composition of paired outcomes can allow one outcome to inform upon the marginal distribution of the other. If this type of ISS ignored, it can bias marginal association estimates by allowing subgroup composition to influence how clusters contribute to the analysis. We develop two weighting approaches for estimating marginal association between paired outcomes in this setting. The weights are derived using within-cluster resampling arguments and extend conventional cluster weighting to paired response subgroups. We adapt an existing test of marginal dependence to assess ISS by examining this subgroup structure in both directions. In simulated data exhibiting this bidirectional ISS, pair-based weighting reduced bias when association was primarily carried through the paired observations. These weights also allow scientifically or clinically meaningful dichotomizations to enter directly into the weighting scheme, with the choice of subgroup definition further improving estimation in several simulated settings. Application to NHANES oral-health data showed strong evidence of ISS for filled-surface outcomes across subgroups defined by clinical attachment loss and pocket depth, with the corresponding periodontal--caries associations generally small and positive. These methods provide a practical approach for estimating marginal association when paired subgroup composition is informative.

Risk Measures for the Climate Transition Age: Why Symmetric Risk Budgets are unlikely to mitigate two-dimensionally asymmetric Climate Change risks
Authors: Lars Dijkstra, Andreas G. F. Hoepner, Fabiola I. Schneider, Agnieszka Słomka-Gołębiowska
First: 2026-10-13T13:36:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T13:36:55+00:00
Comments: 13 pages, 1 figure, 9 equations| Keywords: climate change mitigation, Climate Transition Benchmarks, deviation, fiduciary duty, Paris-Aligned Benchmarks, risk, standard risk,

Abstract
The most widely used investment risk measures - variance, standard deviation, and tracking error - treat upside deviations as equally undesirable as downside losses. In stable markets with assumed symmetric return distributions, this simplification is potentially harmless. In the climate transition age, it is not. Climate risk is two-dimensionally asymmetric, across assets and over time. Both dimensions are negatively skewed against investors. Hence, climate transition investment strategies deliberately deviate from benchmarks that reflect an insufficiently decarbonising global economy. When fiduciaries enforce tracking error budgets that penalise outperformance just as heavily as underperformance, they inadvertently constrain exactly the portfolio return opportunities that climate-aware investing seeks. We argue that replacing symmetric deviation-based risk measures with their downside-only counterparts - semi-variance, standard risk and trailing error - resolves this conflict without sacrificing any fiduciary discipline. The distinction is not merely semantic: as the gap between climate transition trajectories and market benchmarks widens over the coming decades, symmetric risk budgets will become binding constraints on transition strategies well before actual investment risk warrants it. We propose specific changes to mandate design that would allow institutional investors to pursue climate transition opportunities within genuinely risk-aware (rather than deviation-averse) frameworks.

The most widely used investment risk measures - variance, standard deviation, and tracking error - treat upside deviations as equally undesirable as downside losses.

Abstract
Understanding how different aspects of basketball performance translate into overall team efficiency is central to analysis and strategy. This paper derives a mathematical expression that presents team offensive, defensive, and net ratings directly in terms of a team's four factors and shooting percentages. The resulting formula captures the full nonlinear structure of how shooting, turnovers, rebounding, and free throws jointly interact to determine team efficiency. Using this relationship, we introduce a Shapley value-based framework that decomposes team net rating into a sum of contributions from individual factors. This way, different facets of the game are made commensurable. The decomposition requires only standard box-score statistics and a single parameter $μ$, which represents the relative frequency of possession-ending free throws and remains constant for a given league and season. Consequently, it can be applied equally to individual games, full seasons, or league-wide trends. Moreover, it is immediately applicable to box-score statistics from any league or competition level by a simple change of reference point and the estimation of league- and season-specific parameter $μ$. Empirical analyses using NBA data from 2000 to 2025 validate the offensive ratings produced by the formula against exact play-by-play data, showing a nearly perfect correlation ($r = 0.999$) and a low Mean Absolute Error of $0.188$ points per 100 possessions. These analyses further illustrate how the proposed framework can be used to quantify how teams achieve success in different ways or to explain the results of individual games.

Preferential concentration of cloud droplets: Coupled effects of phase change and gravitational settling
Authors: Zi-Mo Liao
First: 2026-10-13T12:22:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T12:22:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cloud droplets respond to turbulent motion with finite inertia and settle under gravity, while condensation and evaporation change their sizes. The nonuniform accumulation of cloud droplets in turbulent flows, known as preferential concentration, has attracted sustained interest because it affects droplet collision and coalescence rates. We develop an analytical theory that quantifies the coupled effects of droplet inertia, gravitational settling, and phase change on concentration changes. Applying this theory to simulations of two-dimensional homogeneous thermal convection reveals that phase change markedly enhances preferential concentration in this flow, primarily through spatial differences in settling speed. These differences arise because droplets experience different thermodynamic histories along their trajectories and thus acquire different sizes. Droplet transport further increases this settling contribution by rearranging droplets of different sizes. Fluid deformation partly offsets this enhancement by thinning concentration structures, reducing the preferential concentration measured at a fixed spatial scale.

Recognition of Nanoparticle and Microdroplet Parameters in Levitating Microdroplets of Suspension by Speckle Image Analysis Using Convolutional Neural Networks

Abstract
Retrieving suspension parameters from light scattered by a suspension microdroplet is a difficult inverse problem because the recorded optical pattern is shaped by droplet morphology and by perturbations of the internal optical field introduced by the dispersed phase. Here we demonstrate experimental, data-driven recognition of microdroplet and nanoparticle-suspension parameters from laser speckle images recorded for individual levitated suspension microdroplets. We confined slowly evaporating TiO$_2$ nanoparticle suspensions in diethylene glycol in a linear electrodynamic quadrupole trap and classified their speckle patterns using a convolutional neural network (CNN). The CNN was trained and tested to recognize droplet diameter, nanoparticle mass concentration, and nanoparticle diameter, first separately and then in simultaneous two- and three-parameter tasks. The CNN recognized droplet diameter most robustly, with an estimated accuracy of approximately 6% on independent test data. Nanoparticle concentration required sufficiently separated classes. Nanoparticle-diameter classes were also recognized, although particle-size effects were coupled to changes in particle number at fixed mass concentration. We demonstrated simultaneous three-parameter classification across 27 classes. As an exploratory out-of-distribution test, we applied a CNN trained on single-component suspensions to dual-component suspensions containing two nanoparticle diameters. The CNN retained meaningful predictive capability, especially at higher concentrations, although robust decomposition of mixed nanoparticle populations was not achieved. These results show that speckle images of single levitated suspension microdroplets contain sufficient information for CNN-based multi-parameter recognition and support further development of speckle-based optical diagnostics for complex microdroplet and aerosol-like systems.

Abstract
Symmetries are key properties of physical models and of experimental designs, but any proposed symmetry may or may not be realized in nature. In this paper, we introduce a practical and general method to test such suspected symmetries in data, with minimal external input. Self-supervision, which derives learning objectives from data without external labelling, is used to train models to predict 'which is real?' between real data and symmetrically transformed alternatives. Crucially, our method handles filtered data, which often arise from inefficiencies or deliberate selections, and which could give the illusion of asymmetry if mistreated. Abstract
Fronts, eddies, jets, and meanders populate the ocean surface and play a key role in organizing transport and mixing across a wide range of scales. From a Lagrangian perspective, frontal structures are conveniently detected as surface filaments that act as barriers to horizontal transport. Abstract
The main difficulty in 3D gravity inversion is that surface observations lack vertical wavenumber information, making the problem underdetermined and depth resolution poor. Building on the author's spectral-domain pseudo-inverse theory for unitary diagonalizable systems, this paper presents a true 3D inversion method. Surface data are analytically continued upward via Laplace's equation to form a 3D data volume, providing the vertical wavenumber sampling for the 3D Fourier transform. A general analytical expression for the half-space spectrum is derived, separating the horizontal spectrum from the vertical propagation kernel. The Green's function of the 3D Poisson equation is unitarily diagonalized, yielding the forward spectral response $λ(\kk) = -i 4πG k_z / K^2$, and a stable inverse filter $q_α(λ) = \barλ/(|λ|^2 + α)$ is constructed. This filter is proved to have bounded stability and consistency (reducing to the exact inverse as $α\to 0^+$). Validation with a homogeneous sphere model shows correct recovery of the anomaly location and singular behavior at the source. The contributions are twofold: (1) upward continuation constructs a 3D volume from 2D surface data, which does not add physical observations but unfolds the 3D spatial variation information (data innovation) derived through the governing physical equation; (2) the inversion is reduced to one forward 3D Fourier transform, one spectral scaling, and one inverse transform. Abstract
The Hybrid forecast covariance model is an operationally successful way to build a background error covariance matrix. It is constructed as a weighted sum, with scalar weights, of the climatological covariance matrix and a localized ensemble covariance matrix, with a localization matrix typically depending on a localization length. For best performance, the Hybrid needs to be optimized by tuning those weights and, possibly, other parameters, such as the localization length. Both the hypervariational method and that linear fit procedure are shown to produce an optimal weight value matching (up to a tolerance of $0.1$) the optimal value known from the theory.
