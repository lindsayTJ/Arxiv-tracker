Value of Adding AI Weather Model Ensembles to a Multi-model Probabilistic Blend
Authors: Harris Sop Nkuiate, Gavin Evans, Christopher A. T. Ferro, Frank Kwasniok, Caroline Bain, Stefan Siegert
First: 2026-10-30T17:57:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:57:25+00:00
Comments: 29 pages, 11 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many weather forecasting organizations use a multi-model blend of ensemble members from different prediction systems to obtain a more accurate forecast. Recent studies have shown that blending deterministic AI-based models alongside physics-based models improves the overall forecast skill. However, the incorporation of an ensemble AI-based model into a multi-model blend is yet to be investigated. This study explores the value of blending the ensemble version of the Artificial Intelligence Forecasting System (AIFS-CRPS) with the Met Office's convection permitting physics-based ensemble, MOGREPS-UK. We show that blending site temperature (and wind speed) forecasts over the United Kingdom from both ensemble physics-based and ensemble AI-based models leads to an increase in overall forecast skill spatially, across lead times and across exceedance thresholds, especially when the models have different error characteristics. By decomposing the continuous ranked probability score of the blend into component means and diversity terms, we show that a more accurate blend can be achieved by assigning more weight to more skillful models and using models with complementary errors. For example, we show that a blend of MOGREPS-UK with either AIFS-CRPS or the physics-based Integrated Forecasting System (IFS) is better than a blend of IFS and AIFS-CRPS which are not very diverse. This has implications for the importance of weather model diversity and may lend weight to different model development approaches being pursued in tandem. We also demonstrate that calibrating AIFS-CRPS forecasts yields an increase in forecast skill comparable to calibrated ensemble physics-based forecasts, and calibrating the source forecasts before blending and the blended forecast after blending improves the overall forecast accuracy.

SATFRONT: Detecting Atmospheric Fronts with Satellite Data using Machine Learning
Authors: Ioannis Dravilas, Miruna Stoicescu, Roope Tervo, Carlos Horn, Oriol Hinojo Comellas, Sina Montazeri, Andrea Meraner, Ana Lucic
First: 2026-10-30T17:48:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:48:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Atmospheric fronts are associated with much of the high-impact weather in the mid-latitudes, yet their identification and classification remain largely manual and subjective. Existing deep learning approaches rely primarily on gridded reanalysis or forecast data, limiting their applicability to satellite observations available for time-critical applications. We introduce SATFRONT, a machine learning framework for detecting cold, warm, and occluded fronts over Europe from geostationary satellite observations, with reanalysis data as an optional additional input. The model is trained on frontal analyses from five European national meteorological services and evaluated against UK Met Office analyses for 2024. Using satellite observations alone, SATFRONT achieves a Critical Success Index (CSI) of 0.428 at a 150 km tolerance, within the range of agreement between analyses from different meteorological services (0.277-0.501) and higher than that of an operational rule-based front detector that uses satellite data (0.133). Adding ERA5 reanalysis increases the CSI to 0.591. ERA5 substantially improves the placement of individual fronts, although its effect on their annual spatial distribution varies by front type. These results demonstrate that cold, warm, and occluded fronts can be detected directly from geostationary satellite observations, providing a basis for automated frontal analyses at the high temporal frequency required for time-critical applications.

Abstract
Li and Matin (2005a, 2005b) reported three regularities of the induced visually-perceived vertical (VPV), the direction judged upright. For small tilts, a peripheral line shifts it nearly linearly with tilt, two lines combine nearly linearly, and mirror-image tilts cancel. Each line votes at twice its angle, weighted by its strength. The votes' vector sum $c_1$ is the first circular moment of the doubled angles; half its angle, $φ=\tfrac12\arg c_1$, is the induced vertical, a stimulus axis, not the judged upright. This is the orientation order-parameter model (PLUMB). We prove that $c_1$ is, up to a constant factor, the only additive summary of axes (lines without arrowheads) that scales with line strength and turns twice as far as the field rotates. With Li and Matin's own length gain and no per-configuration fit, the model matches their multiline coefficients in-sample. Their data rule out complete summation but not simple averaging for long lines. But a line's effect is fourfold (period $90^\circ$): it reverses past $45^\circ$ (Li and Matin, 2005a; Vingerhoets et al., 2009), and near-horizontal lines shift VPV as near-vertical ones do, which $c_1$ forbids. In a two-harmonic fit to Li and Matin's data, $c_1$ carries an estimated 2--4% of a near-vertical line's slope and a fourfold term the rest. To first order in tilt, for near-vertical segments, the readout equals the committee rule Shavit, Li, and Matin (2026) fit to eight line-array experiments, with its drives read as concentrations. If the half-fields' signals are read as sum and difference, perceived eye level (VPEL) is the difference, recovering Shavit, Li, and Matin's (2013) reversed integration rules; a fourfold term gives the same signals, so this does not single out $c_1$. Re-analysed data (30 observers) show the predicted signs and nulls, an internal check only; the cross-field gain is fitted.

Abstract
Mineral dust is one of the most abundant atmospheric aerosol types and remains a major source of uncertainty in estimates of aerosol radiative forcing. This uncertainty is particularly important in the far-infrared (FIR) spectral region, where the optical properties of natural mineral dust, especially its complex refractive index (CRI), remain poorly known. Previous measurements have commonly relied on pellet- or film-based methods, which can alter particle size, shape, and vibrational modes, inducing errors in the CRI determination. In this study, we present a laboratory investigation on six natural mineral dust samples with contrasting compositions and originated from major dust source regions worldwide, including Arizona, Atacama, China, Niger, Saudi Arabia, and Tunisia. Suspended dust aerosols were generated under controlled dry conditions and characterized using extinction spectroscopy from the infrared 250 to 4000 cm^-1 (2.5 to 40 micrometers) to the Ultraviolet-Visible (UV-Vis) from 10000 to 40000 cm^-1 (0.25 to 1 micrometer) spectral range, combined with particle size distribution measurements. The real and imaginary parts of the CRI were retrieved for all six samples, revealing a significant absorption and pronounced spectral variability in the FIR. These variations should be accounted for in remote sensing and dust radiative effect studies.

Generative sampling with physics-informed kernels
Authors: Friederike Ihssen, Renzo Kapust, Jan M. Pawlowski
Venue: PoS LATTICE2025 (2026) 055
First: 2026-10-30T15:17:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T15:17:36+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We construct a generative network for Monte-Carlo sampling in lattice field theories and beyond, for which the layerwise propagation is computed and optimised independently on each layer. The architecture uses physics-informed renormalisation group flows that provide access to the layerwise propagation step from one layer to the next in terms of a simple first order partial differential equation for the respective renormalisation group kernel through a given layer. Thus, it transforms the generative task into that of solving once the set of independent and linear differential equations for the kernels of the transformation. As these equations are analytically known, the kernels can be refined iteratively. This allows us to structurally tackle out-of-domain problems generally encountered in generative models and opens the path to further optimisation. We illustrate the practical feasibility of the architecture within simulations in scalar field theories.

Commutative Coarse-graining on the Sphere
Authors: Chayut Teeraratkul, Hussein Aluie
First: 2026-10-16T17:52:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T15:12:09+00:00
Comments: Submitted to Europhysics Letters: Focus Issue on Complex Flows and Complex Fluids associated with the StatPhys29 satellite conference
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Filtering by spatial convolution underlies coarse-graining and Large Eddy Simulation, the analysis of multiscale dynamics, and data processing across the sciences. Yet, as commonly practiced on the sphere, it violates a basic consistency requirement: commutation with differential operators, which is necessary for deriving coarse-grained dynamics and preserving the physics across lengthscales. The root of the problem is nonlocality of filtering, which compares directions in different tangent planes. The same reason underlies the Foucault pendulum's precession, and it is why the commutation failure is intrinsically geometric rather than being due to the coordinate system or numerical grid. A recently proposed generalized spherical convolution is, for a given scalar kernel, the unique linear operator commuting with the radial projection, divergence, and curl. Any consistent linear operator, be it spectral truncation, wavelet transform, diffusion, Green's-function inversion, or a machine-learned convolution, necessarily takes this form. From a simulation of an unstable jet, we demonstrate commutation to within numerical round-off, whereas common filtering that ignores curvature produces significant spurious divergence where none exists and gradient errors comparable to the gradients themselves.

Abstract
A large number of photometric and photographic position data of noctilucent clouds (NLC) obtained at 54-61 deg N, 29-50 deg E in 2020-2026 is used, comprising about 20,000 individual measurements of the altitude of NLC fragments recorded by a number of cameras. Comparison with published long-base triangulation measurements starting from 1890 reveals a descending trend in NLC altitude with a 99.5% confidence level. The current descent rate is -1.2 km per century, or half of the model prediction based on the current increase of CO2 in the atmosphere. Other factors that can influence NLC altitude are considered: solar longitude (season), local solar time, lunar tides, latitude, and solar activity. Two independent methods of NLC altitude determination based on the same photometric data are considered and compared.

From fleet trajectories to single-cell promises: hierarchically weighted conformal prediction for battery SOH monitoring
Authors: Xiangyu Liu
First: 2026-10-30T13:02:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T13:02:52+00:00
Comments: Under review at Applied Energy
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Interval predictions from conformal prediction are usually read as "90% coverage", but on nested battery data that number promises the fleet average, not the individual cell. We measure the gap on 124 public LFP cells under a preregistered three-tier reporting protocol (fleet, batch, cell): pooled coverage looks healthy (0.875-0.906 across splits) while the worst cell is covered 0.1-2% of the time, with exact binomial tests against the nominal level. We then repair part of the gap with a hierarchically weighted conformal engine whose weights consume only side information frozen at prediction time - signature features, batch labels, cycle indices - and never touch residuals. The preregistered repair criterion passes: 10th-percentile cell coverage moves 0.750 to 0.830 (53% of the gap), the worst cell 0.642 to 0.694, with zero deferrals and a flat sensitivity spectrum. What the engine does not repair is localized rather than left open: under protocol drift the frozen weights harm monotonically on the SOH scale (the absolute scale is reported in full), a structural dead end we report instead of tuning away; and repair capacity is bounded by calibration-pool battery diversity, measured on both datasets. On a cross-chemistry external set the calibrated intervals collapse (0.286), while the within-domain skeleton replicates (pooled 0.933, worst cell 0.686). The deliverable is a reporting protocol - three tiers of promise anchored to measured regimes, worst-cell statistics, deferral share - that makes per-cell interval claims auditable before deployment.

Hyperbolic embedding of higher-order simplicial networks
Authors: Martin Guillemaud, Vito Latora, Mario Chavez
First: 2026-10-30T12:50:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T12:50:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many biological interactions involve groups rather than pairs, as when several proteins assemble into a multi-protein complex. Simplicial complexes naturally capture these collective interactions. Existing hyperbolic embeddings represent network hierarchy and similarity through a few interpretable coordinates, but rely only on pairwise links: they cannot distinguish three units engaged in a common interaction from three units connected only in pairs. We introduce here the hyperbolic higher-order (HHO) embedding, which embeds a simplicial complex into the Poincaré disk, while incorporating its group interactions. The hyperbolic area of the triangle spanned by three interacting nodes then measures how compactly their interaction is represented. Testson synthetic and real-world networks, including a co-authorship network and the protein interaction network of \textit{Escherichia coli}, show that HHO reduces the hyperbolic area of triangles representing simplices relative to those obtained from pairwise network embeddings. HHO also recovers node groups more accurately than pairwise embeddings and a weighted-clique alternative, with performance competitive with Euclidean embeddings, while providing a low-dimensional representation that can be analyzed with the tools of network geometry.

Many biological interactions involve groups rather than pairs, as when several proteins assemble into a multi-protein complex.

Abstract
Satellite-derived bathymetry (SDB) is often proposed as a low-cost complement to multibeam surveys of ports, but its accuracy is seldom measured on an area the model has never seen. We test SDB in the turbid, shallow and tidal waters of the Abu Dhabi coast against 285,619 multibeam soundings from twelve surveys at six port areas, reduced to Lowest Astronomical Tide. The Stumpf ratio, the Lyzenga transform, a monotonic gradient-boosted tree model (LightGBM) and a U-Net ensemble were trained on 72 Sentinel-2 images and tested in transfer to an unseen site and with local control (about 20% of a site's soundings, spatial blocks beyond a 225 m buffer). The validation design changed the result more than the model: the same LightGBM model gave 0.57 m RMSE under a random pixel split, 1.30 m under spatial blocks and 1.66 m at an unseen site. In transfer the Stumpf ratio (1.45 m) was not beaten by LightGBM, the U-Net or Lyzenga. With local control a hybrid of the tree output and the band ratio reached 1.11 m over 0-11 m, 67% of pixels within the IHO S-44 Order 2 tolerance and a conformal 95% interval with 95% observed coverage; in clear water it reached 0.29-0.53 m. The seabed could not be read beyond 11 m, and summer images were five to six times more turbid than winter ones. ICESat-2 ATL24 agreed with the surveys to 0.25 m after a depth-dependent offset was removed. A 60 m reconnaissance mosaic of 17,200 km2 of the UAE coast, calibrated cell by cell on ICESat-2 without any vessel survey, reached 1.42 m RMSE on held-out blocks over half of the product. Sentinel-2 SDB with about a hundred control depths per image is a usable reconnaissance and change-monitoring tool in these waters to about 10 m; it does not meet S-44 Order 1 without local control, and accuracies from random splits should not be taken as operational accuracy.

Abstract
Battery swapping replaces a depleted EV battery with a charged one, reducing waiting time and enabling off-peak charging. Evidence on acceptance, however, remains concentrated in Asian markets, and most studies examine individual adoption intentions. This study asks whether current UK EV users judge swapping differently as a purchase option and as a technology for the transport system. Using Behavioural Reasoning Theory, an integrated choice and latent variable model, and latent class analysis, we analyse perceived usefulness, energy-system concerns, practical barriers, and satisfaction with current charging. Usefulness and energy-system concerns relate more strongly to expectations about the system. Battery compatibility and station coverage relate more strongly to personal preference, while spare battery costs relate to expectations about network viability. User groups differ in how they balance benefits, barriers, and satisfaction with existing charging. Policy should address driver barriers and network viability as distinct but connected priorities.

Abstract
Accurate estimation of aboveground biomass density is essential for quantifying forest carbon stocks. NASA's GEDI mission provides valuable canopy structure data, but its sparse sampling necessitates the use of simulators to calibrate biomass models at field inventory locations. The widely used simulator of Hancock et al. (2019) emulates GEDI waveforms from airborne LiDAR point clouds, yet it has never been validated over European temperate forests. Here, we compare approximately 8,100 pairs of observed and simulated GEDI relative height (RH) profiles across French forests using the national airborne LiDAR program as input. We separate two sources of error: waveform modeling differences, assessed by referencing both simulated and real RH metrics to a common ALS-derived ground elevation, and ground detection bias, evaluated by comparing each GEDI L2A processing algorithm against the ALS reference. Under the baseline configuration, the mean absolute bias across the full RH profile reaches 0.67 m in leaf-on and 1.20 m in leaf-off conditions. Switching to intensity-based return weighting and selecting the a3 L2A algorithm reduces these biases to 0.43 m and 0.42 m respectively. The a3 algorithm also achieves near-unbiased ground detection (+0.04 m versus -0.82 m for the default), directly reducing a previously overlooked source of error. We also show that leaf-off acquisitions and low-sensitivity shots, both typically excluded from standard biomass products, are simulated as reliably as their counterparts, substantially expanding the potential calibration and inference datasets.

Abstract
The Beaujolais vineyard, located in France's Auvergne-Rh{ô}ne-Alpes region, is internationally recognized for its geological diversity and the strong identity of its ten crus. Guaranteeing authenticity of these appellations is essential for producers and regulators, as mislabeling and fraud can undermine both economic value and consumer trust. Scenario-conditioned flow matching for probabilistic generation of three-component ground-motion waveforms
Authors: Yi Ding, Jinjun Hu, Su Chen, Xianwei Liu, Zhongxiang Zhang, Zongchao Li, Xiaojun Li, Lili Xie
First: 2026-06-30T08:41:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T08:47:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Performance-based seismic risk assessment requires three-component acceleration histories compatible with specified source, path, and site conditions. Conventional ground-motion prediction equations provide scalar intensity measures, while many generative waveform models learn amplitude and waveform shape within a single high-dimensional target. We present WaveFlowGMM, a two-stage probabilistic ground-motion model that uses peak ground acceleration (PGA) as an amplitude interface between scenario conditioning and waveform generation. The amplitude stage uses physics-informed symbolic learning to estimate component-wise PGA medians and a full cross-component covariance. The waveform stage uses few-step AlphaFlow in an invertible wavelet-packet coefficient space to generate normalised three-component histories that are rescaled by sampled PGA. Tests on an event-level NGA-West2 holdout set show that the generated motions recover the main magnitude, distance, and site scaling, keep peak and spectral residuals close to zero, preserve three-component amplitude dependence, and yield velocity and displacement histories without systematic drift after integration of the generated three-component acceleration histories. The framework provides an interpretable and computationally efficient candidate component for waveform-level seismic hazard and risk analysis.

Abstract
Temporal energy distribution strongly affects nonlinear structural response and cumulative damage. We propose a multi-conditional diffusion framework for ground motion synthesis that simultaneously matches temporal energy evolution and target response spectra. Abstract
Achieving net-zero emissions requires rapid innovation, yet the necessary technological knowhow is scattered across industries and countries. Abstract
Parameter estimates of stochastic actor-oriented models for network evolution are notoriously hard to interpret. Especially a comparison of parameter estimates is problematic, both between different models for the same data set and between the same model for different data sets. Effect size measures based on the calculation of marginal effects are proposed as an alternative to standardize the scale of interpretation but can and should not universally erase the effects of differences in scale and heterogeneity. Abstract
The ngVLA intends to utilize Water Vapor Radiometry (WVR) as an approach to lower calibration overheads for tropospheric phase fluctuation correction in its high frequency bands (5 \& 6; 30.5-116 GHz) to levels below those feasible with fast switched reference gain calibration. As part of the program to develop a WVR system for the ngVLA, this memo summarizes the current landscape of water vapor radiometry experience, the situation with the CWVRs, how they relate to the ngVLA WVR requirements, and the work needed to operationalize the CWVRs as useful test systems and to make broader progress. A path involving tests to establish the functionality, stability, sensitivity, and switched power cal/data synchronism of the CWVR system in the lab and on antenna, followed by on-sky tests, and a parallel effort to assess the utility of autocorrelation outputs of the VLA K-band receivers for delay estimation is recommended. Abstract
Understanding how much and where information is nested in sets of data obtained using different parameters and metrics is a typical problem in the analysis of high-dimensional datasets.
