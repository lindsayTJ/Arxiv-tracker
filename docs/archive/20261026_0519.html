Causal inference in staggered adoption panels: The correct comparison set depends on your intervention
Authors: Ian Lundberg, Yaling Xu
First: 2026-10-22T16:46:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:46:04+00:00
Comments: 36 pages, 11 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A powerful data structure for causal inference is staggered adoption: many units are observed over many time periods, during which some units adopt an irreversible treatment at various time points while others remain untreated. Popular methods that apply in this setting include difference in difference, fixed effects, matching, and synthetic control. All of these methods compare the future outcomes of treatment adopters and non-adopters to answer the question: for units who adopt treatment, what outcomes would have been realized if they had remained untreated? We show that this question actually hides two very different causal questions of interest. The first is what would have happened under a momentary intervention to prevent treatment at its factual adoption time. The second is what would have happened to a treated unit under a longitudinal ban preventing treatment at all periods until outcome measurement. We show how to answer each question under conditionally ignorable treatment adoption or under parallel trends. We highlight that common methods cannot answer the second question because this question requires a time-varying comparison set with time-varying weights.

Detecting adverse high-order drug combinations from individual case safety reports using computational statistics on disproportionality measures
Authors: Jules Bangard, Einar Holsbø, Kristian Svendsen, Vittorio Perduca, Etienne Birmel{é}
Venue: Computo, 2026
First: 2025-04-01T10:56:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:38:40+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Adverse drug reactions linked to the intake of drug combinations are a critical concern in pharmacovigilance, particularly as the controlled environment of clinical trials often lacks the scale and diversity to detect rare events involving multiple medications. While spontaneous reporting systems provide the necessary breadth for post-market surveillance, identifying overrepresented drug cocktails within such large-scale data remains a significant computational challenge. This study introduces a computational framework for the detection of drug cocktails associated with adverse events, leveraging disproportionality analysis on individual case safety reports. By integrating the Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical classification, the framework extends beyond individual drugs to capture hierarchical pharmacological relationships, enabling exploration of the space of drug combinations beyond pairwise analysis. To address biases inherent in existing disproportionality measures, we employ a hypergeometric risk metric, while a Markov Chain Monte Carlo algorithm provides robust empirical p-value estimation for the risk associated with cocktails. A genetic algorithm further facilitates efficient identification of high-risk drug cocktails. A post-treatment step based on penalized logistic regression allows distinguishing true pharmacological interactions from combined individual effects for cocktails of any size. Validation on synthetic and FDA Adverse Event Reporting System data demonstrates the method's efficacy in detecting established drugs and drug combinations associated with myopathy-related adverse events. Implemented as an R package, this framework offers a reproducible, scalable tool for post-market drug safety surveillance.

A Diagnostic for Bias in Linear Mixed Model Estimators Induced by Dependence Between the Random Effects and the Corresponding Model Matrix
Authors: Andrew T. Karl, Dale L. Zimmerman
Venue: Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference, 211, March 2021, Pages 107-118
First: 2020-03-18T08:25:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:47:58+00:00

Abstract
We explore how violations of the often-overlooked standard assumption that the random effects model matrix in a linear mixed model is fixed (and thus independent of the random effects vector) can lead to bias in estimators of estimable functions of the fixed effects. However, if the random effects of the original mixed model are instead also treated as fixed effects, or if the fixed and random effects model matrices are orthogonal with respect to the inverse of the error covariance matrix (with probability one), or if the random effects and the corresponding model matrix are independent, then these estimators are unbiased. The bias in the general case is quantified, and a diagnostic for its random-effects contribution is compared to a randomized permutation distribution of the predicted random effects, producing an informative summary graphic for each estimator of interest. This is demonstrated through the examination of sporting outcomes used to estimate a home field advantage.

Modelling wide-swath altimeter observation-error correlations with a regularised diffusion operator
Authors: Olivier Goux, Anthony Weaver, Andrea Piacentini
First: 2026-10-22T15:10:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:10:25+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Unaccounted-for observation-error correlations have long been known to be detrimental to the quality of atmospheric and ocean analyses produced by data assimilation (DA) systems. While progress has been made over the past decade on modelling inter-channel error correlations, modelling spatial error correlations remains a challenge for large datasets. Common strategies for mitigating the harmful effects of spatial error correlations, such as variance inflation or data thinning, prevent the extraction of valuable small-scale information from the observations. With the emergence of new sources of high-resolution observations, such as those from the Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) wide-swath altimeter, the need to address this limitation of DA systems has become increasingly pressing. To do so, we consider a diffusion-based observation-error correlation operator, extending previous work on modelling correlated errors in conventional nadir altimeter data. When observation-error correlation length scales are large, the original method can suffer from an excessive sensitivity of the inverse correlation operator to small-scale perturbations. Since actual estimates of observation-error correlations from SWOT suggest the presence of large length scales, a more robust formulation of the method is required. We present a regularised formulation of the diffusion-based correlation operator that eliminates this excessive sensitivity while preserving computational efficiency. We describe the implementation of the operator for a mesh constructed from SWOT observation locations and demonstrate its application in a global ocean DA system. To validate the new formulation, we perform DA experiments with simulated SWOT observations. These experiments confirm that the regularised correlation operator provides an effective, robust and practical approach for representing wide-swath altimeter observation-error correlations in operational ocean DA systems.

Abstract
When individuals make simultaneous decisions across multiple ordered dimensions, standard multivariate ordered choice models impose narrow bracketing, with each dimension decided as if the others did not exist. We develop a general rectangular structure model capturing broad bracketing while nesting the standard model. The framework introduces two layers of dependence, one through the threshold structure associated with decision rules and the other through the correlation of latent utilities. We provide microfoundations, prove identification, and discuss estimation. We showcase the model in applications to parental investment in children's education, cryptocurrency familiarity and expectations, and health insurance, where the latter separates moral hazard from adverse selection.

Abstract
Tropical cyclones (TCs) pose significant risk to infrastructure and human safety, and the frequency and intensity of large TCs are expected to increase in the future. Quantifying such risk to coastal and inland regions is challenging, largely due to the rare nature of TCs which limits historical data availability. We introduce a new stochastic model of TC wind fields conditional on a small number of physical variables commonly found in synthetic and observed storm track databases. The model consists of a physically-motivated mean trend and an expansion of the residual field into a wavelet frame. We demonstrate the approach on a dataset consisting of historical data for storms impacting the northwest Gulf of Mexico. The model adequately captures asymmetry and nonstationary space-time correlation structures of observed wind fields.

Abstract
We revisit a neural negative binomial model for weekly earthquake counts on a 3 x 3 degree grid over the Tian Shan and Pamir, and ask which of its conclusions survive a careful evaluation. At the completeness magnitude of 4.5, the 144-week test period holds 2448 cell-weeks; six cell-weeks, in five distinct weeks, have three or more events. Two conclusions survive. First, Poisson forecasts, temporal ETAS included, understate the probability of such a busy week: ETAS expects 0.72 where six occurred, a shortfall by a factor of 8.3 (p = 0.00009, exact test). One dispersion parameter removes it (ETAS with dispersion expects 4.45, p = 0.58); simulating aftershock chains within the week also reproduces the tail but overshoots the total count by about a quarter. Second, the network's advantage over a linear model disappears once that model has an intercept per cell: the remaining gain is 3.4% of deviance, not significant (p = 0.57 over five test years), and it comes from one cell-week out of the 4420 scored; without it the network is 0.7% worse. A blind test on 2024 to September 2026, with every model fitted on data through 2023, confirms the tail result: Poisson forecasts are rejected (p from 3 x 10^-8 to 0.001), dispersion models are not (p between 0.25 and 0.84), except the NB GLM, whose level blew up out of sample and which is rejected at p = 0.003. Out of sample, model families differ in how they extrapolate beyond the training range, not in the per-cell term. We also measure the evaluation traps met on the way: a biased reference level, a single seed (five of six effects in v1's results table change sign), too few bootstrap resamples, and a threshold below completeness, which shrinks the ETAS shortfall from 8.3 to 2.8 times. Optimal Search Strategies under Finite Resource Constraints: The Role of Environmental Memory and Directional Information in Informed and Blind Protocols
Authors: Hugues Meyer
First: 2026-10-22T13:25:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T13:25:39+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Improving the efficiency of a stochastic search often requires to provide the searching agent with resources that modify its dynamics and help it explore space more quickly. Although such resources are often assumed to be unlimited and treated as a cost to mitigate, many realistic search processes operate under a finite resource budget, e.g. in processes with environmental memory in which the searcher deposits long-lasting cues along its path that it can interact with at later times to guide its further search. Here, we address the question of how to allocate a limited budget to maximize search efficiency by investigating a one-dimensional diffusing agent whose access to resources is constrained. We consider three resource models, namely an external force applied by an external controller to pull the searcher towards the target, directional flags deposited by the searcher along its path that redirect its motion towards the target, and self-generated chemorepulsive cues that act as a force field. For all these models, we find that resource usage should be concentrated in narrow regions of space when information about the searcher's current and past positions is available. In contrast, in blind strategies where a predefined time protocol is followed, resources should be used at a steady rate to ensure it to be used at most of its capacity by the time it reaches the target. We further show that blind strategies in non-Markovian processes with environmental memory are significantly less efficient due to the inherent fluctuations in the locations of deposited cues.

Can We Trust Evaluations of AI Weather Forecasts for Extremes? How Evaluation Protocols Shape Model Rankings
Authors: Tobias Biegert, Sam Allen, Zhongwei Zhang, Sebastian Lerch
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-22T13:19:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T13:19:16+00:00
Comments: Accepted paper at NeurIPS 2026 Workshop: TAE (Trust-AI-Eval): Can We Trust AI Evaluation?
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
AI weather models perform competitively with traditional physics-based models at a fraction of the computational cost, and they are thus already used operationally. However, studies reach conflicting conclusions about their ability to forecast extreme weather events, and comparisons across studies are complicated by differing evaluation protocols. We discuss the challenges that arise when comparing AI and physics-based weather forecasts for extreme events. Within a common experimental design, we find that model rankings are highly sensitive to the choice of evaluation metrics: the considered physics-based model tends to perform best when evaluation is restricted to observed extreme events, whereas AI models generally perform better when evaluated using weighted scoring functions. A contingency table analysis shows that AI models underpredict the frequency of extremes, so that their scores are generally dominated by misses, while the physics-based model forecasts extremes more frequently and its scores are dominated by false alarms. Different evaluation metrics penalise these errors differently. Further score decompositions confirm that the score differences arise primarily from miscalibration rather than discrimination ability. This sheds light on the contrasting results in the literature, and highlights the importance of the evaluation metrics used to evaluate AI models.

Authors: Ioana Colfescu, Alice Pigelet, Chen Lu, Massimo A. Bollasina, Silvio Gualdi

Abstract
Air quality policy will cut aerosol and ozone precursor emissions, and studies disagree on how the tropical Pacific circulation and the El Nino Southern Oscillation (ENSO) will respond. Here we show, in 12 Earth system models with and without air quality controls, that the cuts strengthen the Pacific trade winds and offset half to two thirds of the weakening projected without them. The response is absent with prescribed sea surface temperatures and detectable only after 2040. It is consistent with the Indian Ocean warming relative to the Pacific, which also ranks the responses to cuts made region by region. ENSO amplitude changes by less than a tenth of its value, the smallest change this ensemble could detect. The change implied by the mean state shift is a sixth of that, and an ENSO model trained without the reduced emission runs predicts none. El Nino's regional impacts still change, because the background climate does.

Order-Disorder in Fe-Si Alloys: Implications for Seismic and Transport Properties of Earth's Inner Core
Authors: Cong Liu, Xin Deng, R. E. Cohen
First: 2025-11-28T06:07:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T13:15:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Understanding the structure and dynamics of Earth's inner core is essential for constraining its composition, thermal evolution, and seismic behavior. Silicon is widely considered a major light element in the core. Here, we use first-principles molecular dynamics and thermodynamic modeling to investigate the structural, elastic, and transport properties of iron-silicon (Fe-Si) alloys under inner-core conditions. By computing the Gibbs free energies of multiple solid phases, including ordered B2, hexagonal close-packed (hcp), face-centered cubic (fcc), and body-centered cubic (bcc) Fe-Si, we construct a phase diagram relevant to inner-core pressures and temperatures. We identify a pronounced miscibility gap between hcp and cubic Fe--Si phases, with phase coexistence over approximately 6--11 wt\% Si at 6000 K. The cubic phase exhibits a strong order-dependent elastic response, with disordered bcc showing a large intrinsic shear anisotropy (22.9\% at 6000 K), in contrast to the nearly isotropic hcp phase (0.6\%). Meanwhile, disordered bcc Fe--Si produces shear wave velocities ($V_S=3.73$ km$\cdot$s$^{-1}$) and Poisson's ratios comparable with seismological constraints. In addition, disordered bcc Fe--Si exhibits a lower thermal conductivity than hcp Fe--Si, providing a quantitative constraint on phase-dependent electronic heat transport. These results demonstrate that chemical ordering strongly controls the stability and physical properties of cubic Fe--Si alloys, and suggest that metastable or localized cubic Fe--Si phases may contribute to the structural and transport complexity of Earth's inner core.

Prediction Uncertainty Controlled Computer Model Calibration
Authors: Ryan Heejoon Byun, Dayeon Yoon, Won Chang
First: 2026-10-22T12:22:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T12:22:01+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Computer-model calibration is usually undertaken in service of prediction, yet neither Bayesian Kennedy-O'Hagan (KOH) calibration nor amortized simulation-based inference is built to control the predictive error of the calibrated simulator. When parameters are weakly identified or the data-model discrepancy is misspecified, the simulator-output bands induced by a nominal posterior level can be far from nominal. We propose a two-layer framework that targets predictive miscoverage directly. First, a conditional flow-matching model learns an amortized posterior for the calibration parameters, trained on simulator outputs perturbed with Gaussian-process discrepancies of varying structure. Second, conformal risk control arranges posterior draws for the target observation into nested candidate sets and selects the smallest one whose fixed-rank pointwise bands attain a prescribed time-averaged miscoverage on a synthetic calibration sample. Under exchangeability of calibration and target observations, the bands control expected miscoverage in finite samples. In a synthetic benchmark, the proposed bands are roughly 20% the width of KOH-MCMC bands with miscoverage near the 10% target. In calibrating an age-structured SEIR model to the 2010 Malawi measles outbreak, they are again sharper and near-nominal, and the weeks they fail to cover coincide with the asymmetric epidemic decline, exposing structural inadequacy of the mechanistic model.

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) can drive scientific software through function calling, but free-form tool use is fragile on long, data-dependent processing chains. Models omit arguments, invent units, feed the previous artifact into the wrong input, and report completion without a verifiable product. We present GeoAgent, an agent system for seismic data processing built on Madagascar's roughly 1{,}965 command-line programs. GeoAgent exposes 54 operator agents (51 generated from a declarative specification), retrieves from an index of 1{,}937 operators, and encodes the processing flow as a nine-stage state graph. Four explicit contracts---named artifacts, input resolution, parameter takeover, and artifact validation---move everything the graph can determine out of the model's discretion. A deterministic geometry tool parses the self-describing SPS/RPS/XPS and SEG-Y headers, cross-checks eight consistency conditions, and reproduces the reference shell recipe exactly. The system runs in containers with session- and task-level isolation, bounded execution, and defenses against silent failures. On a 2-D land dataset, the nine-stage pipeline completes in about six to eleven minutes and yields a verified artifact at every stage, while retrieval reduces per-turn tool context from about 12k to 2k tokens. We report the design, deployment experience, and motivating failure modes.

First: 2026-10-22T10:49:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T10:49:29+00:00

Abstract
Frequency-domain wavefield modeling underpins seismic imaging and inversion, where repeated Helmholtz solutions become costly as velocity models, source locations, and frequencies vary. Neural operators learn mappings between physical fields, and their accuracy can decline outside the training distribution. Diffusion models learn wavefield distributions and generally require repeated sampling evaluations to produce accurate fields. A conditional mean flows model is introduced for complex scattered-wavefield generation. The analytical background wavefield serves as a condition alongside the velocity model, supplying spatially organized information about the source location and frequency. Learning the scattered wavefield avoids the point-source singularity in the target and supports generalization across these physical conditions. The model estimates the average transport velocity over a finite interval connecting Gaussian noise to the scattered field. This representation captures the accumulated displacement and permits both full-interval one-step generation and interval-partitioned sampling. Trained on OpenFWI, the model is evaluated on in-distribution and out-of-distribution velocity structures, frequency generalization, and inference efficiency. In most evaluated settings, one-step mean flows sampling achieves lower wavefield error than multi-step diffusion. Because each sampling step requires another network evaluation, accurate one-step generation also reduces inference time. These results indicate that mean flows can provide accurate and efficient frequency-domain scattered-wavefield generation.

Authors: Shuo Yang, Dan Zhao, Tingting Xue, Chunhua Zeng, Yongwen Zhang, Xiaosong Chen

Abstract
The dynamical basis of stratospheric polar vortex (SPV) predictability remains unclear, particularly the relative roles of persistence, structural variability, and cross-level coupling. Here we combine eigen microstate theory with mesoscopic Granger-causality analysis of ERA5 geopotential height fields to quantify the predictive contributions of eigen microstates. We show that short-term predictability is dominated by the persistence of the leading stratospheric state, whereas higher-order stratospheric structures and tropospheric variability provide additional predictive information at subseasonal leads. Including these additional states increases the all-winter 30-day prediction correlation from 0.24 to 0.33 and reduces forecast error. These contributions exhibit strong lead-time dependence and vary between winters with and without sudden stratospheric warmings. Part of this predictive information overlaps with that contained in preceding 100 hPa eddy heat flux, linking state-resolved predictability to wave-related circulation variability. Our results unify SPV predictability within a multimodal, state-resolved framework and provide a physically interpretable pathway.

Abstract
Accurate, high-resolution vertical profiles of optical turbulence ($C_n^2$), which reflect local meteorology and topography, are crucial for ground-based optical astronomy and free-space optical communication. However, measuring these profiles or generating them with numerical weather models requires substantial operational or computational effort. In this work, we present OTProf, a deep-learning method that estimates high-resolution $C_n^2$ profiles from widely available coarse-resolution ERA5 reanalysis data. OTProf is based on the Squeezeformer architecture, modified for super-resolution by introducing grid-agnostic interpolation in the latent space. We train and evaluate OTProf in the Netherlands using high-resolution $C_n^2$ data obtained from the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) model. The commonly used Hufnagel-Valley model serves as the baseline. Overall, OTProf reproduces the vertical structure of WRF-based $C_n^2$ more accurately than Hufnagel-Valley and yields more accurate estimates of the Fried parameter $r_0$ and the scintillation index $σ_I^2$. Out-of-domain performance is assessed using a smaller WRF-based $C_n^2$ dataset covering the Canary Islands. Initial results indicate a performance drop because the orography differs markedly from the training domain. We show that output bias correction based on a few local simulations can help OTProf to adapt, but more work for domain adaptation is needed. OTProf is therefore primarily recommended for in-domain applications, e.g., generating $C_n^2$ climatologies from long ERA5 records.

Abstract
Soil moisture and temperature govern plant-water availability and microbial activity, making them key indicators for evaluating land-restoration efforts on degraded landscapes. However, the physical and data-driven models normally used to forecast them both need information that a newly instrumented restoration site does not yet have: hydraulic parameters for the former and months of per-site training data for the latter. We evaluate TimesFM 1, a 500M-parameter time-series foundation model, zero-shot on 15-minute telemetry from 27 sensor stations across three land-management regimes (Control, Treatment, Scald) at a semi-arid restoration site, forecasting 5 cm and 30 cm soil moisture and enclosure temperature at seven lead times from 1 h to 168 h. A CatBoost baseline trained walk-forward on the same origins with full history and weather covariates never wins overall, and we show that its weakness is partly a cold-start effect specific to short deployments. We additionally quantify the inference cost of zero-shot forecasting on commodity CPU hardware. Our results highlight a practical trade-off between zero-shot forecasting capability, predictive performance, and computational efficiency.

Learning Cole-Hopf Coordinates for Stochastic Growth Dynamics: the KPZ class
Authors: Lorenzo Mensi, Simone Cuonzo, Maria Chiara Angelini, Sergio Chibbaro, Cyril Furtlehner, Alberto Rosso
First: 2026-10-22T08:38:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T08:38:27+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Inferring effective equations from observations of nonlinear stochastic processes is challenging because individual trajectories are noisy realizations, while the relevant physical information is encoded in ensemble statistics. We introduce a data-driven framework that learns a change of variables in which nonlinear stochastic dynamics become linear. The model combines a neural change of representation with a linear stochastic latent evolution and is trained through a short-time moment-matching objective, thereby avoiding trajectory \rev{simulation} during optimization. This construction yields an interpretable generative model: both the learned transformation and the latent evolution operators can be directly inspected. We validate the method on the one-dimensional Kardar--Parisi--Zhang (KPZ) equation, a paradigmatic model of stochastic growth for which the Cole--Hopf transformation maps the nonlinear dynamics to the stochastic heat equation. From simulated height trajectories alone, the model recovers the local exponential Cole--Hopf map and the associated linear stochastic operator. Generated trajectories reproduce the main statistical signatures of the KPZ universality class, including the growth law $\mathrm{Var}(h^{t})\sim t^{2β}$ with $β=1/3$ and the GOE Tracy--Widom height distribution for flat initial conditions. These results show that interpretable stochastic equations can be inferred from data by learning representations in which the dynamics closes in a simple form.
