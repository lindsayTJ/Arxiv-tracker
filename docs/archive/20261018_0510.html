Causal Fingerprints Reveal Hidden Mechanisms in Partially-Observed Dynamical Systems
Authors: Zhang Jiang, Marios Andreou, Nan Chen
First: 2026-10-15T17:41:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:41:50+00:00
Comments: 42 pages, 15 figures, 6 tables. Submitted to Chaos
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In partially-observed dynamical systems, distinct hidden mechanisms can produce similar observable summary statistics while explaining individual events differently. We introduce causal fingerprints: realization-dependent patterns that track how information about a hidden state is revealed through subsequent evolution. Within a prescribed dynamical model, we compare the filtering posterior, based on observations up to a given time, with the smoothing posterior, obtained after adding an expanding window of future observations. We quantify this retrospective information gain using Kullback-Leibler divergence and track its evolution as the future window of observational data expands. The fingerprint reveals both the strength and temporal reach of hidden influence, which can vary across events and their stages. Because the observed trajectory is held fixed while hidden-state inference depends on the candidate model, fingerprints reveal how competing dynamical mechanisms explain the same observations even when selected statistics agree. In linear Gaussian systems, we derive a characteristic fingerprint timescale for the temporal reach of hidden influence. Calibrated surrogates matching the observable mean, variance, and decorrelation time can still produce distinct fingerprints when feedback memory, hidden-noise memory, or hidden forcing timescales are misrepresented. Nonlinear examples reveal mechanism-dependent signatures for extreme bursts, oscillatory variability, and regime switching, including multiplicative instability versus additive forcing, deterministic nonlinear feedback versus state-dependent multiplicative noise, and intrinsic bistability versus hidden double-well forcing. Causal fingerprints therefore provide a time- and event-resolved diagnostic for asking not only whether a model reproduces observed statistics, but how its hidden dynamics explain individual events.

Learning the information contained in the microstructure of data distributions
Authors: Lucrezia L. Baldo, Chiara Lionello, Edward D. Donkor, Matteo Becchi, Giovanni M. Pavan
First: 2026-10-15T17:41:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:41:31+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Understanding how much and where information is nested in sets of data obtained using different parameters and metrics is a typical problem in the analysis of high-dimensional datasets. Approaches based on relative data-driven metrics may be poorly quantitative and difficult to interpret, while accurate methods may be computationally expensive and impractical for large datasets. Here, we report an efficient, quantitative, and transferable method useful to this end, which we call "Micro-Information Analysis". Its foundation is based on the tight relationship that exists between the microscopic features of data distributions and the information they contain. The method performs preliminary unsupervised clustering, capturing microstructural features of data distributions such as the dominant Gaussian microdomains (microstates) present in them, their population, separation, and their residual non-Gaussian fraction. Leveraging the principle of clustering-and-minimization of Shannon entropy, this allows quantifying in bit (that is, in absolute metric) the information contained in the microstructure of the data, and how much information is nested in each Gaussian and non-Gaussian microdomain. The output of the analysis is a set of easily interpretable Information-Microstructure plots that allow to readily compare and rank data based on their information content and to graphically map the information breakdown among their various microenvironments in diagrams that are easy to interpret. We prove the efficiency of our method by analyzing and ranking data generated synthetically or from molecular simulations, demonstrating how "Micro-Information Analysis" is a physically-grounded quantitative tool useful for the study of physical systems from their data and for data science in general.

A New Method for Constraining 3D Exoplanet Atmospheres: Application to WASP-94A b's Panchromatic Spectrum
Authors: Sagnick Mukherjee, Luis Welbanks, Guangwei Fu, David K. Sing, Michael R. Line
First: 2026-10-15T17:35:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:35:06+00:00
Comments: Accepted for publication in The Astrophysical Journal Letters
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A growing number of transiting exoplanets exhibit spectroscopic evidence that their leading and trailing limbs differ. Interpreting these observations is now the limiting step. The standard approach of fitting limb-resolved transit depths results in highly anti-correlated transit depths, and treating them as independent measurements can result in strongly biased ($3σ$) retrieved molecular abundances. We present an inference framework that avoids these correlations by using the temporal signature of limb asymmetry instead. This wavelength-dependent shift in mid-transit time, or `time-shift spectrum', carries the limb-asymmetry information, is measurable from existing light curves with a spherically symmetric transit model, and is uncorrelated with the spherically-averaged transit depth. Simultaneously fitting the resulting transmission and time-shift spectrum with an independent atmospheric column for each limb enables the retrieval of limb-resolved properties from two independent measurements. We demonstrate that this approach reduces the mean squared error on the retrieved molecular abundances by factors of $\sim30-50$ relative to next best of the alternative approaches currently adopted in the field, and improves retrieved abundance precision by $\sim3-6\times$. We apply the framework to WASP-94A b and present its panchromatic limb-resolved spectrum, spanning 0.8-5~$μ$m and combining NIRISS/SOSS and NIRSpec/G395H observations. The planet's morning limb remains aerosol-obscured across this entire range, while its evening limb constrains the abundances of H$_2$O, CO$_2$, CO, and H$_2$S. We measure a limb-to-limb temperature difference of $415\pm58$~K at 1~mbar. This limb-resolved analysis finds [M/H]=+0.32$^{+0.11}_{-0.11}$ and C/O=0.724$^{+0.013}_{-0.013}$, consistent with the host star.

Can AI Forecast the Extremes? Evaluating Probabilistic Weather Models on Tropical Cyclones
Authors: Nathan Chalumeau, Xavier Renard, Remi Meynadier, Hugo Rakotoarimanga, Marcin Detyniecki, Emmanuel de Bezenac
First: 2026-10-15T15:35:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T15:35:37+00:00

Abstract
Weather prediction is undergoing an important shift, with data-driven AI models outperforming leading numerical weather prediction systems on average at a fraction of the computational cost. Yet, whether this success extends to extreme events remains contested. Earlier evaluations have exposed important shortcomings, but have largely focused on deterministic models and metrics, leaving the capabilities of the latest probabilistic models less understood. We conduct an extensive benchmark of tropical cyclone forecasting, comparing GenCast, FourCastNet 3, and FGN with ECMWF's operational physics-based ensemble, IFS-ENS. We develop a suite of probabilistic metrics and diagnostic analyses to assess cyclone tracking, intensity biases, uncertainty, and the wind-pressure relationship that characterizes storm structure. We find that, despite remaining limitations in intensity and ensemble spread, leading AI models substantially outperform IFS-ENS in cyclone tracking: FGN achieves better probabilistic location scores for 90% of evaluated storms in 2025. Further analyses reveal model-specific failure modes and suggest a lingering influence of pretraining data, pointing to opportunities for targeted improvement. Nonetheless, these results suggest that AI models are capable of capturing physical relationships that remain predictive well beyond typical weather conditions, motivating further investigation into how these capabilities emerge and how to strengthen them.

Abstract
Quantifying and evaluating the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) remains a fundamental challenge in modern data science and artificial intelligence. In this paper, we consider LLM evaluation based on their performance across items in multiple benchmark domains (e.g., mathematical reasoning and coding) within a leaderboard framework. Our goal is to address two core questions: (1) How do we derive more accurate domain-specific scores by borrowing information across domains? and (2) How do we define and estimate an overall score that aggregates performance across multiple domains? To solve these problems, we propose a novel statistical framework based on representation Multi-task Learning (MTL) and an item response theory model. Specifically, we define overall and domain-specific LLM traits through an Item Response Theory (IRT) model, and propose an MTL approach to estimate these traits from item-level response data. We develop a computationally efficient estimator and establish its minimax optimality under certain asymptotic regimes. This framework provides a rigorous measurement foundation for systematic LLM evaluation. We conduct extensive simulations, demonstrating the superior performance of the proposed method over competing methods. Crucially for the Applications and Case Studies section, we apply the proposed framework to MMLU response data from the Hugging Face Open LLM Leaderboard, covering 4,272 LLMs and 13,232 items across 56 subjects. The empirical analysis reveals substantial heterogeneity in domain size and difficulty, together with strong positive cross-domain dependence, highlighting the practical value and substantive insights generated by our approach.

Abstract
The Glacier Model Intercomparison Project (GlacierMIP) provides an international framework for the coordinated evaluation and comparison of global glacier evolution models and their projections of glacier change. Its fourth phase, GlacierMIP4, updates previous global glacier projections through a standardised experimental design. For the first time, the protocol includes simulations extending to 2300 and an overshoot scenario for a subset of climate projections and requires monthly output of individual mass-balance components such as melt, accumulation and refreezing, in addition to annual results for glacier mass and area. While permitting model-specific approaches to calibration and other aspects of model implementation, the protocol prescribes common atmospheric forcing data, inventory boundary conditions, and reporting conventions to enable consistent analyses of inter-model differences. Here, we describe the GlacierMIP4 experimental protocol, including the simulation domain, initialisation, atmospheric forcing, and standardised output requirements. More broadly, we emphasize the need for standardised terminology, units, sign convention and file formats in global glacier modelling to improve the traceability, interoperability, and comparability of results, and recommend this protocol as a blueprint for future global glacier modelling studies.

Authors: Marika D'Agostini, Massimo Ventrucci, Francesca K. de'Donato, Andrea Ranzi

Abstract
Surface urban heat islands (SUHIs) are commonly studied using satellite-derived land surface temperature (LST), but many analyses rely on static summaries or pointwise urban-rural contrasts, providing limited inference on temporal change, spatial dependence, and uncertainty. This study models LST as a stochastic spatio-temporal process using a spatially varying coefficient formulation that decomposes LST into a baseline spatial field and a location-specific temporal trend. This structure allows persistent thermal differences and local warming rates to be estimated within a unified probabilistic framework. SUHI is derived from the posterior distribution of the baseline spatial field by contrasting the estimated LST at a given pixel with the minimum estimated LST among non-urban pixels. Posterior excursion probabilities are used to identify areas where temporal changes are supported under simultaneous probability control. The framework is applied to summer Landsat 8 LST observations for Bologna, Italy, from 2013 to 2025. Results show strong spatial heterogeneity in SUHI, with higher values over impervious surfaces and lower values over forests and water bodies. Summer LST increased overall, but local trends varied markedly across the municipality. Joint excursion sets identify areas with warming greater than the domain-wide average and smaller clusters with statistically supported cooling. These results reveal changing thermal patterns across Bologna and help distinguish areas experiencing increasing surface heat from locations where thermal conditions have improved over time. The framework therefore provides an uncertainty-aware basis for monitoring urban thermal change.

Optical Coherent Combining in Multi-Aperture Free-Space Receivers through Atmospheric Turbulence via Optically Computed Alignment Gradients
Authors: Chen Cheng, Tong Luo, Siyu Gong, Qun Zhang, Linsheng Fan, Qi Wu, Yanfu Yang, Jiayin Xue
First: 2026-10-15T13:44:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T13:44:30+00:00

Abstract
Multi-aperture reception converts spatial diversity into a coherent-combining gain, but only if the relative phase and polarization of the collected field replicas are aligned before they are summed. In a satellite-to-ground downlink the atmosphere perturbs amplitude, phase and polarization, so the alignment must track a fluctuating channel rather than a fixed offset. This paper proposes a new optical-domain combining scheme in which the alignment information is computed by light itself: the ascent directions that maximize the combined output power are formed by optical interference and read out directly as differential photocurrents of balanced detection, so no estimate of the channel, no deliberate perturbation, and no per-branch digital processing is needed to obtain them. Every branch is aligned against one shared reference - a passive tap of the combined field itself - so no independent laser or optical frequency lock is required, and the update directions of all branches are available simultaneously in a single averaging window without frequency, code, or time multiplexing. The data path needs a single dual-polarization coherent receiver regardless of the aperture count, and the readout is built from elementary parts only - a 3 dB directional coupler, static phase shifters, a Faraday rotator, and balanced photodiodes - so no 90-degree optical hybrid, mixer, or polarization-diverse hybrid is required. The phase and polarization loops are fixed-gain and division-free, and each branch carries one phase modulator and one polarization controller with no amplitude actuator. A closed-form combining-gain and loss budget is derived, the assumptions and limitations of the scheme are stated explicitly, and experimental validation remains open.

Contact, Power, and Balls in Play: A Three-Rate Decomposition of Batting Average and the Disappearance of the .400 Hitter
Authors: Jim Albert

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Batting average factors exactly as BA = c [h + (1-h) b], where c is the rate of avoiding a strikeout, h is the home-run rate among non-strikeout at-bats, and b is batting average on balls in play (BABIP). The bracketed term is the rate f at which non-strikeout at-bats become hits, so the composition nests the two-factor identity BA = c f. Using the 256 major-league hitters with at least 300 at-bats in 2025, every season from 2004 to 2025, and 20,357 qualifying seasons from 1901 to 2019, we ask which rates behave like skills and how the corresponding abilities are related. Over 19 consecutive-season pairs the median year-to-year correlation is 0.86 for c, 0.74 for h and 0.41 for b; batting average (0.44) is no more repeatable than BABIP. A trivariate logistic-normal random-effects model gives single-season reliabilities of 0.91, 0.76 and 0.30, and a talent correlation between contact and power of -0.67 (95% profile interval -0.75 to -0.57), while BABIP talent is only weakly related to either. Historically, the negative covariance between c and f, which was near zero in the dead-ball era, explains why batting averages have become less dispersed, and so why no one has hit .400 since 1941. That covariance runs almost entirely through home runs: removing the contact--power channel from the 2010s covariance recovers 72% of the gap between modern and dead-ball dispersion in batting-average talent.

Abstract
Neural diffusion processes provide a scalable, non-Gaussian approach to modelling distributions over functions, but existing formulations are limited to single-task inference and do not capture dependencies across related tasks. We propose multi-task neural diffusion processes (MT-NDPs), an extension that incorporates a task encoder to enable task-conditioned probabilistic regression and few-shot adaptation across related functions. The task encoder extracts a low-dimensional representation from context observations and conditions the diffusion model on this representation, allowing information sharing across tasks while accommodating variable-sized context sets and preserving permutation invariance with respect to the ordering of context observations. We evaluate MT-NDPs through synthetic experiments and two real-world applications involving wind turbine data and school examination outcomes. The results demonstrate improved predictive accuracy relative to single-task diffusion baselines when context observations are available, alongside competitive performance against neural-process and Gaussian-process alternatives. In the wind-energy application, MT-NDPs achieve the lowest average coverage error among the evaluated models at larger context sizes, demonstrating improved predictive uncertainty calibration. Overall, our findings highlight the potential of task-conditioned diffusion models for uncertainty-aware multi-task regression and few-shot adaptation to previously unseen tasks.

Abstract
Tropical cyclones can produce extreme winds in the lowest portion of the boundary layer, posing high risks to critical energy infrastructure, including wind energy systems. This study evaluates the dynamic response of an idling wind turbine to turbulent inflow generated during the passage of a hurricane. Abstract
Reliable uncertainty quantification is central to modern forecasting. Conformal prediction offers model-agnostic intervals with coverage guarantee under exchangeability, but existing software often targets general prediction problems or individual time series procedures rather than integrated workflows for multistep forecasting applications. We introduce conformalForecast, an open-source R package for constructing, updating, and evaluating conformal prediction intervals for multistep time series forecasts. The package separates point forecasting from calibration, accepts forecasts from diverse sources through a common interface, and preserves horizon-specific forecast errors and calibration states. It implements several conformal calibration schemes spanning empirical-quantile, feedback-based, and dependence-aware approaches, together with tools for assessing coverage, interval width, and interval scores over time and across horizons. Fitted objects can be extended as new observations arrive while retaining previously issued intervals and avoiding recomputation at earlier forecast origins. An application to short-term electricity price forecasting across five Australian regions illustrates the workflow. Aggregate coverage is generally close to the nominal level, while rolling coverage and interval efficiency vary across methods and horizons. In the reported experiment, incremental updating reproduces full-reconstruction intervals under identical forecasting and calibration settings while substantially reducing computation. conformalForecast provides a reproducible and extensible framework linking conformal methodology with sequential forecasting practice.

Abstract
High-resolution origin-destination (OD) tables derived from digital mobility traces can preserve fine-scale collective movement structure, but sampling, population expansion, and privacy-motivated low-count suppression create heterogeneous observational support across OD entries and make apparent zeros difficult to interpret. We develop a reliability-aware refinement framework that combines each released OD entry with a time-resolved, data-informed radiation baseline through a closed-form cell-wise Gamma-Poisson update, without smoothing neighboring OD pairs. Applied to a 125 m hourly Tokyo OD product, the method preserves concentrated directional flows while adding lower-intensity, baseline-supported links. The relative contribution of released and model-supported structure varies substantially across OD pairs and departure hours, including among positive-flow entries: released flows are most strongly retained at 7:00, followed by 17:00, whereas the radiation baseline contributes more at 13:00. The inferred characteristic radius broadens the movement range represented by the baseline and exhibits structured variation around major station areas and rail corridors. Tokyo and Fukuoka also show a common increase in data-baseline separation at long distances. Under controlled Poisson thinning, calibrated refinement yields lower realization-averaged mean absolute error than an independently resampled no-refinement table across the tested conditions. This experiment provides internal calibration under imposed degradation, not external validation against true unsuppressed flows. The framework thus provides a transparent way to preserve fine-scale empirical mobility structure while making the cell-wise contribution of structured model support explicit.

Abstract
Demographic data are often collected across multiple dimensions such as time, age, subnational region, and sex. Modeling such data is challenging due to the interplay of high dimensionality, heterogeneity between groups, and stochastic variability within small groups. This paper proposes a Bayesian adaptive tensor smoothing model for multiway demographic counts that represents the latent log-rate array as a sum of rank-one components. Global-local shrinkage priors control the effective rank of the decomposition, the number of modes along which each component varies, and the smoothness that Gaussian process priors impose along ordered modes such as age and time. Posterior inference relies on a data augmentation Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm that interweaves centered and noncentered parameterizations in every layer of the model to keep mixing satisfactory in large and sparse count arrays. Applied to Swedish county-level and sex-specific mortality from 1969 to 2024, the model recovers stable demographic surfaces and life table quantities from noisy small-population data. In cross-validation exercises, the method outperforms both canonical demographic benchmarks and existing count tensor factorizations.

Abstract
Extrapolating treatment effects from related studies is a promising strategy for designing and analyzing clinical trials in situations where achieving an adequate sample size is challenging. Bayesian methods are well-suited for this purpose, as they enable the synthesis of prior information through the use of prior distributions. While the operating characteristics of Bayesian approaches for borrowing data from control arms have been extensively studied, methods that borrow treatment effects--quantities derived from the comparison between two arms--remain less well understood. In this paper, we present the findings of an extensive simulation study designed to address this gap, across six clinically motivated case studies covering continuous, binary, recurrent-event, and time-to-event endpoints. We varied target sample size, treatment-effect drift, and endpoint-specific design assumptions. Power and estimation performance were compared using a frequentist analysis of the target data alone, calibrated to the same actual type I error rate. Our results provide insights into the strengths and limitations of existing methods in the context of confirmatory trials. At matched actual type I error rates, power differences were usually small, with occasional gains over the reference analysis and larger losses for some adaptive procedures. Borrowing reduced MSE when source and target effects were consistent, but increasing drift eroded this benefit and could substantially reduce interval coverage. Commensurate priors with appropriately chosen hyperpriors offered the most favorable balance between estimation gains and robustness to conflict. The conditional power prior was competitive for estimation at comparable type I error rates, despite its lack of adaptation. Test-then-pool procedures often performed poorly. No method dominated across all criteria.

Abstract
The airborne fraction, the share of anthropogenic $CO_2$ emissions that remains in the atmosphere rather than being taken up by land and ocean, is a compact diagnostic of sink efficiency and a direct input to the remaining carbon budgets behind climate targets. Here we show that it has risen over the observational record, from $0.32$-$0.40$ in $1959$ to $0.47$-$0.50$ by $2024$ (ranges across estimators), above the $0.44$ commonly reported for recent decades. Applied to IPCC AR6 assessments, this rise reduces the remaining carbon budget by $23$-$48$ $GtCO_2$ for a given temperature target, up to roughly one year of current global fossil emissions. The trend has been difficult to detect because two features of the standard analysis obscure it. First, three high-leverage years following the $1991$ Pinatubo eruption dominate the volcanic term of the natural-variability adjustment and attenuate the estimated trend; smoothing those years, or using a volcanically robust $ENSO$ index, removes the artifact. Second, collapsing land-use and land-cover change emissions to a single series discards the disagreement among models that carries the information needed to resolve a small trend. Propagating the dispersion of $69$ land-use series together with the reported fossil-emission uncertainty into delta-method weights, weighted and generalized least squares recover a positive and significant trend, corroborated by a mixed-effects model and robust across a grid of $49$ natural-variability specifications that maps which treatment choices reproduce which published result.
