Quantifying Injection-Driven Interphase Mass Transfer within Porous Media via Time-Elapsed X-ray micro-Computed Tomography
Authors: Christopher A. Allison, Ruotong Huang, Anindityo Patmonoaji, Lydia Knuefing, Anna L. Herring
First: 2026-04-09T02:59:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:51:04+00:00
Comments: More feedback from reviewers we have reworked the paper to improve clarity and tone, with better emphasis on the distinction and utility of each approach
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Understanding interphase mass transfer is essential for a variety of applications in porous media, from groundwater remediation to geologic energy storage. While X-ray micro-Computed Tomography (micro-CT) is a measurement technique that can provide critical in situ observations, quantifying transient mass transfer requires models and workflows capable of compensating for the spatial and temporal resolution constraints. Current literature presents three analytical frameworks for evaluating interphase mass transfer using time-lapsed sequences of X-ray micro-CT scans: the Slice-Averaged Concentration approach, the Non-Classified per-Cluster approach, and the Classified per-Cluster approach. We systematically evaluate the frameworks and results for each of the three approaches as applied to several sequences of time-lapsed scans, each observing hydrogen dissolution experiments at varying injection rates. Estimates of average mass transfer coefficients vary within an order of magnitude, increasing as injection rate decreases. However, approach estimates diverged when approximating more complex, pore-scale phenomena, such as aqueous solute concentration. Per-Cluster approaches preserve more information, enabling quantification of important localized physicochemical processes such as dissolution fingering and cluster remobilization. Given the lack of ground-truth benchmarks for mass-transfer kinetic data in the conditions of interest to fields such as geologic storage, additional research is still needed to understand how these approaches vary under different state and flow conditions. The utility of one approach over another is ultimately determined by the desired level of system detail, at the cost of the computational resources required to achieve it. Our results provide a framework for researchers to select analytical approaches based on the desired level of physical detail.

Abstract
We present a method that models the evolution of an unbounded number of time series clusters by switching among an unknown number of regimes with linear dynamics. We develop a Bayesian non-parametric approach using a hierarchical Dirichlet process as a prior on the parameters of a Switching Linear Dynamical System and a Gaussian process prior to model the statistical variations in amplitude and temporal alignment within each cluster. By modeling the evolution of time series patterns, the method avoids unnecessary proliferation of clusters in a principled manner. We perform inference by formulating a variational lower bound for off-line and on-line scenarios, enabling efficient learning through optimization. We illustrate the versatility and effectiveness of the approach through several case studies of electrocardiogram analysis using publicly available databases.

The formulation is inspired by the shared frailty multivariate survival model for clustered data, and it allows for closed forms for all the relevant quantities, which allows for an efficient and fast computational solution of the associated inferential and prediction problems. Abstract
Understanding how much each variable contributes to an outcome is a central question across disciplines. A causal view of explainability is favorable for its ability in uncovering underlying mechanisms and generalizing to new contexts. Based on a family of causal explainability quantities, we develop methods for their estimation and inference. In particular, we construct a one-step correction estimator using semi-parametric efficiency theory, which explicitly leverages the independence structure of variables to reduce the asymptotic variance. For a null hypothesis on the boundary, i.e., zero explainability, we show its equivalence to Fisher's sharp null, which motivates a randomization-based inference procedure. Finally, we illustrate the empirical efficacy of our approach through simulations as well as an immigration experiment dataset, where we investigate how features and their interactions shape public opinion toward admitting immigrants.

Abstract
Ocean wave forecasting is essential for maritime safety, offshore operations, and coastal resilience, yet remains challenging due to systematic biases in physics-based models. Physical models, while widely used, rely on approximations and parameterizations that limit their accuracy under complex ocean-atmosphere conditions. To enhance ocean wave forecasting, we propose BridgeCast, within a physics-AI hybrid framework for bias correction. BridgeCast is a probabilistic model based on conditional flow matching (CFM) that learns to transform physical model forecasts into reanalysis-like fields. It treats physical forecasts as corrupted observations and employs a continuous-time generative process to bridge their distribution toward that of reanalysis data. BridgeCast is parameterized by a Transformer-based architecture that enables spatiotemporal modeling, incorporation of exogenous atmospheric variables, and flexible inference via both ordinary and stochastic differential equation formulations. Extensive experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate that BridgeCast consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across regions and forecast lead times.

Abstract
While deep learning offers powerful capabilities for scientific research, its application is often hindered by a lack of quantitative reliability. To address this, we introduce a probabilistic denoising framework that simultaneously extracts denoised signals and element-wise predictive uncertainties from noisy data. We demonstrate this approach on three-dimensional angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy data, showing that the model reliably recovers the spectral features of a cuprate superconductor from Poisson-distributed noise with an average count of only 0.02 electrons per voxel. Discovering Hidden Predictive States in Kinetic Transport
Authors: Pengfei Zhu
First: 2026-10-20T15:33:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T15:33:38+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
How do autonomous macroscopic transport laws emerge from kinetic dynamics, and which hidden degrees of freedom must survive coarse-graining? We address these questions by formulating kinetic state reduction as a problem of predictive sufficiency rather than conventional moment truncation. Starting from Boltzmann transport dynamics, we establish a quantitative connection between unresolved microscopic information, irreducible prediction error, and finite-horizon observability. Microscopic preparations with identical macroscopic observables but distinct subsequent evolution provide direct witnesses of incomplete dynamical closure. We develop an entropy-constrained learned state-reduction framework, demonstrated here through linear projections, that identifies the additional predictive states without prescribing their physical identities and determines their necessity through independent response tests, state deletion, and dynamical consistency. An exactly solvable three-velocity model demonstrates that a hidden higher-order moment can influence future energy transport despite identical initial energy and heat flux. Numerical experiments recover this missing direction and reveal how the required state dimension depends on observation scale, prediction horizon, preparation ensemble, and accuracy. In a synthetic 32-channel Boltzmann system, the learned representation identifies predictive combinations spanning multiple kinetic branches and relaxation groups that are not captured by low-order global moments. We further distinguish observable prediction accuracy from autonomous state closure and quantify the information retained through unresolved memory. These results establish an operational framework for testing the emergence of effective transport laws and identifying the microscopic information required for their predictive validity.

Abstract
Solar energetic particle (SEP) events, operationally defined as proton flux exceeding 10 pfu in the >10 MeV channel, threaten spacecraft systems, astronaut safety, and high latitude aviation, yet continuous real-time forecasting with adequate lead time remains difficult. We present SEPNET-PRISM-R, a near-real-time system that issues hourly, 24 hour ahead probabilistic SEP forecasts. The model integrates multimodal solar data - including active region patches, flare features, coronal mass ejection (CME) characteristics, and historical proton flux - to concurrently predict SEP occurrences, peak proton flux, soft X-ray flux, and CME velocity, with the primary objective being SEP forecasting. Building on existing literature, SEPNET-PRISM-R enhances operational performance by incorporating a postprocessing module that enables cross-validated decision making and interpretable quantification of feature importance. The training dataset spans from February 1986 to September 2025, with subsequent forecasts generated sequentially through the present via a real-time space weather prediction platform that provides predictive probabilities, feature attributions, and historical analogs, enabling more reliable and informed operational decision-making. The proposed framework substantially suppresses false alarm rates while maintaining robust event detection capabilities, as evidenced by case studies and aggregate statistics.

Diversity, Efficiency, and Incentives in Political Prediction Markets
Authors: Harry Crane, Koleman Strumpf
First: 2026-10-20T14:31:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T14:31:01+00:00
Comments: 71 pages, 11 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Critics of political prediction markets argue that prices are biased because traders are not representative of voters. We test this claim by analyzing data from political betting markets spanning more than 100 years. Our analysis yields two main findings: (1) Acros markets, traders were not representative of the voters. (2) Prices were not biased by political preferences of participants. For the 2024 election, we analyzed anonymized data from Kalshi, a CFTC-regulated exchange, the largest regulated political prediction market in the U.S. Kalshi's traders were 86% male and half were under 30. In its first month of listing election contracts, Kalshi's prices were more accurate than every poll-based forecast in the national race, the seven battleground states, and nineteen Senate races, and no poll-based forecast would have been profitable to trade against them in any group of presidential contests. Its volume came from areas that voted 28% Republican, while 60% of volume went to Republican positions. Traders with a single trade placed 73% of their volume on Harris. The association between a trader's location and the direction of positions declines with trading activity and is zero among active traders, who account for most of the volume. Prices can be accurate with unrepresentative traders when the forecasts of profit-motivated traders do not depend on their demographics. Two other markets corroborate our findings. In New York's 1900 market, bettors were older than the population and drawn from finance. On PredictIt in 2016, which limited each trader to $850 per contract, positions followed registered party more closely, as our theory predicts when market restrictions do not allow profit-motivated traders to set the price. Prediction market efficiency is driven by financial incentives over demographic representativeness.

Composite Variable Strategies for Continuous Endpoints
Authors: James Bell, Oliver N. Keene, Christian Bressen Pipper, Thomas Drury, Lorenzo Guizzaro, Marian Mitroiu, Tobias Mütze, Khadija Rerhou Rantell, Marcel Wolbers, David Wright
First: 2026-10-20T14:21:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T14:21:04+00:00

Abstract
Composite endpoints are common in clinical trials for binary, time-to-event or recurrent outcomes. Recently, there has been interest in estimands for continuous endpoints that employ a composite variable strategy for handling intercurrent events, whereby a value on the continuous scale is defined as the outcome. In this paper we describe these continuous composite endpoints where participants who experience the intercurrent event are assigned a pre-specified value on the outcome scale (which we call the failure value) instead of a measurement. We describe these endpoints and the difficulty of choosing the failure value when the mean is the chosen summary statistic. The large impact of the choice of failure value on the variance of the distribution of the endpoint is quantified as a function of the probability of the event and the standardised difference of the failure value from the mean of the unaffected values. Strategies for choosing failure values are discussed, emphasising the need to balance the clinical relevance and interpretability of the estimand with the need to reliably estimate it. Two potential solutions to situations where there is no relevant and viable choice of failure value are proposed; using failure values based on distributional characteristics to generate clinical relevance and using alternative summary measures to avoid the need to select specific values.

Abstract
The Galton--Watson process (GWP) is a discrete-time branching process model that provides a powerful tool for analyzing epidemic data and estimating key epidemiological parameters such as the basic reproduction number. When used with surveillance-based cluster size data, the GWP can also elicit information about the extent of transmission heterogeneity, even when each transmission process is not directly observable. When cluster size distribution data are available, the parameters that govern the transmission can be statistically inferred by using the probability mass function that corresponds to the observed cluster size data. For multi-type GWPs, however, real-world applications remain limited, possibly because of the absence of conceptually and practically straightforward approaches for deriving the closed-form solution of the final size distribution. In the present study, we propose a framework for computing the final size distribution of multi-type GWPs, using a method for the choice of the Cauchy integral contour. We provide examples of how our framework can be applied to both simulated data and real-world data of Middle East respiratory syndrome, and discuss potential pitfalls surrounding the identifiability of parameters for statistical inference when using likelihoods that are not conditioned on extinction.

Abstract
Attribution of observed historical climate evolution to externally forced change and internal variability is fundamental to understanding climate change and evaluating climate models. Yet such attribution remains challenging because the forced response is intertwined with internal variability in observations. Here, we develop a convolutional long short-term memory network (ConvLSTM) to disentangle these two components. Trained and evaluated using large-ensemble simulations from multiple climate models, ConvLSTM consistently outperforms existing model-based, statistical, and machine-learning approaches. Applying ConvLSTM to observations reveals an evolving spatial pattern of the forced response and substantial contributions from internal variability to recent multidecadal climate trends. For several key climate features whose attribution remains debated, ConvLSTM indicates that internal variability amplified the forced reversal of Atlantic interhemispheric temperature asymmetry, dominated the observed La Niña-like warming pattern over the equatorial Pacific and the associated precipitation decline over southwestern North America since 1980, and modulated Arctic amplification around its nearly constant threefold forced value. Comparison between the ConvLSTM-estimated and model-simulated forced responses reveals broadly consistent patterns but also important discrepancies, with climate models simulating stronger Northern Hemisphere warming, weaker subtropical high intensification, and smaller land precipitation declines. Overall, ConvLSTM provides a robust framework for attributing historical climate evolution and evaluating the fidelity of climate models

Abstract
SAXS/SANS determine the size and structure of particles and macromolecules in solution. However, the conventional low-angle determination of the radius of gyration, Rg, cannot separately resolve its scattering-weighted mean and the width of its distribution. Because the apparent Rg includes a contribution from ensemble variance, the distribution width is required even to recover the mean Rg. Here, we introduce TENOR-SAXS, a method for estimating the scattering-weighted width of an Rg distribution by applying two anisotropic digital smearings to a single 2D scattering image. Comparing the smeared images provides curvature information unavailable from the conventional radial profile. The resulting observables require no explicit model of the native instrumental point-spread function and show only weak sensitivity over the range of native resolutions typical of SAXS measurements examined here. Analytical expressions are derived in the low-angle and narrow-distribution limits for ensembles described by a known or independently constrained single-particle form factor. For verification, we simulated scattering by particles with several form factors and ensembles with different size distributions, all agree well with the analytical theory. To leading order, the principal observable depends on the scattering-weighted mean and variance rather than on the distribution's functional form or higher moments. For simulated data corresponding to feasible synchrotron exposure times for biological samples, the distribution width and, expressed as a fraction of its mean, was recovered with a typical discrepancy of ~0.1, while the scattering-weighted mean Rg was recovered with ~2% relative discrepancy. TENOR-SAXS thus provides a framework for estimating ensemble width from existing 2D scattering data without explicit instrumental desmearing and for correcting the variance contribution to the apparent radius

Abstract
Accurate river prediction is essential for water, food, and energy security, yet extending it reliably from sparse in-situ gauges to the full river network remains an open challenge. Although machine learning has transformed Earth-system modelling, the same sparse and uneven observations have largely confined its advances for rivers to discrete basins rather than connected river systems. Here we introduce GraphRiverCast (GRC), a topology-informed river foundation model for reach-level prediction across the global river network. Exploiting the connectivity and energetics of rivers, it is trained on sparse gauges and predicts daily multivariate hydrodynamics with no prior river conditions. More broadly, harnessing the structure and energetics of a system offers a strategy for machine learning in other observation-limited Earth systems.

Abstract

Abstract
Groundwater in Ghana's Densu River Basin remains a key domestic water source, but multi-metal contamination threatens children's health. Previous deterministic hazard index assessments provide limited treatment of uncertainty, co-exposure, and spatial variability. This study applies a Bayesian probabilistic framework for non-carcinogenic risk assessment using 94 groundwater samples and measurements of Fe, Mn, Ni, Pb, Cd, and As. The framework provides uncertainty-aware, spatially explicit information to support groundwater monitoring, risk mitigation, and public health planning in data-limited regions.

Abstract
Understanding how fast atmospheric variability shapes slow climate variability and sensitivity remains a central challenge in Earth-system science. Recent advances in machine-learned (ML) atmospheric models have demonstrated remarkable skill on weather timescales, but their emergent behaviour in a fully coupled climate system remains largely unexplored. We present early results from a new hybrid modelling framework, in which the ACE2 ML atmospheric emulator is interactively coupled to the NEMO ocean model. We report on a set of 70-year coupled simulations (1950-2020 historical forcing and fixed-1950s control). These experiments represent, to our knowledge, one of the first multi-decadal integrations of a machine-learned atmosphere interacting with a full-depth dynamical ocean. These results provide a unique test of physical realism and generalisability for atmospheric emulators and evaluate the possible role of entirely machine-learned components in next-generation Earth system models.

Abstract

Abstract

Abstract
In statistical models for categorical responses, including binary, nominal and ordinal responses, a phenomenon called ``separation'' may occur which can render maximum likelihood estimate nonexistent and/or nonunique. Based on Sablica et al. (2026)'s general characterization of separation phenomena via structure vectors, in this article we discuss computational, numerical and practical aspects of separation phenomena in different software packages, including consequences, remedies and ways of diagnosing them. Abstract
Uncertainty in the terrestrial carbon cycle remains a major constraint in climate projections, partly due to imperfect land surface representations in Earth system models. Abstract
Earth's magnetic field is generated by fluid motion in the liquid-metal core and has been active for billions of years. However, prior to the onset of inner-core growth, the sources of mechanical power that drove the geodynamo remain uncertain. During this period, the core may have been overlain by a basal magma ocean (BMO), creating two immiscible fluid layers separated by a density interface, beneath the solid mantle. We develop a theory for lunar tides in this core--BMO system, in which the tidal potential acts on the density contrast between the two layers. The resulting dynamics differ fundamentally from previous models driven by imposed deformation of the core's bounding envelope. In the inviscid limit, the response transitions from an equilibrium tide to an interfacial gravity wave as forcing frequency increases. The two regimes are separated by a resonance that occurs when the forcing frequency matches the natural frequency of the degree-2, order-2 interfacial mode. The inviscid core flow is formally identical to the canonical elliptical vortex, linking the problem to the theory of elliptical instability. Finite viscosity regularises the resonance, introduces phase lag and generates oscillatory boundary layers. Combined with parameterised models of lunar recession and BMO crystallisation, the theory predicts enhanced core-boundary ellipticity, core-flow speed, magnetic Reynolds number and instability metrics, particularly near resonance. These results identify a previously unexplored mechanism for tidally driven flow in differentiated planetary bodies and show that a BMO can enhance tidal coupling to the core, potentially contributing to dynamo action.

Abstract
The wintertime Arctic boundary layer is characterized by two distinct states, radiatively clear (cloud-free) and radiatively opaque (cloudy), which is reflected in observations as a bimodal distribution. Previous studies hypothesized that these regimes are related to an underlying bistability of the system's dynamics. We apply a drift-diffusion framework based on the two leading terms of the Kramers-Moyal expansion to observations from 15 winter periods in Ny-Alesund, Norway. Our model captures the bimodality; we find a single stable point for the deterministic drift, however, which questions the existence of bistable deterministic dynamics. Instead, the observed regimes can be attributed to state-dependent stochastic fluctuations with maximum amplitude close to the clear-cloudy transition. The position of the stable point depends on the meteorological conditions. Controlling for synoptic variability does not remove the state-dependence of diffusion, however. Through an illustrative example, we suggest a possible process-based interpretation of our statistical analysis. The nonlinear onset of cloud formation, once atmospheric humidity reaches saturation, could map state-independent fluctuations in humidity to state-dependent fluctuations in cloudiness.
